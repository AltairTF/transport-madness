# M5 plan: maintenance, depots, full incidents

Status: planned (decisions taken 2026-10-21 in an interview with the developer). Done when (game-plan.md): the management layer is playable end to end, here meaning that a fleet wears, breaks down, is serviced by a policy the player sets in depots that can do the work, and the incidents that follow cost money and goodwill. Architecture: ADR 0005. Vocabulary: `CONTEXT.md`.

Contracts and the random events (festival, industry boom or slump) of the first plan are not here. The campaign (ADR 0006, `docs/campaign-plan.md`) replaces contracts with government orders and gives random events a war setting; only the event feed, which the incidents need, is built in M5. M5 is built after the first campaign slice, C1 (the new time scale), so that the wear and repair numbers are calibrated once.

## What was decided

- **Condition, 0 to 100, for the Company's fleet only.** Every road vehicle and every train (one value for the consist) has one; rail signals get their own in 5c and road sections in 5f. Ambient cars have none and do not break down; they only meet the incidents that come out of the driving physics. A vehicle starts at 100.
- **Wear.** Condition falls with the distance driven (starting point: one point per 12 km) and 30 % faster with a full load. A vehicle that was patched wears 1.5 times as fast until its next Service or Overhaul. There is no permanent age wear and top speed never falls.
- **Effects of low Condition.** The risk of a breakdown is a hazard per metre driven: none above 60, rising smoothly down to 20, high below it, drawn from the seeded RNG. Low Condition also lengthens the braking distance, which feeds rear-end collisions. A breakdown makes a Wreck: it blocks its lane, a crew clears it, the cargo is lost, and the vehicle then goes to the garage of the nearest Depot with minimal Condition instead of vanishing. A broken-down train stops its line until a crew clears it (crews stay abstract, a straight line from the depot, as today).
- **Repairs.** Patch: +15, leaves the "patched" mark, 20 s, 2 % of the vehicle's price. Service: +50, 60 s, 6 %. Overhaul: up to 95, clears the patches, 180 s, 25 %. Times are simulated seconds. These are starting points, set by a probe like `RailBalanceProbe` after C1 changes the day length. Balance target: with the default company policy maintenance costs 8 to 12 % of a line's revenue, and without maintenance the fleet loses more than that to breakdowns within about 90 days. If that is missed the tuning is wrong, not the design.
- **Depots as capability.** The level sets what a depot can do: Patch at any, Service from a Workshop, Overhaul only at a Complex. A depot is upgraded in place by a command that pays the price difference. A repair takes one of the depot's bays for its duration (Simple 1, Workshop 2, Complex 3); the rest queue inside as garage vehicles do. The cost of repairing an old vehicle is a field in the tuning that has no effect until the campaign's model years exist.
- **Maintenance policy at three levels.** Company, Line and Vehicle, the most specific winning, each also able to say "inherit". A vehicle in the garage with no line uses the company's. A policy has a trigger (days since the last repair and/or Condition below a floor, either or both, each can be off), a kind of repair (Patch, Service or Overhaul), or is off, which leaves the risk to the player. The default for the company is a Service every 60 days or below 40. A due vehicle finishes unloading at its next stop, does not load again and goes to the nearest Depot able to do that repair (garage and ghost mechanics reused), waits for a bay, is repaired and goes back to its line. If no Depot can do it, the vehicle keeps running and the game says so. Signals follow the company policy: a crew from the nearest depot services one whose Condition is under the floor.
- **Full incidents (5c).** New causes: breakdown from low Condition and signal failure. A failed signal shows red for several minutes, trains wait (a deadlock can result, and the warning already covers it) and a crew repairs it. Trains still cannot hit each other (ADR 0004 holds). Consequences: compensation per injured passenger on a Company vehicle (injured is a number, a share of the passengers aboard), a fine only for negligence (a breakdown with Condition under a critical value, a signal failure left unrepaired for long), a fall in the satisfaction of a Town when a Company vehicle's incident is inside it or blocks its access road for over an hour, and no insurance. Everything the player needs to see goes in an event feed (incidents, breakdowns, warnings such as no depot can do the repair) with a toast for the serious ones and a click that moves the camera to the place; the campaign adds to it.
- **Frequency option.** Off, Low, Normal, High, chosen when a game starts and changeable by a logged command. It scales the random events M5 and the campaign add (breakdowns, signal failures, later random events and bombing). Overtaking that fails and junction crashes come out of the physics and are not scaled, so Off does not remove all accidents; the screen that offers the option says so.
- **Roads (5f).** Every road section has a Condition that falls with the vehicles that pass (starting point: by traffic only; no weather). It affects incident risk and speed. Resurfacing is done by a road crew, costs per metre, and blocks the road like a Wreck for a fixed time. Rail track is left out for now.
- **Hashes.** Condition, the time of the last repair, the policy and the frequency enter the state hash from the start, so the pinned hashes of every world with Company vehicles are set again once and checked on macOS and Linux (Docker); road-only worlds with no Company vehicle keep theirs.
- **Interface (as simple as the unplayed 3D allows).** A Condition bar in the vehicle and train windows and a Condition column in the Vehicles list; in 3D a vehicle under 40 is darker and under 20 reddish. A Maintenance window with the company policy and the fleet by Condition; the Line and vehicle windows get the policy block, the vehicle window also shows which policy applies and where it comes from. A depot window with the upgrade button and the bays (who is being repaired, who waits). The event feed in a corner.

## Slices

| Slice | Content | Tests |
|---|---|---|
| 5a | Condition and wear for vehicles and trains, the breakdown hazard and Wreck, the garage return, repair kinds with durations and prices, the three-level policy and the automatic visit, all as commands; state hash and golden hashes | `ConditionTests`, `BreakdownTests`, `MaintenancePolicyTests`, determinism and replay with maintenance |
| 5b | Depot capability by level, upgrade in place, bays and the queue, the event feed's first form, the Maintenance and depot windows, Condition on the vehicle windows and in 3D | `DepotCapabilityTests`; HUD run headless |
| 5c | Signal Condition and failure, compensation, fines, town satisfaction, the frequency option and its command, the feed with toasts and camera links | `SignalFailureTests`, `IncidentConsequenceTests`, `FrequencyOptionTests` |
| 5f | Road Condition, risk and speed, road crews and Resurfacing, price per metre, the road in the Maintenance window | `RoadConditionTests`, `ResurfacingTests` |
| Balance | A probe in the style of `RailBalanceProbe` (env gated) for the 8 to 12 % target and the breakdown rate without maintenance; the tuning set from it | probe, one economy test pinning the target |

The order is the order of dependency: 5a feeds the others. The game layer of each slice is compiled and its GDScript parsed here; the user plays it in 3D.

## Left out on purpose

- Weather (wet or icy road): M6, with the visual side it needs.
- Breakdowns of ambient cars: with tier 1 and 2 ambient traffic.
- Contracts: replaced by the government's orders (`docs/campaign-plan.md`, C5).
- Insurance, and wear and repair of rail track: not planned.
- The "old vehicle" cost multiplier is a tuning field only until the campaign has model years (C8).
