# The game becomes a campaign about a region at war, 1938 to 1945

The management game stays what it is (roads, rail, vehicles, towns, industries, cargo, incidents, maintenance), but its frame changes. The player no longer runs a transport company: they chair the development committee of a small region of a fictional country that goes to war, and manage its military and civil industrialisation. The calendar runs from January 1938 to December 1945. There is no sandbox for now; one can come later and will be designed then. The world stays fictional (country, region, towns, names) with a timeline and technology taken from the period, because the earlier "no real nations or brands" decision still holds and because it leaves the design free. Casualties are numbers only. Bombing is shown (bombs falling, buildings, roads and track damaged) without graphic content.

**Time.** A day and a month are separated. The day is the cycle of people's lives (trips, shifts, loading) and gets 1200 s of simulated time, so a 2 km commute takes about three hours of the clock instead of almost six. The month is the calendar and gets 3 days, so 96 months are 288 days: 96 hours at 1x, about 12 at 8x. Everything that is paid, grown or consumed per day, month or year is re-derived once (milestone C1), which also moves every golden hash.

**Money is a budget.** Fares and cargo pay still earn money for the regional treasury. A monthly allocation from the government, set by the phase of the war and the player's standing, is added to it. The loan becomes the ministry's advance. Bankruptcy is replaced by dismissal: government standing below 20 for six months in a row ends the game. There are two scores, the government's standing (rises with orders met, falls with orders failed, fines and deficit) and the towns' satisfaction, and they compete because what goes to the state is not available to the towns. The campaign ends with one of four grades, not a win or loss.

**Orders replace contracts.** The government asks for production quotas delivered to the state, state works (it founds a factory on the map and wants it supplied and connected, such as a military truck plant), and civil needs. The scripted phases (rearmament 1938-39, mobilisation 1939-41, total war 1942-44, the end in 1945) set the mix and the size of the allocation; the concrete orders inside a phase are drawn by the seed at random intervals.

**The outside world comes in through gateways.** Two gateways on the map edge, one road and one rail, behave like stations that are also industries. The player asks for an import shipment (coal, ore, rubber, fuel) and pays from the budget, dearer per unit than producing it locally; it arrives as stock at the gateway and the player carries it to where it is needed. What the state demands (steel, trucks) is delivered to a gateway as an export. Mining coal and ore at home is the alternative to importing, so coal and iron ore exist on the map but scarce, and rubber and oil never do.

**Chains are deep only where it pays.** About fourteen cargo types and thirteen industries: coal, iron ore, wood, stone and grain; steel, planks, building materials and fuel; engines, bodies and tyres for the military truck, assembled at a plant that exports it; food and goods as they are. Electricity is a regional capacity, not a cargo: power plants fed with coal add megawatts for the whole region, each industry has a demand, a shortage lowers every output in proportion, and the government sells power from outside by the month. Big industries have jobs filled by residents through the existing trip groups, on foot, by car or by the player's lines; staffing sets output, and the player's bus lines to the factories help it but do not replace the people's own movement.

**War reaches the systems** in four slices, in this order: rationing (dearer and scarcer fuel and rubber), conscription (waves of residents leaving, which lowers jobs), requisition (the army buys a part of the fleet at fixed moments) and bombing (from 1942, on gateways, factories and railway, as incidents).

**Vehicles are gated by year, not by era.** Each model has the year from which it can be bought; the roster stays in data files so that adding models is cheap, because a wide choice of vehicle models is meant to become one of the selling points of the game. The three eras of the first game plan (1900 to 2020) are dropped.

Consequence: milestone M5 (maintenance) is built first and keeps its design; its contracts and events slices are replaced by the campaign slices (`docs/campaign-plan.md`), which follow it, with the old M6 (save and load, overlays, balancing, art, audio) after them.
