# Rail is a network of its own, and trains move by reserving track

Trains run on a `RailNetwork` beside the `RoadNetwork`, not as a road type on the lane graph. The road simulation is built around lanes, car-following, lane changes and junction paths between lanes, and every one of those rules is wrong for a train: a train is a hundred metres long, has no lanes, brakes over a kilometre, and may only enter track that it has been given. Making rails a lane type would have meant special cases in the hot loops of the road simulation (and risked its golden hashes) to get behaviour that none of it shares. The two networks meet at level crossings only.

The rail graph is nodes and single tracks (straight or circular arcs, the same `RoadGeometry` as roads, so the terrain, cut and fill, bridge and tunnel rules apply with rail numbers). A track runs both ways. A node connects two of its tracks when the turn between them is gentle (`rail.maxTurnAngle`), and a train slows through the bend.

Safety comes from reservation, not from collision checks: a train may only move on track that it holds, and it asks for the track beyond a boundary (a signal, a station or a depot) before it gets there. A block signal asks for every track reachable beyond it up to the next boundary; a path signal asks only for the path the train will take. Whatever a train holds is released as its tail passes. Two trains therefore cannot be on one piece of track, with or without signals; what they can do is wait for each other for good, and a ring of trains that have waited for track held by the next is found and reported as a deadlock. We rejected a collision-and-incident model for trains (derailments, head-on crashes between trains) for this milestone: it needs a train physics that lets one run into another, and the reservation model gives the player the thing the plan asks for, signals that matter and deadlocks to solve.

Trains are push-pull (a cab at each end) so that they turn round in a station without a run-round loop; this keeps the line editor and the single-track case playable. The cost is that a train reverses where a real one could not. A train held up by another on its route looks for another way to its station that avoids track held by trains, so passing loops work without timetables.

Trains and road vehicles share the world at level crossings. A crossing closes when a train that will cross is about to arrive, road vehicles stop at a line before it and do not enter if the road beyond is queued up, and a train stops short of a crossing with a wreck or a standing vehicle in it. A train that cannot stop in time hits what is there: the vehicle is wrecked, the crossing is blocked until a rescue crew clears it.

Consequence: the simulation state hash mixes the rail in only when a railway exists, so worlds without one keep the hashes they had; rail runs have pinned hashes of their own.
