# Campaign plan: a region at war, 1938 to 1945

Status: C1 to C9 done on 2026-10-21 (sections 5.23, 5.25 to 5.35 of `project-status.md`); only M6 is left, and six more slices (C10 to C15) were decided on 2026-10-21 to come before it (decisions taken 2026-10-21 in an interview with the developer). The reasons and the shape are in ADR 0006; this file is the list of decisions, the slices and what is still open. It sits between M5 and the old M6 in the milestone table of `game-plan.md`.

## What was decided

- **Frame.** The player chairs the development committee of a small region of a fictional country that goes to war and manages its military and civil industrialisation. Calendar: January 1938 to December 1945, 96 months. Fictional world, period technology, casualties as numbers only, bombing shown without graphic content (bombs falling, buildings, roads and track damaged). Only the campaign exists; a sandbox may come later and will be designed then.
- **Time.** Day 1200 s (the cycle of people's lives), month 3 days (the calendar), so the campaign is 288 days: 96 hours at 1x, about 12 at 8x, and in practice 20 to 30 hours of play. The step (0.05 s) does not change.
- **Budget.** Fares and cargo pay still earn money for the regional treasury; a monthly allocation from the government is added, set by the phase of the war and the standing. The loan becomes the ministry's advance (same mechanics, new name). Imports are paid from the budget. Bankruptcy is replaced by dismissal.
- **Two scores.** The government's standing (0 to 100) rises with orders met and falls with orders failed, fines and deficit. The towns' satisfaction is the existing number. They compete: what goes to the state is not available to the towns, and a discontented town loses workers. Standing under 20 for 6 months in a row is dismissal and the end of the game. The campaign ends in December 1945 with one of four grades, from the final standing, the production delivered and the average satisfaction of the towns.
- **Orders replace contracts.** Three kinds: a production quota (deliver N of a cargo to the state at a gateway by a date), a state work (the government founds a factory on the map, for instance a military truck plant, and wants it supplied and connected, with an output target) and a civil need (what the towns already ask for). Meeting one gives a bonus in the budget and standing; failing costs both. Scripted phases (rearmament 1938-39, mobilisation 1939-41, total war 1942-44, the end 1945) change the mix of orders and the size of the allocation; the concrete orders inside a phase are drawn by the seed at random intervals (not on a monthly beat), with the deadline and the reward drawn within a range, a cap of 3 active and 5 open, an unaccepted offer expiring after two months, and no repeat of the same cargo and pair right after. An order can only be accepted when the player already has stations that cover its source and destination. Cargo counts when loaded at a station covering the source and unloaded at one covering the destination, by any vehicle of the company on any line (as built it counts whatever its source, see "Decided while building C5 to C9"). Late delivery pays in proportion to what arrived; failing costs a quarter of the bonus and, when the destination is a Town, a little satisfaction. The first order comes in month 1, is easy (for example wood or food to a gateway) and doubles as the tutorial.
- **Gateways.** Two, on the map edge, one road and one rail, placed by the seed, to be connected by the player. Each is a station that is also an industry. Import: the player asks the government for a shipment (coal, ore, rubber, fuel), pays per unit from the budget (dearer than producing it locally), and it arrives as stock at the gateway, one shipment per request with size and interval from the tuning. Export: what the state asks for is delivered to a gateway and counts toward the orders.
- **Cargo chain.** About 14 cargo types and 13 industries (as built: 19 cargo entries, passengers and workers included, and 16 industry types), deep only where it pays. Raw: coal, iron ore, wood, stone, grain (rubber and oil only by import). Materials: steel (iron ore and coal), planks, building materials, fuel (from imported oil). Components, for the military truck only: engines (steel and fuel), bodies (steel and planks), tyres (imported rubber). Final: the military truck, from engines, bodies and tyres, exported to a gateway. Food and goods as they are. Coal and iron ore exist on every map but scarce (one deposit each, far from each other), so mining at home is an investment to weigh against importing. Rubber and oil never exist locally. State works appear by order, in the middle of the campaign, at a feasible place drawn by the seed.
- **Electricity** is a regional capacity, not a cargo and not a network. Power plants that are supplied with coal add megawatts to the whole region; each industry has a demand; a shortage lowers every output in proportion; the government sells power from outside by the month at a price in the tuning, with no transport.
- **Workers.** Big industries (steel mill, truck plant, engine works, power plant) have jobs. Residents fill them through the existing trip groups, on foot, by car or by the company's lines (as built, workers are a cargo carried by workers' buses and not trip groups, see 5.30 and below). Output is proportional to staffing. The people's own movement fills part of the jobs; the player's bus lines to the factories are what lets the rest be filled, a facilitator the player has to manage, not a replacement.
- **Fleet by year.** A model has the year from which it can be bought. Road: bus (1938) and a bigger bus (1941); light truck (1938) and heavy truck (1940). Rail: steam locomotive (1938, cheaper, slower, dearer to keep) and diesel (1942); coaches and freight wagons as now, and freight stays generic (one road truck or wagon carries any freight cargo). The old-vehicle rule of `CONTEXT.md` stays as a repair-cost multiplier by the age of the model, with no permanent wear. Later the player is to choose among many models, which is meant to be a selling point, so the roster stays in the tuning data and the code never names a model. The three eras of 1900 to 2020 in the first game plan are dropped.
- **Effects of the war**, four slices in this order: rationing (oil, fuel and rubber dearer and scarcer in the phases of war), conscription (waves of residents leaving every town, which lowers the jobs and so the output), requisition (the army buys part of the fleet at fixed moments at a table price; the player cannot refuse and can prepare for it) and bombing (from 1942, on gateways, factories and railway: Wrecks, blocked track, damaged buildings, cleared by the crews; casualties as numbers; shown with bombs falling and the damage left on the map). Rationing and bombing are scaled by the frequency option of M5.
- **Start of a game.** Towns with streets and some industries in place, no links between them, as today; the first order in month 1; the budget and allocation from the tuning.

## Names (decided 2026-10-21)

- **Country:** the Republic of Veldmar. **Region:** the Marren Valley. The committee is the Marren Valley Development Committee. Neighbours, the enemy and the allies are never named: the world beyond the gateways is "the rest of the world", the war is "the war". This keeps ADR 0006's fictional world.
- **Towns:** 24 names in `towns.names` of `data/tuning.json`, invented in a Central European manner (-burg, -au, -feld, -moor, -holt, -hof) and checked not to be real places: Aldermark, Brennau, Corvenholt, Dravik, Eskerfeld, Falkenmoor, Grauwald, Harlenburg, Isenhaven, Jelenfeld, Karnbruck, Lorvenau, Marrenstadt, Norvik, Ostenhal, Pellenau, Quernheim, Rothmoor, Sarnfeld, Tavenburg, Ulvenhof, Varnholt, Wessenau, Zorvenburg. They replaced the English placeholders. There are still 24, shuffled by the seed, so no world changes and no golden hash moves (names are not in the hash).
- **Industries and gateways** are named for their town and type as now ("Aldermark Steel Mill"); the two gateways are named from the edge they sit on and their kind ("North Road Gate", "East Rail Gate"), see C3.
- The country and region names are in the `campaign` section of the tuning (`country`, `region`), read from there by the HUD and the Orders window.

## Deposits (decided in C2)

A deposit of coal or iron ore is one per map in every setting, within 2200 m of a town and 1500 to 2200 m from the other kind, so that a steel mill can stand within 1500 m of both. If the map has no such place it is put anywhere that will do and the new-game note says so. The numbers are `industries.generation.deposit*` in the tuning.

## Budget and standing (decided in C4)

Phases start at months 0, 20, 48 and 84 (Rearmament to August 1939, Mobilisation to December 1941, Total war 1942 to 1944, The end 1945). The allocation is 45 000, 60 000, 80 000 and 50 000 a month at a standing of 50, times 0.5 + standing/100. Standing starts at 60 and each month moves by deliveries to the gateways against what the phase expects (within 3 points), minus 2 for a deficit, minus a point per 2 000 of fines (at most 5). Under 20 for 6 months is dismissal. The grade score is half the standing, 30 % the value delivered against 600 000 and 20 % the towns' satisfaction, with thresholds 75, 55 and 35. All of it is in the `campaign` section of the tuning and is a first guess to be balanced once the orders (C5) exist.

## Decided while building C5 to C9 (2026-10-21)

These were taken without the developer seeing them; each is described in the section of `project-status.md` named. Any of them can be reversed by tuning or a small change.

- **Orders (5.28).** Cargo counts for an order whatever its source (the plan asked for loading at a station covering the source); the producer check stays at acceptance. A quota names one gateway, so road and rail gateways are asked for in turn. At the deadline half delivered is *late* (paid in proportion, no penalty), under half is *failed* (fine of a quarter of the reward, standing falls by 1.5 times the gain). Orders add standing directly; the monthly delivery term of C4 was not turned down. A state work is offered once, in Mobilisation, and founds the truck plant and its three feeder works near a town when accepted.
- **Electricity (5.29).** The free grid is sized from the industries that stand at the start (times 1.1), so the first game runs at full power and only later works need a plant or bought power. A power plant is part of the map, not an order. Demand is constant, working or not. Towns use no power.
- **Workers (5.30).** Workers are a cargo carried by a workers' bus, not trip groups (an industry is not a building). The people's own movement staffs part of an industry by its distance to the nearest town. Trains do not carry workers. On a map without towns staffing is 1.
- **Fleet by year (5.31).** Steam locomotive 1938, diesel 1942, heavy truck 1940, large bus 1941. An old model is only dearer to repair (12 % a year after three years, at most 60 %); there is no permanent wear. Owned models are never taken away when a year passes.
- **Rationing (5.32).** Oil, fuel and rubber only; price and shipment size are fixed when a shipment is asked for. The frequency option scales the change.
- **Call-up (5.33).** Fixed waves of 6 to 10 % of the people of every trip group, for good; a workforce gap that cuts output (70 % of the gap) and closes by 1.2 points a month. Not scaled by the frequency option. The towns' building capacity (`StatsOf`) is not lowered.
- **Requisition (5.34).** The army takes freight first, then the biggest, garage included, at 80 % of the price new whatever the Condition, with a month's notice; the player cannot refuse.
- **Raids (5.35).** From January 1942, at gaps of the phase scaled by the frequency option; an industry stops for three days, a gateway is shut for two, a rail raid fails a signal (and needs a signal to exist), a town loses two buildings with 35 % of their people counted as casualties; craters on the nearest roads. Nothing is rebuilt by the state.

## Decided with the developer, 2026-10-21 (before M6)

The 3D side of M5 and of C2 to C9 was played by the developer and works. The interface is awkward and hard to reach (many buttons and windows); it is deferred until the features are done. Balance is ok for now. Before M6 six slices are built, in the order of the table (C10 to C15). Left for after M6: trains carrying workers, wages and shifts, a standing graph and an orders history, sandbox mode, a wider choice of models, a depot with its own stock, and a factory of ammunition. Each slice has its tests and hashes and is one commit and push; design decisions are asked of the developer first, and the tuning numbers are chosen by Claude and listed. The decisions below were settled in an interview on 2026-10-21 (the first plan of four slices was replaced by this one when the developer asked for a dynamic war and for residents as workers).

- **Access points (C10).** An industry, and a gateway, has an access point on its edge facing the nearest town and is a node of the road network, as in Transport Fever. A road built so that its end falls within about 60 m of the point is drawn to it; a road that only passes within that distance gets a short spur of its own type to the point. The preview shows it and the price is in the bill. Depots and stations already stand on nodes. Coverage does not change: a station still covers an industry within `stations.coverageRadius` (150 m), so a station at the point is the direct line and a station farther off, inside the radius, is a hub as now. The access point is also the destination of people going to work. A depot with its own stock is left for after M6.
- **Residents as workers (C11).** An industry with jobs is a workplace candidate for the trip groups of towns within `workRadius`; they arrive on foot, by car or by a line, and a line ends at the station that covers the industry. The `workers` cargo, the `workerbus` model and the own-staff formula by distance are removed. Staffing is the share of the jobs filled by groups that reached the industry that day, computed when the day closes; there are no shifts. This reverses the decision of C7 (workers as a cargo) and moves the rail and maintenance goldens and about 12 to 15 tests.
- **Dynamic raids (C12).** One side of the map is drawn per game as the side the aircraft come from. A flight has 1 to 6 aircraft (more in Total war) and chooses its target by value (industries that work and matter weigh most; also gateways, towns, stations, depots, rail). The aircraft fly straight, are simulated entities that are stepped, hashed and drawn from the simulation state, and drop a stick of bombs as they pass the target. Each bomb has a blast radius and its effect comes from what is inside it: buildings are destroyed with a chance that falls with distance, an industry accumulates damage per bomb (more days stopped, more stock lost), a station or depot is shut for some days, vehicles become wrecks and their passengers are casualties, the road gets a crater, a rail hit fails the nearest signal. A stick that misses the target can still hit its surroundings.
- **Anti-aircraft (C13).** A battery is an industry the player places on free ground by a command, with jobs filled by residents (C11) and ammunition, a new cargo imported at a gateway (price, shipments and rationing as fuel), spent per salvo. Inside its range each aircraft has a chance per second to be shot down, the base times the staffing, zero without ammunition. Under fire the bombs scatter more and may be dropped early; a downed aircraft drops no more bombs. The range is drawn as a circle when positioning. The enemy weighs a target less the more anti-aircraft covers it (about half at strong cover), so defence moves flights to weaker targets. No shelters are built.
- **Civilians (C14).** The siren sounds when a flight enters the map; the warning time is the flight time to the target. People on foot or waiting in a station queue run to the nearest standing building they can reach in time; people in cars and buses keep driving; people already in a building stay and die if it falls. Groups stay blocks of up to 10 but casualties really lower their people (until now casualties were only a number). The feed says "air raid warning, N aircraft from the north-east, arriving in 40 s"; the target is shown in the last 10 s. The aircraft are drawn in 3D from the simulation state each frame, with pause and speed respected.
- **C15a Rebuilding.** Destroyed buildings become free lots and the town founds them again through its growth, from the construction stock the player delivers; there is no rebuild command and the state pays nothing. A raid never destroys the last standing building of a town, so a town can always be rebuilt. Industries, gateways and signals recover by their timers as now.
- **C15b Requisition.** The month of notice lets the player choose the rule for that wave: freight first and the biggest (as now), the oldest first, or passengers first. The price stays at 80 % of new. Buying back is not added (buying a new one is already possible).
- **C15c Call-up.** Each wave lowers for good the people and jobs capacity of the buildings of every town (`StatsOf`), so needs, jobs and trips fall with it. Nobody returns before 1945.

## Open

Items marked *needs play* cannot be settled in the headless simulation. Since 2026-10-21 the developer has played the campaign: balance is ok for now and the 3D side works (see the section above); what is listed below is what remains.

**Balance (ok for now; further tuning in `data/tuning.json` as play finds it)**
- The standing against the orders. An idle committee is dismissed around month 55 and a company that only runs buses, breaks down without maintenance and is fined, around month 28. How much a good player earns from orders against the monthly delivery term, and whether that term should be turned down, was not measured; nor were the allocation, the rewards (60 to 170 a unit, 1 400 to 1 500 for a truck), the fines, the grade thresholds and the pace of offers.
- Electricity: megawatts of a plant (20), the demand of each industry, the price of bought power (1 500 a megawatt and month).
- Workers: how much a workers' bus must carry to keep a mill staffed (a 30-seat bus a day gives about 75 % with the town's own 30 %), the own-staff reach and share.
- The war: the rationing factors, the size and months of the call-up and requisition waves, the gap and damage of raids.
- Import prices and shipment sizes (first guesses since C3).

**Design not done**

Before M6 (slices C10 to C15, decided 2026-10-21, see the section above): access points, residents as workers, dynamic raids, anti-aircraft, civilians taking cover, rebuilding, requisition rule and the call-up lowering capacity.

After M6:
- A depot with its own stock that nearby industries draw from; a factory of ammunition (until then ammunition is imported only).
- Trains carrying workers; a wage for workers; shifts at factories and what a day of 1200 s does to the trip schedule.
- A standing graph (the history is kept); an orders history beyond the last five.
- Sandbox mode (every `fromYear` at the start) and the wider choice of vehicle models.

**Play-test and presentation**
- The 3D side of everything from M5 on was played by the developer on 2026-10-21 and works. The interface is awkward and hard to reach (the number of buttons and windows); it is deferred until the features are done.
- Event feed entries without a place (orders, call-ups, requisitions) take the camera to the origin when clicked.
- The look of the war in general, and the presentation of raids, remain open.

**Technical**
- Save and load must carry the new state: orders, the order, raid and war random streams, damage timers, the workforce gap, called-up people per group, the requisition and call-up wave indices, the power purchase, and staff stocks (M6); after C10 to C15 also the flights in the air, the ammunition stock of each battery, the side the aircraft come from and the people lost by each group.
- The hash of a trip group does not include its people unless a call-up has fired; keep that in mind if groups ever change size by other means.
- Tests that run into Total war need raids switched off (`War.Bombing.FromMonth`), tests that buy trains or new models need `Worlds.Late`, and trip tests need a day of at least 300 s.
