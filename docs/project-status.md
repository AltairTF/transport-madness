## 4. Architecture

Follows ADR 0001: the simulation assembly never references Godot. `SimHost` (C#, in the Godot project) owns a `Simulation`, advances it in fixed steps against the frame clock and draws it. GDScript only emits intent (speed, tool, action) and calls `SimHost`'s PascalCase methods.

Stepping (`SimHost._Process`): accumulates `delta * speed`, runs whole 0.05 s steps while under a wall-clock budget (10 ms at 1x, 70 ms above), and drops the backlog when over budget, so effective speed degrades instead of the frame rate (ADR 0002). The wall clock only decides how many steps run, never what a step computes.

Rendering: roads are boxes with a centre line (avenues get a median); every vehicle is one instance of a single `MultiMesh` filled in bulk each frame; rescue crews use a second small `MultiMesh`. Vehicle positions are extrapolated by a fraction of a step so motion is smooth between the 20 Hz steps. The buffer starts at 2048 instances and doubles when needed.

## 5. Simulation model

### 5.1 Road network (`RoadNetwork`)
- Roads are straight or circular arcs (`RoadGeometry`, section 5.7). Nodes have x/z and a height; a `Road` joins two nodes and owns `ForwardLanes` and `BackwardLanes` (lane 0 is right-most).
- `TwoLane`: one lane per direction, opposing lanes linked as `Oncoming` (overtaking allowed). `Avenue2x2`: two lanes per direction, linked `Left`/`Right`, no `Oncoming`.
- Junction connections are rebuilt whenever a road is added: each incoming lane connects to the lane of every other road at the node, same lane index where possible; a dead end turns traffic around onto the opposite lane.
- `MovesConflict(inA, outA, inB, outB)`: two moves conflict if they enter the same lane (merge) or their paths cross. Each move has a path: a cubic curve from where the incoming lane enters the junction area (8 m before the node, at the lane's own offset) to where the outgoing lane leaves it, keeping each lane's heading. Two moves conflict when their paths cross (segment intersection on 12-point polylines) or run closer than 3.3 m apart (a vehicle reaches half its length ahead of its path and half its width beside it), so lane offsets, junction width and arcs count, and the left turn against oncoming straight traffic is a conflict. Results are cached per move pair (lane geometry never changes). The simulation keeps its own flat copy: each node has a small square table over its moves (an incoming lane with one of its next lanes), filled on first use and laid out again when the network changes (`SyncConflictTables`); a step with 1000 vehicles got about 7 percent faster.
- `GetConflictZone(inA, outA, inB, outB, distance)` gives, for a conflicting pair, the stretch of each path where the centres of two vehicles come within `distance` (5 m, `conflictDistance`) of each other, as distances from the start of the path; merges into the same lane stay in conflict to the end of both paths. The simulation caches it with the conflict bit, so the table entry for (a, b) holds a's stretch.
- `JunctionLength`, `JunctionTurn` and `JunctionPointAt(from, to, distance)` describe the same path (length, total heading change, pose at a distance; heading is blended between the polyline's pieces so it changes smoothly). `JunctionReach` is 8 m, or half a short lane. Vehicles drive along these paths, see 5.9.
- `BuildRoad` plans first (null means "cannot build"): snaps to nodes within 0.5 m at its ends, finds every road it ends on or crosses, splits those roads, then adds the pieces. Refused when shorter than `MinRoadLength` (10 m), when a piece duplicates an existing road, when a piece is too steep, or when it would cross within 10 m of another junction (unless it passes over or under that road, 5.8).
- `SplitRoad` retires the old road and lanes and records for each old lane its two replacements and the split distance. Vehicles migrate in `Simulation.SyncNetwork`.

### 5.2 Vehicles and the step (`Simulation`, partial class)
Data layout: parallel arrays indexed by vehicle id (lane, position, speed, traits, manoeuvre state, kind...). Position is the front bumper's distance along its lane. Vehicles are grouped per lane in ascending position (counting sort by lane, insertion sort by position, ties broken by id), rebuilt each step (a lane change re-files just that vehicle, `MoveInOrder`). Lane links (`Oncoming`, `Left`, `Right`, `Next`, road) are flat arrays refreshed when the network version changes. Ids are reused after a vehicle is removed.

Step order (`Simulation.Step`): `SyncNetwork`; when rail is in use `SyncRail` and `UpdateCrossings`; the traffic lights and signs when there are any; `DecideManoeuvres`; `PrepareOvertakes` (observe oncoming traffic, advance overtakes); `GenerateCargo`; `UpdateIndustries`; `UpdateGateways`; `StepOrders`; `StepRaids`; `UpdateTrips`; `UpdateFleet`; `StepRepairs`; `StepRoadWorks`; `StepSignals`; `StepGhosts`; acceleration for every vehicle from the start-of-step state; integrate and advance lanes; `RebuildOrder`; `StepTrains` and `DetectCrossingCollisions` when there are trains; `StepCondition`; `DetectIncidents` (collisions, debris, crews); `CloseDay` at the last tick of a day; `Tick++`.

Car-following is the Intelligent Driver Model, taking the most restrictive of: the leader in the lane (or the rearmost vehicle on the lane about to be entered), an oncoming overtaker, and junction yielding. Braking is capped at 9 m/s^2 and builds up at most 15 m/s^3, so drivers need a moment to react; that delay is what makes some situations unrecoverable.

Junctions are uncontrolled; see 5.9 for who waits for whom. Vehicles drive through them.

### 5.3 Manoeuvres
- **Lane change** (avenues): evaluated every 0.5 s per vehicle (staggered by id) when held up by a slower leader; gap checks ahead and behind in the target lane; returns to the right lane once the way is clear. Not within 30 m of either end of a lane. The sim swaps lanes instantly; `VehicleState.Shift` carries a 3.5 m offset that fades over 1.25 s so the renderer can show a smooth move.
- **Overtaking** (2-lane roads): after being stuck behind a slower leader for `1.5 + 6 * patience` seconds, a driver pulls into the oncoming lane if it believes the pass fits. Phases: `MovingOut`, `Passing`, `Returning`. Attention limits how far the driver sees, makes it underestimate oncoming speed and how long the pass takes; aggression shrinks the safety margin. It never overtakes a halted vehicle, a queue with a halted vehicle within 80 m, or a leader slower than 1 m/s, and not within 15 m of the start of a lane or when the lane is too short.
- **Giving up:** an overtaker aborts when oncoming time-to-collision is too short, when something is within 15 m, or within 30 m of a junction: back in behind its target if still behind it, straight in if already clear. Alongside the target it cannot back out. An overtaker standing still nose to nose with oncoming traffic for 4 s cuts back in regardless (a sideswipe if the target is still beside it).
- Oncoming traffic brakes for an overtaker through a virtual leader; overtakers out in the oncoming lane follow each other.

### 5.4 Incidents, depots and crews
- **Detection** by box overlap: follower against the vehicle in front (also across a lane end), and each overtaker against everything in its own and the oncoming lane (oncoming positions mapped into its frame, lateral offset `3.5 m * lateral`, vehicle width 1.9 m). A vehicle inside a junction is checked as a rotated box against the others in it (separating axis test on their poses on the junction paths; two on the same path are the following check's business). Kinds (`IncidentKind`): `Breakdown`, `RearEnd`, `HeadOn`, `Sideswipe`, `Junction` (two vehicles met inside a junction), `LevelCrossing` (a train and a vehicle on a crossing, 5.19) and `Bombing` (debris of a raid's crater, 5.35).
- **Wrecks** are halted vehicles (`VehicleKind.Wreck`); a head-on with the overtaker out in the oncoming lane also spawns `Debris` in its own lane so both directions are blocked. A car that hits a wreck joins that incident. Wrecks count against the lane they physically block, and drivers prefer turns onto unblocked roads.
- **Crews**: `AddDepot(node, level)`. A new incident takes the nearest depot with a free crew: arrival after `10 s + straight distance * 1.4 / 16 m/s`; clearing takes 45 s, then wrecks are removed and the crew is free again (it does not drive back). If that response would take 300 s or more, or no depot exists, or every crew has been busy for 300 s, a distant fallback service arrives instead. A car piling into a wreck being cleared restarts the clearing timer.

### 5.5 Determinism (ADR 0003)
One seeded RNG (`SimRandom`, xoshiro256** seeded through SplitMix64, own implementation so it never depends on the runtime), vehicles processed in id order, no wall-clock or Godot values inside a step. `StateHash()` is FNV-1a over all vehicle state, incident and depot state, the network version and the RNG state. Roads built while running are picked up at the next step or spawn.

Player actions are commands (`SimCommand`; about 45 record types, see `SimCommand.cs`). Groups: roads (build, remove, upgrade, downgrade, speed limit, move a node, resurface, traffic lights and road signs); depots (add, upgrade); stations and lines (build, rename, close, add bays, create, edit, rename, colour, delete); road vehicles and trains (buy, sell, buy parked, assign, park, sell parked); rail (track, signal, rail station, rail depot, build and remove); maintenance (policy, repair, frequency option); the campaign (loan and repay, import, accept or decline an order, power purchase); and the test tools (`SeedTrafficCommand`, `CrashSomeoneCommand`). Road, depot and station commands charge their price and are refused (result 0 or -1, nothing changes) when the company cannot pay. `Simulation.Execute` applies one between steps and appends it to `CommandLog` with the tick it ran at (refused commands too: they change nothing and are refused again on replay). `Simulation.Replay(log, untilTick)` runs a fresh simulation (same seed, same initial network) to a tick, executing the log at the recorded ticks. `DeterminismTests` builds its whole world through commands, so the log plus the seed alone reproduces the run. Anything that changes the network or the simulation without going through `Execute` is not replayable; the game's `DemoWorld` grid is the one such initial world (the demo's depots and traffic are commands).

### 5.6 Terrain and elevation (M1)
- `Terrain` is a heightmap centred on the origin (water, which has its own surface height at each point: see 5.8): 512 x 512 cells of 8 m (4 km square), divided into 16 x 16 chunks of 32 cells. Carving a road records which chunks it touched (`TakeChangedChunks`) so a renderer rebuilds only those. `Terrain.Generate(seed)` sums five octaves of integer-hashed value noise and shapes it so there are plains between hills (up to about 110 m); `Terrain.Flat()` is level. Same seed, same map (`Hash()` in tests). `HeightAt` is bilinear.
- `RoadNetwork(Terrain?)`: nodes have a height (terrain height when created on the ground, interpolated along the road when a junction splits it). A road's profile is the straight line between its node heights. `Lane.Grade` is rise over run in the direction of travel; the lane's `SpeedLimit` falls linearly from the posted value at 4 percent to half of it at 12 percent. Lane length is still the horizontal distance.
- `Plan` refuses a piece steeper than `MaxGrade` (12 percent), so `CanBuildRoad` and `BuildRoad` check it. A deep fill or cut does not refuse the road: it becomes a bridge or tunnel (5.8).
- `EstimateCost` returns length, cut and fill volumes and money: the volume between ground and road surface sampled every 4 m, cross section `h * width + 1.5 * h^2`, plus a price per metre by road type. These are real prices now: commands charge them (5.10).
- `BuildRoad` then carves the terrain (`Terrain.CarveRoad`): the carriageway becomes level with the road, blending back to the old ground over a 6 m shoulder; the flat zone is never narrower than 1.42 cells so every sample read under the road is level. This bumps `Terrain.Version`.

### 5.7 Arcs, removal and upgrade (M1)
- `RoadGeometry` is a straight line or a circular arc: start point, start heading, signed sweep (total turn, positive is an increasing heading) and arc length. `PointAt(s)` and `HeadingAt(s)` take distances along the centre line; `Slice` and `Reversed` give the parts and the other direction. Positions of vehicles on every lane of a road are distances along the road's centre line, so parallel lanes share a length; the lane's sideways offset is only used for drawing, junction paths and incident points.
- `AddRoad(..., sweep)`; `BuildRoad(..., sweep)`. Limits: radius at least 25 m (`MinRadius`), total sweep at most 2.8 rad. Curve speed limit is `sqrt(3 * radius)` (about 8.7 m/s at 25 m, 17 m/s at 100 m), combined with the grade factor. Splitting an arc splits its sweep in proportion.
- `Plan` intersects straight against straight analytically as before; any arc involved uses 3 m polylines with a bounding box test, then the same rules (ends within 0.5 m snap to nodes, no junction within 10 m of an end).
- The tool draws a curve from three clicks: start, a point on it, end (`RoadGeometry.SweepThrough`). Terrain carving follows the polyline of the arc (`Terrain.CarvePath`).
- Terrain is never restored: removing or moving a road keeps its cut and fill, as a real demolition would.
- `RemoveRoad(id)` retires the road's lanes with no replacement; the simulation removes vehicles on them (wrecks included, taking them out of their incident) and rebuilds the junctions at both ends, so a node left with one road becomes a dead end. Cost is `10 per metre` demolition; the terrain keeps its cut and fill.
- `UpgradeRoad(id, speed)` turns a two-lane road into an avenue on the same centre line; vehicles move to the matching new lane. Cost is the avenue price minus half the two-lane price, plus earthworks for the extra width. Only two-lane roads can be upgraded.
- `DowngradeRoad(id, speed)` narrows an avenue to a two-lane road on the same centre line (`DowngradeCostPerMetre`; the ground keeps its earthworks). Right-lane vehicles carry on in the new lane; left-lane vehicles are moved into it as well, and one that lands within a vehicle length of another is removed (`Lane.SplitMerges`, checked in `SyncNetwork`).
- `SetSpeedLimit(id, limit)` re-signs a road (`SpeedLimitChangeCostPerMetre`; refused when unchanged or not a positive finite number). Upgrade, downgrade and speed change share `Reshape`: retire the old road, add a new one on the same nodes and sweep, hand every old lane to the matching new one. Lanes are immutable, so a limit change is a new road, not an edit.
- `MoveNode(node, x, z)` retires every road at a node and rebuilds each one from the new place to its other end with the same total turn (`Sweep`), through `BuildRoad`: a rebuilt road joins the roads it ends on or crosses, and bridges and tunnels are worked out again for the new line. The price is demolition plus new construction (`EstimateMoveNode`, with the bridge and tunnel lengths). Refused when the node has no roads, the target is within 1 m of the old place, on another node or in water, a rebuilt road would be too short, too steep or bend too tightly (the same turn over a shorter chord is a smaller radius), two rebuilt roads would leave in the same direction (under 0.05 rad apart) or cross each other. Vehicles are carried over: every old lane gets a `LaneRemap` to the lanes of the rebuilt pieces, and a vehicle moves to the point at the same share of the road (`SyncNetwork`); where the new road is shorter, vehicles that end up on top of another are removed. A depot at the node moves with it (`MoveNodeCommand` re-points it).
- A depot whose node has lost every road is closed (`Depot.Active` false, checked in `SyncNetwork`): it sends no more crews, a crew on its way is lost and the incident falls back to the distant service, counted from that moment. `AddDepotCommand` is refused at a node with no road. The state hash only mixes in a closed depot, so runs without closures keep their hash.
- `RoadNetwork.Version` increases on every change; the simulation re-syncs when it differs, so a change that adds no lanes (removal) is noticed too.

### 5.8 Water, bridges and tunnels (M1)
- **Water.** `Terrain.WaterSurfaceAt(x, z)` is the height of the water surface: 0 at the middle of the map, falling by 0.0012 m per metre (about 5 m over the map) toward a side chosen from the seed (`WaterSlopeX`, `WaterSlopeZ`), so rivers run downhill and end in the sea. Ground below the surface is under water (`Terrain.IsWater`). `Generate(seed, water: true)` keeps the plains 2.5 m above the local surface, cuts lakes (a noise high) and rivers (a thin band around the middle of another noise field) into the lowlands only, up to 4 m deep, and cuts a sea along each edge the water runs toward (400 m wide when it runs straight at the edge, narrower the more obliquely, none when the water runs along the edge to within 17 degrees), hills or not; roughly 10 to 35 percent of a map is water depending on the seed. Lakes follow the same slope (a few tenths of a metre across a lake) rather than being exactly level. `Terrain.Create(cells, cellSize, shape, waterSlopeX, waterSlopeZ)` builds a hand-shaped map for tests (level water unless a slope is given). Decision: no tides and no boats. Tides would move the shore under roads while the sim runs, and ships are out of scope (game plan); bridges therefore do not check clearance for boats.
- **Rules.** A road must start and end on dry land (`Plan` refuses an end in water); nodes made by hand with `AddNode` are not checked.
- **Structures.** `Plan` walks every stretch between two meetings every 4 m and marks it `Bridge` where the ground is under water or the road would sit more than `maxFillDepth` (6 m) above it, and `Tunnel` where the road would be more than `maxCutDepth` (8 m) below it. Each run is widened by `structureMargin` (8 m) each way (a bridge wins over a tunnel where they meet), and any run shorter than `MinRoadLength` is absorbed by its neighbour. A new meeting (a plain node) is added wherever the kind changes, with the height of the straight profile at that point, so a long road over one gorge is ground, bridge, ground. Each piece is a `Road` with `Road.Structure`; the lanes are ordinary lanes.
- **Cost.** A structure costs `bridgeCostPerMetre` or `tunnelCostPerMetre` (times `avenueStructureFactor` for an avenue) instead of the road price and earthworks, and does not carve the ground. `RoadCost` carries `BridgeLength` and `TunnelLength`; the tool shows them.
- **Junctions on a structure.** A road can join a bridge or tunnel in the middle (`SplitRoad` keeps the structure on both halves) or end on its node; the junction node sits at the height of the deck or bore, so the joining road has to get there within the grade limit (usually on a bridge of its own).
- **Grade-separated crossings.** Where the planned road meets a road in the middle (not at a node), it passes over or under it, with no node and no junction, when the two surfaces are at least `crossingClearance` (5.5 m) apart in height and there is a deck or bore for it: the upper road is a bridge there, or the lower one is a tunnel (`SeparateCrossings`, `PassesOver`). The planned road's height at the crossing is its own straight profile between the other meetings, so dropping the junction also drops the height it would have forced. Otherwise the crossing is a junction as before (and a junction too close to the end of a road is refused). Crossings at a node are always junctions. Vehicles on the two roads never interact there.
- **Limits.** Upgrade, downgrade and re-signing keep the structure; upgrading a structure has no earthworks. Existing roads are never turned into structures, except that moving a node rebuilds its roads and works their structures out again.

### 5.9 Driving through junctions and right of way (M1)
- A vehicle stays on its lane while it crosses a junction: the lane has a tail past its end, the path to the next lane. The tail starts `reach` metres before the lane's end (`reach` = `JunctionReach`, 8 m) and is `P` long, the length of the path for the chosen next lane (`_moveLength`, per move). When the front bumper passes `zoneStart + P` (`ZoneEnd`) the vehicle is on the next lane, `reach` metres into it, with the overshoot carried over (`AdvanceLane`). For a straight crossing `P` is exactly the old jump (`reachIn + reachOut`), so only turns change length. `VehicleState.Position` can therefore exceed the lane length by up to the tail.
- Following: a vehicle in the tail is an ordinary leader for those behind it in the lane; across the junction the gap to the rearmost vehicle on the next lane is `ZoneEnd - pos + theirPos - reach - length` (`GapAcrossJunction`, also used by the rear-end check).
- Turn speed: each move has a speed limit from the mean radius of its path, `sqrt(turnLateralAcceleration * P / turn)` (floor `minTurnSpeed`, none under 0.05 rad of turn). `FreeSpeed` lets a vehicle approach it at `sqrt(limit^2 + 2 * comfortDecel * distanceLeft)`, so it brakes smoothly; IDM tracks that profile with up to about 25 percent overshoot at the start of the tail. At 4 m/s^2 a right-angle turn is taken at about 7 m/s.
- Drawing: `Simulation.JunctionPoseOf(vehicle, centre)` gives the pose of the vehicle's middle on the path (on the path to its next lane past the start of the tail, or on the path it came by while less than one reach into its lane); the game uses it instead of the lane's line, so cars really turn.
- **Right of way** (`JunctionInteraction`, within `junctionRange` 40 m of the end of the lane). A vehicle that has not entered the junction (front bumper before the edge, 8 m from the node) and can still stop before it (`v^2 < 2 * maxBraking * distance`) waits at the edge, as an obstacle at standstill for the IDM, for any other vehicle whose move conflicts with its own and which (a) is inside the junction or has come out of it with its tail still in, and has not yet left the stretch of its path where the two meet (5.1, `GetConflictZone`), or (b) would reach the edge sooner. Arrival time is `TimeToCover(distance, speed)` with the IDM acceleration, ties to the lower id; it is the same for every pair, so the order is total and no ring of vehicles can wait for each other (an earlier version compared arrival at each pair's own stretch, which is not transitive, and a 1000-car grid locked into four-way rings). A vehicle that would arrive second still goes when it can be through its stretch, with `crossingSafetyTime` (1 s) to spare, before the other gets to its own. A vehicle already inside the junction never waits, and one that cannot stop in time goes on.
- **Box clear.** A vehicle also stops at the edge when the rearmost vehicle on its exit lane is crawling (under `exitQueueSpeed` 3 m/s) with less than a vehicle length plus `minGap` between the start of the lane and its rear: entering would leave the car standing in the junction.
- **Collisions.** `DetectJunctionCollisions` puts every vehicle with its middle inside a junction (past the start of the tail, or less than one reach into its lane after one) on its path pose and tests rotated boxes (4.5 x 1.9 m) pairwise; overlap on different paths is an `IncidentKind.Junction` and wrecks both. With the rules above none happens in the test grids; it is what remains when a vehicle cannot stop in time, a wreck stands in the crossing, or a scenario puts cars there by hand.
- Effects seen: grids see fewer overtakes (about 4 on average per 25 minutes with 600 cars on an 8 x 8 grid, against about 10 before and 35 before turns were slowed) because everyone slows for each turn and waits for crossing traffic; 1000 cars on a 10 x 10 grid with avenues run at a mean 6.7 m/s with about one in six standing at a junction edge, against 8.6 m/s and one in thirty when crossing paths were only yielded to (cars drove through each other: 17 overlaps in 10 minutes). Conflict stretches use 5 m between centres and paths count as meeting 3.3 m apart; smaller values let collisions through again.
- Not done: left-turn waiting in the middle of the junction. Traffic lights are 5.20, priority, yield and stop signs 5.22.

### 5.10 Company, stations, lines and vehicles (M2)
- **Money** (`Company`, `Simulation.Economy.cs`). Whole currency units in a `long`. The company starts with `economy.startingMoney` and can borrow up to `maxLoan` (`TakeLoanCommand`, `RepayLoanCommand`). A command that costs money is priced first (`EstimateCost`, `DemolitionCost`, `EstimateUpgrade`, ... the same numbers the tools show), refused if `Money < price`, and charged only when the work was done. A ledger keeps a running total per `LedgerEntry` (construction, stations, depots, vehicles, income, upkeep, interest). `CanAfford(cost)` is public for the UI. The simulation's state hash includes money, loan and everything below, so the golden hashes in `DeterminismTests` were re-pinned when this landed (ambient behaviour did not change: they matched again after the vehicle-length work).
- **Days.** One day is `economy.dayLength` (1200 s, 24 000 ticks since C1; it was 60 s and then 600 s). At the turn of a day the company pays upkeep for every open station and every vehicle of the fleet and interest on the loan (the ministry's advance in the campaign), and at the turn of a month the campaign's books (5.27). Bankruptcy no longer exists: money can go below zero, which costs standing, and dismissal ends the game.
- **Stations** (`Station`). A station stands at a road node. `BuildStationCommand(x, z)` snaps to a node within 12 m, else to the road under the point, which is split there (at least `minApproach` 30 m from both ends); `FindStationSite` is the same decision without building, for the tool's preview. Vehicles stop on the lanes that run into the node and are at least `minApproach` long, with their front `stopMargin` (3 m) before the junction edge. A station whose node loses every road closes (`Active` false), a moved node takes its station along, and lines skip closed stations. Without towns or industries a station simply produces each cargo at `cargo[].supplyPerDay`, up to `maxWaiting`; with them `stations.coverageRadius` (150 m, the walking radius of the trips) decides what it serves. `BuildStationCommand(x, z, bays)` can build a station with bays (5.15).
- **Lines** (`Line`). An ordered list of at least two stations (no station twice in a row, the last may not equal the first); vehicles visit them in order and start over. `DeleteLineCommand` sells the fleet. A line keeps a name, income, upkeep and units delivered; `EditLineCommand` and `RenameLineCommand` change its stops and name (5.15), `SetLineColorCommand` its colour (5.17).
- **Vehicles** (`BuyVehicleCommand(depot, model, line)`). The roster is `vehicles[]` in the tuning file (id, cargo, capacity, length, top speed, price, upkeep, dwell times). A new vehicle appears on the right-most lane that leaves the depot's node, the one with the lower route cost to the line's first station, when the first `length + spawnClearance` of it is free and nothing is within 40 m of the end of any lane running into that node (otherwise a car would run into its tail: this was found as rear-end crashes in a 30-day grid run). Refused for an unknown model, a model not yet on sale (5.31), a closed depot, a deleted line or no money; it is never refused for lack of room, a vehicle that cannot come out at once waits in the depot (5.21). Company vehicles are ordinary tier 0 vehicles with `_line >= 0`: same IDM, lane changes, overtaking and incidents. `SellVehicleCommand` pays `sellRefundShare` of the price.
- **Routing** (`Simulation.Routing.cs`). For each station a field over the lane graph: the time to drive from the start of each lane to the station's stop, found backwards from the stop lanes by Dijkstra (lane time = length / limit, plus the junction path). Made when first needed and again when the network version changes. At each junction (`AdvanceLane`, `Depart`, network sync) a company vehicle takes the turn with the lowest `field[next] + move time`; a lane with a wreck costs 600 s extra; if no turn leads there it picks at random as ambient traffic does. Ambient traffic still chooses at random (the random walk stays until trip groups exist). Dijkstra distances do not depend on queue tie order, and ties between turns go to the first in lane order, so routes are deterministic.
- **Buying** (`Simulation.Fleet.cs`). A vehicle is put on a lane leaving the depot that can reach the line's first station; a depot with no road to it refuses (`BuyVehicleCommand` returns -1), and `DepotsByDistance(line)` lists the connected depots nearest first, which is what the game uses, so a depot on a road of its own never takes a vehicle it cannot send anywhere.
- **Stops** (`Simulation.Fleet.cs`). A vehicle heading for a station brakes for an obstacle at the stop position (`StopInteraction`, same IDM term as a leader). It has arrived when it is within `arrivalGap` of the stop and slower than `arrivalSpeed`; it then unloads (pay, below), loads up to its capacity from the station's waiting cargo of the type it carries, and stands halted for `dwellSeconds + secondsPerUnit * (unloaded + loaded)`. While halted it is an obstacle to followers and the junction rules treat it as standing still outside the junction. After loading it retargets the next open station and turns toward it. A vehicle unloads everything at the next stop, so cargo has one origin (the previous stop of the line).
- **Pay.** `units * km * payPerUnitKm * share`, with `km` the straight-line distance between the two stations and `share` falling from 1 at `freeSeconds` of travel to `minPayShare` at `zeroSeconds`. Passengers and goods are separate cargo types (`cargo[]`).
- **Vehicle length.** `_length[]` per vehicle (a car is 4.5 m, a bus 10 m, a truck 8 m by the roster); gaps, collisions, lane changes, overtaking, spawning and junction poses use the right one. The junction conflict tables assume car-sized bodies, so a pair with a longer vehicle uses a second set of tables (`wideConflictDistance` 8 m and `wideBodyClearance` 6.1 m in the tuning file: a bus reaches 5 m ahead of its path). Widening the stretches by a guessed margin did not help; using the larger distances in the real geometry did (a 5 x 5 grid with 150 cars and 8 company vehicles: 3 junction collisions in 25 days became 0 over 12 seeds of 30 days). Width is still the car's 1.9 m for every vehicle.
- **Game layer.** The Lines panel (right) lists each line with its colour, stations, fleet, loading count and money, and has buttons to buy a vehicle for it (at the nearest depot with a way to the line) or delete it. The top bar shows the money, the standing and the date; the Company window has the books and the buttons "Ask the ministry for an advance of 100k" and "Repay 100k". Tools: **Station** (hover shows the spot, the price and the coverage radius) and **New line** (click stations in order, Enter finishes). Company vehicles are drawn at their real length, taller than cars, in their line's colour. `--selftest-lines` builds a road, a depot, two stations and two lines through the host API, runs 30 days and prints the books.

### 5.11 Towns and buildings (M3a)

Files: `Town.cs` (types), `TownGenerator.cs`, `RoadIndex.cs`, `Simulation.Towns.cs`; tuning in `towns` (section 8).

- **Time.** `economy.dayLength` was 600 s so that a commute fits inside a day (a bus crossing 900 m takes about a minute); since C1 it is 1200 s and a month is 3 days (5.23). `Simulation.Today` gives year, month and day of the month from the day counter (12-month years from `economy.startYear`, HUD only), `TimeOfDay` the share of the day gone (0 at midnight).
- **Generation.** `TownGenerator.Generate(net, seed, count)` runs before the `Simulation` exists, so its roads are free and not in the command log; a replay generates the same towns again from the same seed. It draws from its own `SimRandom` (seed xor a constant), so the simulation's stream and every old golden hash are untouched. For each town it tries random places (`attempts`) that are dry (`waterMargin` above the water surface everywhere under the disc around the grid), level (`maxHeightRange`), `minSpacing` from other towns and `edgeMargin` from the map edge; the first failure ends the search, so fewer towns than asked can come out (`WorldPlan.Requested` keeps the number asked). Streets are a square grid of 3 to 5 blocks of 120 m, rotated to run along the contours of the slope (a drawn angle on level ground), the line through the middle in one direction an avenue and the rest two-lane, all built with `RoadNetwork.BuildRoad` (so they cut and fill the terrain and cross water on bridges like any road). Buildings stand on the four sides of each block, two to a side, 30 m apart and centred so that neighbours at a block's corner do not touch; a building is as far from its street as an avenue is wide plus its own half-size plus a gap, so widening a street never reaches one. Three rings out from the middle (`rings`) set how many lots are built on, the mix of home, commerce and industry, and the size (three tiers each, `buildingTypes`). A building is kept only if a road really is within `accessRadius` of it. Names come from a shuffled list.
- **Model.** `Town` (name, centre, satisfaction) and `Building` (type, tier, position, facing, state, the day it entered that state) are plain classes in id-ordered lists; ids are never reused and a demolished building stays in the list. `Simulation.AddTowns(plan)` loads a `WorldPlan` once, before the first step. A 128 m grid of building ids answers `BuildingsNear`. The state hash includes the buildings and satisfaction only when there are any, so maps without towns hash as before.
- **Access and abandonment.** When the network version changes, `EvaluateAccess` builds a `RoadIndex` (a coarse grid of road centre lines) and sets each building Active (a road centre line within `accessRadius` of its middle) or Inactive. At the turn of each day an Inactive building older than `inactiveMonths` months becomes Abandoned (the town loses `abandonedPenalty` satisfaction) and an Abandoned one older than `abandonedMonths` is demolished by the town. A road that comes back before that makes it Active again. Each month every town regains `recoveryPerMonth` satisfaction, up to the maximum.
- **Roads through buildings.** A new road (`BuildRoadCommand`) or a node move (`MoveNodeCommand`, using `RoadNetwork.PlannedMoveRoads`) demolishes every building whose footprint (1.2 times its half-size) comes within half the road's width of the road's centre line. The price of each building (by type and tier) is added to the road's own price: the command is refused if the total cannot be paid, otherwise the buildings are demolished, the price is charged under `LedgerEntry.Demolition` and the town loses `demolishedPenalty` satisfaction per building. `EstimateRoadDemolition` and `EstimateMoveDemolition` give the same figures for the build preview.
- **Stations.** A station covers the Active buildings within `stations.coverageRadius` of its node (cached, refreshed when buildings, stations or nodes change). Until M3c it also collected passengers from them at a fixed rate per building; that is gone: on a map with towns people appear at a station only when a trip group queues there (5.13). On a map without towns the fixed `supplyPerDay` of the cargo list still applies (the older tests and the demo grid use it).
- **Game layer.** `SimHost.NewTownsWorld(seed, towns)` (hills terrain, towns, 30 ambient cars per town) returns the number of towns that fit; buildings are one MultiMesh of boxes (kind colour, grey when cut off, dark when abandoned) redrawn when `Simulation.BuildingsVersion` changes, with a floating name per town. The new-game screen has a Towns map (the first on offer) with a 2 to 10 slider; the HUD shows the date and time of day, a Towns window (people, jobs, buildings by kind, satisfaction bar, Go to) and, in the build preview, the buildings a road would demolish and what they cost; the station preview shows how many buildings it would serve.

### 5.12 Industries, cargo and building needs (M3b)

Files: `IndustryGenerator.cs`, `Simulation.Industries.cs`, changes in `Simulation.Fleet.cs`, `Simulation.Towns.cs`; tuning in `industries`, `cargo` and the `sustain` and `construction` lists of each building tier.

- **Cargo.** Eight types at M3b: passengers, goods, wood, planks, stone, materials (building materials), grain, food (ten more came with C2, 5.25: 18 entries in `cargo` now; C7 added `workers` as a cargo and C11 took it away again, 5.37). Chains: wood to planks (sawmill), stone to materials (cement works), grain to food (food plant), planks to goods (factory). The fixed `supplyPerDay` of a cargo only applies on a map with neither towns nor industries (`Settled` false), where every station also accepts everything; this keeps the demo grid and the older tests as they were.
- **Industries.** Seven types in `industries.types` at M3b (16 now: eight more in C2, 5.25, and the power plant in C6, 5.29): a primary industry has no inputs and makes `perMonth` of its output (amounts per month were divided by five when the month became 3 days, 5.23); a processor makes at most `perMonth`, each unit using the listed amounts of its inputs, and stops when an input runs out or the output stock (`maxOutput`) is full. Production is continuous (a month's amount spread over the month's ticks). Each step an industry's output goes, evenly, to the stations that cover it (centre within `stations.coverageRadius`), as far as they have room (`cargo.maxWaiting`); the rest stays in its output stock. `IndustryGenerator.Generate` runs after the towns: types in file order, `counts.few|normal|many` of each, on level dry ground (`maxHeightRange`) clear of every town's grid (`townClearance`), `minSpacing` apart, with its own random stream; a processor is first tried only where a producer of each of its inputs and a town lie within `generation.chainReach` (1500 m), and only when no such place turns up is it put anywhere that will do and listed in `WorldPlan.Unlinked`. Roads to industries are the player's.
- **Acceptance.** A station accepts a cargo when something it covers uses it: any standing building (passengers, whose destination is the station itself; the queues are per destination station, 5.13), a building whose tier lists it under `sustain` or `construction`, or an industry that lists it as an input. `Simulation.Accepts` is cached with the coverage, which is refreshed when buildings, stations or nodes change.
- **Freight trucks.** The truck's model cargo is `freight`: at a stop, when empty, it takes the freight cargo (anything but passengers) with the most waiting (at least one unit) among those the next stop on the line accepts; a bus takes passengers on the same rule. A load is unloaded only at a stop that accepts it; otherwise it stays on board and the vehicle takes nothing more. Pay is for what is taken, by the origin station's distance and the cargo's rate as before.
- **Deliveries.** `Distribute` splits the units over the buildings and industries in range that have room, by room (a building's room is what is missing from its monthly stock plus what is missing from its construction stock; an industry's is `maxInput` less its stock). Units beyond the total room are lost and not paid; when the units cover all the room every taker is filled exactly (5.16). A building fills its monthly stock first, then its construction stock.
- **Needs.** Each building starts with `initialStockMonths` months of its `sustain` amounts and holds at most `sustainStockMonths`. At the turn of each month every standing building uses up its monthly amounts; the share of the town's needs that were missing is `Town.UnmetShare`, and the town loses `unmetPenalty` times that. The construction stock fills and M3d spends it on growth (5.14).
- **Game layer.** `NewTownsWorld(seed, towns, industries)`; industries are boxes in the buildings MultiMesh with their names above them; the Industries window lists output, stock and inputs, the Stations window what is waiting and what each station accepts, the Towns window the share of needs met; the new-game screen has Few, Normal and Many.

### 5.13 Trip groups (M3c)

Files: `Trips.cs` (`TripGroup`, `TripMode`, `TripLeg`, `PaxLot`), `Simulation.Trips.cs`, `Simulation.Transit.cs`, car routing in `Simulation.Routing.cs`; tuning in `trips`.

- **Groups.** `AddTowns` makes the groups once: the people of each home split into groups of at most `groupSize` (10; a tier 1 home of 20 people has two). A group has its home, a workplace, a commerce building for the evening and a car or none (`carShare`). The workplace is drawn weighted by the jobs not yet taken (workplaces fill up, and crowd when all are taken) times nearness `1 / (1 + (d / nearness)^2)` within `workRadius`, so most work in their own town and some in the next; leisure the same by size. All draws come from their own `SimRandom` (seed xor a constant), so maps without towns and every old golden hash are untouched. At the turn of each day a group that is at home and whose workplace or leisure place is gone (demolished, abandoned, cut off) draws a new one. A group whose home is demolished is retired at the next turn of the day it is not travelling (`TripGroup.Retired`, `People` 0, no workplace or leisure place, never departs again) and the jobs it held go back to its workplace (5.16).
- **Schedule.** Four trips a day: out to work, back, out for leisure, back. Each starts at its group's own point inside the window of the tuning file (`windows`, in hours: 7 to 9, 16 to 18, 18 to 21, 21 to 22.5) plus a fixed jitter up to `jitterMinutes` (45), both drawn once from the group's seed, so a group leaves at the same time every day. A trip never starts before the previous one is over (a slow trip pushes the day back); a leisure trip is made on `leisureChance` of the days (a pure function of group and day), stays at least `leisureStayMinutes`, and is dropped for the day when the group is already past its window. A group without a workplace skips the work trips. Since C1 a day is 1200 s, so a window is about 100 s long (50 s on the 600 s day of M3c) and a 100 m walk takes under a minute.
- **Mode.** Within `walkRadius` (150 m) or when both buildings are reached from the same road node, walk. Otherwise the lowest generalised cost, seconds plus `moneyWeight` times money, of what the group can use: by car (if it has one, from a road node within `carAccessRadius` of each building; the field of the destination node gives the time, plus `carOverheadSeconds`; money is `carCostPerKm`), and by public transport (a station covering each building and a line that has both and at least one vehicle: walk, wait `transitWaitShare` (0.5) of the time between vehicles, which is the line's round trip over its fleet (5.17), ride at `transitRideFactor` times the car time, walk; money is the passenger pay rate). Direct lines only. Nothing usable means the trip is not made (the group stays; counted as "could not go"), except the way home, which is a walk however long. Choices are cached per pair of buildings and car flag and made again when the road network, the lines, their vehicles or the coverage change.
- **Walking** is a timer: `walkDetour` times the straight line over `walkSpeed` (2.5 m/s, quicker than life so that a short walk stays short beside a drive in a day of 1200 s), no pathfinding, no entity. The arrival of every walk, of the last leg of a bus ride and of any other timer is an event in a priority queue (tick, then order of scheduling).
- **Cars.** A car stands for `peoplePerCar` (5) people. A group's cars are queued for the road and put there one every `carDepartGapSeconds`, a few tries per step (`carSpawnAttempts`), at most `maxCars` (200) on the road at once, on the lane out of the origin's access node with room and the shortest way to the destination (`carSpawnClearance` beyond its length; not while a car is about to arrive at that node). The car is an ordinary vehicle (IDM, lane changes, overtaking, incidents) that routes at each junction by the field of its destination node (the same backward Dijkstra as stations, over the lanes into the node, cached per network version). It is taken off the road within `carArriveRange` (30 m) of the end of the lane into that node, so a queue at the destination does not block the junction; after `carTimeoutSeconds` (400) a car still on the road is taken off too and its people count as arrived, so a jam clears itself slowly; a wreck that is cleared counts as arrived as well. A group whose cars never find room is released after the same time.
- **Public transport.** A group walks to the best station and joins its queue as a lot (group, people, destination station, since when). A bus (when towns exist) unloads the lots whose destination is the station it stands at, paid by the distance between the lot's stations and the time on board, then takes on the lots waiting there whose destination is another stop of its line (queue order, lots split to fit), up to its capacity. People get off only at their own destination, walk to their building and arrive; a lot whose destination station closed is let off wherever the bus is. People who have waited `transitGiveUpSeconds` (900) or whose station closed give up. A bus that leaves the world (sold, wreck cleared) puts its people down where they set out from. `Station.Waiting` for passengers mirrors the queue (the HUD still reads it) and `Station.WaitingFor()` splits it by destination. Without towns buses still load the fixed supply as before.
- **Counts.** Each town counts the people-trips of the day (and of the day before) by mode, including those that could not be made. A group keeps the mode and duration of its last completed trip (for M3d's connectivity).
- **State.** The hash includes the groups, queues, riders and the trip stream only when groups exist. Trip cars, groups and queues replay from the seed and the command log (`TripsReplayFromTheCommandLog`).
- **Performance** (measured on the 600 s day, before C1). Five towns with about 3200 people and 330 groups: a mean of 0.04 ms a step in a Release build, 0.15 ms at the 99th percentile, 3 ms at worst, up to about 275 vehicles on the road in the rush; the 1000-vehicle step test is unchanged. In the morning rush a car takes two to ten times the free-flow time from the field (junctions with many cars, queues at destinations); the generalised cost does not yet charge for that.

### 5.14 Growth (M3d)

Files: `Simulation.Growth.cs`; lots in `TownGenerator.LotSites`; `Town` (grid, `Connectivity`, `LastGrowth`), `Lot`; tuning `towns.growth`.

- **Lots.** A lot is a place beside a street where a building can stand: block (column, row), side, lot on the side. Its middle is as far from the street as the biggest size needs, whatever stands on it, so a building can grow in place (generated towns moved a little when this went in). A town keeps the lots of its grid; the buildings of the plan are matched to the lots they stand on (within 1 m; hand-placed ones belong to none), and a demolished building frees its lot. A free lot can be built on when it has a road within `accessRadius` and none closer than `growth.lotClearance`, is dry and on the map, and has no standing building within 22 m or industry on it.
- **Connectivity.** At the turn of each month, a town's connectivity is the share of its active homes from which a group completed an outbound work or leisure trip within `timeBudgetSeconds` (300) that month (`TripGroup.ReachedMonth`). A town starts at 1; one whose people can reach nothing (the road is cut, the workplaces are in another town with no link) falls to 0.
- **Gates.** The same turn, a town whose satisfaction is below `minSatisfaction` (50) or whose connectivity is below `minConnectivity` (0.6) does not grow, and says which (`LastGrowth.Blocked`). It never shrinks except by the abandonment of 5.11.
- **Growth.** For each active building in id order (buildings founded this month wait): when its construction stock holds the whole `construction` list of its size, it spends it and goes up a size; a home gets the new people as new trip groups (from the next day). A building at the top size spends the same list to found a new smallest building on the free lot of its town nearest to it (kind drawn from the mix of the lot's ring, from a stream of its own, seed xor a constant); a home founded gets its groups. A town with no free lot, and `extendGrid`, first adds a column or row of blocks on the side of the grid nearest to the parent: dry ground, no building or industry in the strip, then the streets (the line of each existing street continued by one block, the main street staying an avenue, and a closing street) built with `RoadNetwork.BuildRoad`, free and outside the command log (a replay grows the same way), and the lots of the new blocks. It tries the other sides in turn; with none it waits. The sim picks the new streets up at once (access, routing); the renderer redraws the roads when the network version moves.
- **Observed** (600 s day, before C1). Four generated towns, nothing delivered: no growth, and satisfaction falls about 2 a month once the starting stock is gone and the town stalls at 50 after about 7 months. With every building topped up to full construction stock every day (a cheat, to see the ceiling): people 3200 to 64000 in 8 months, with about 100 new buildings and a block or two a town each month, until the trip car cap (300) keeps people from getting where they go and connectivity falls under the gate, which slows the towns again.

### 5.15 Line editing, windows, bays and balance (M2 follow-ups, first M3 balance pass)

Files: `Simulation.Fleet.cs`, `Station.cs`, `Simulation.Trips.cs`, `game/hud.gd`, `game/SimHost.cs`; probes in `BalanceProbe.cs`, `Worlds.cs`.

The balance figures in this section and in 5.16 were measured before C1, with a day of 600 s and a month of 30 days (pre-C1); the prices and pay rates were not changed by C1, but flows per hour moved (5.23).

- **Editing a line.** `EditLineCommand(line, stations)` replaces the stops (same rules as creating: at least two, open, none twice in a row, the last not the first). Each vehicle of the line keeps heading for its station when the new list has it and takes its new place in the list; when its station was dropped it heads for the stop at the same place (the last if the list is shorter) and takes the new turn at the next junction it has not yet entered (a vehicle inside a junction keeps its path). People on a bus whose station is no longer on the line are put down at the next stop. `RenameLineCommand` names a line (1 to 24 characters; not in the state hash). Both are commands, so they replay.
- **What stations and vehicles remember.** A station counts the people who boarded, got off and gave up waiting today and yesterday (`Station.Today`, `Yesterday`); a vehicle counts the upkeep paid for it (`UpkeepPaidFor`) next to its income, so its profit is known. None of the counters change behaviour; the vehicle upkeep is in `FleetHash`.
- **Windows.** The Lines window has a name box, the stops with up, down and remove buttons, an "add stop" choice and the buy and delete buttons. The Stations window names each station for its town or industry and number ("Aldermark 2"), lists the lines that stop there, what it covers (homes, shops, other workplaces, industries), waiting people by destination, and the counts of yesterday and today. The Vehicles window lists every company vehicle: what it is, where it is heading, its load, income, upkeep and profit, with Go to and Sell. All of it is assembled in `SimHost.GetLines`, `GetStations`, `GetVehicles`; the Godot scripts only emit intent.
- **Bays.** A station built with bays (`stations.bayPrice` more to build, `bayUpkeepPerDay` more a day) takes a loading vehicle on its right-most lane out of the lane: `_bay[i]` removes the vehicle from the per-lane ordering (`RebuildOrder` skips it, `_orderCount` is the number ordered), so followers, collision checks, right of way, spawn checks and overtaking never see it, and it is drawn 3.4 m to the right of the lane. Room is `stations.bayCapacity` vehicles at a time per station; a vehicle that finds them taken loads in the lane as before. A vehicle leaves its bay when no vehicle is within `bayExitGap` (30 m) behind it and none overlaps it or is within the spawn clearance ahead; until then it waits. `Station.InBay`, `Simulation.InBay(v)`, the bay flag is in `FleetHash`.
- **Congestion estimate.** For each hour of the day the simulation keeps how many times longer than the empty-road time a car trip took (`_congestion[24]`, 1 at the start); every car trip that ends moves the estimate for the hour it set out in by `trips.congestionSmoothing` (0.15) of the way to what it took, at most `trips.maxCongestion` (6). The car's seconds in the choice of mode are the empty-road ride times the estimate plus the overhead, so a jammed rush hour sends people with cars to the lines (or leaves their trip made on foot or not made). It is part of the trip hash, and of the mode cache key and version (in quarters), so a choice is made again when people's idea of the jam moves. Without a company line there is nothing to switch to, so on a map with no lines it changes nothing.
- **Cars that stand still.** A trip car that has moved at less than 0.3 m/s for `trips.carStuckSeconds` (90 s, 7.5 percent of a day since C1) is taken off like one that ran out of time, its people counted as arrived (they gave up and walked). The longer `carTimeoutSeconds` stays as the limit for a car that crawls.
- **Pay and coverage, measured.** Probes (`BalanceProbe`, run with `BALANCE_PROBE=1`; settings by environment variable) put a six-stop passenger line with four buses across the two closest of four towns on six seeds, ten days each, and a three-truck line from a forest to a sawmill. Before: coverage 90 m, passengers 40 per unit-km: 30 to 65 people a day carried per line, a mean income of 250 a day against 700 to 1300 of upkeep, and the trucks 930 to 2000 a day each against 450 (three to five times a bus). After: coverage 150 m, passengers 65, freight about 55 percent of its old rates (wood 16, planks 22, stone 14, materials 25, grain 15, food 28, goods 30): buses earn about 2 to 3 times their upkeep on the middling seeds (900 to 7600 a day on four buses, one seed barely pays because its buses crawl through the rush at 1.2 m/s), trucks 1.5 to 2.5 times. `BalanceTests` pins the ranges: a bus line over 1.3 times upkeep, a truck line between 1.3 and 4.5.
- **Bugs found on the way** (both wrecked buses in town traffic; 16 of 24 buses were lost in ten days on the probe maps, 7 after the first fix, 2 after both, the rest overtaking accidents): (1) the rear-end check across a junction compared a car waiting to merge with the first vehicle of the exit lane along the car's own short turn, so a 10 m bus entering that lane from another street seemed to overlap it; it now only applies to a vehicle that came by the follower's lane (`_prevLane`). (2) The room to spawn compared only the distance between fronts, so a trip car could be put on the road inside the body of a bus ahead; `IsClearFor` counts the real length of the vehicles already there.

### 5.17 The medium-priority block (2026-10-19)

Files: `Simulation.Transit.cs`, `Simulation.Towns.cs`, `Simulation.Stations.cs`, `Simulation.Fleet.cs`, `SimCommand.cs`, `game/SimHost.cs`, `game/hud.gd`, `game/main.gd`, `game/settings.gd`.

- **Transit wait from the round trip.** `trips.transitWaitSeconds` (240 s for a line with one vehicle, divided by its vehicles) became `trips.transitWaitShare` (0.5). `Simulation.LineCycleSeconds(line)` is the time one vehicle takes to go once round a line: the road time of every leg from a stop to the next (the last back to the first, as a car would drive it, times `transitRideFactor`) plus the standing time (`dwellSeconds`) at every stop; infinity when a leg has no road. The cycles are counted again when lines, fleets or roads change. A vehicle comes every cycle over fleet seconds, several lines that share two stations add their arrivals, and people wait `transitWaitShare` of the time between vehicles. A longer line therefore makes people wait longer and a line cut in two brings nobody. `TripTests` pin: the wait is the share of the round trip over the fleet (a second bus halves it), a longer line has a longer round trip, a cut line has none and brings nobody.
- **What a town still needs.** `Simulation.NeedsOf(town)` lists, for each cargo its active buildings use or build with, how many are short of this month's need, the units that need lacks and the units of construction stock missing for growth, and how many of the buildings an open station covers. The Towns window shows "Your stations reach n of m buildings" (and, when it is none, that nothing can be delivered), "Short of this month's needs" with the cargo and the number of buildings, and "Still missing to grow" with the units per cargo.
- **Connectivity view.** `Building.Reached` is set for each active home at the monthly evaluation (true until the first one). The Towns window has a "Show connectivity on the map" switch (`connectivity_view_toggled`, `SimHost.SetConnectivityView`): homes are drawn green when someone living there reached work or leisure within the time budget last month, red when nobody did, and everything else is dimmed. The buildings are redrawn when the switch moves and when a home changes (`BuildingsVersion`).
- **By hand.** Four commands, all replayable: `SetLineColorCommand(line, 0xRRGGBB)` (`Line.Color`, -1 until chosen; the game picks one from the line's number until then), `RenameStationCommand` (`Station.Name`, 1 to 24 characters), `CloseStationCommand` (the station is inactive as when its roads are lost: lines skip it, vehicles retarget, people waiting give up; no refund; a new station can stand at its node) and `AddStationBaysCommand` (the bay price, once). The colour and the names are not part of the state hash; closing and bays are. The Lines window has a colour button on each line (the command is sent when the picker closes); the Stations window has a name box, "Add bays (price)" while there are none and "Close" with a confirmation, since it cannot be undone.
- **Bays as a pocket.** `Simulation.BayLanes(station)` gives the right-most lane of each approach and where a halted vehicle's front stands; `SimHost.RebuildStations` draws, beside that lane and as far back as `BayLength` (capacity times a bus and a gap), a slab of road paving where the bay vehicles stand (3.4 m to the right of the lane), next to the station's platform. The stations have a root of their own, redrawn when one is built, closed or given bays, so the roads are not rebuilt for it.
- **Window sizes.** 32 sizes instead of 16 in `settings.gd`.

### 5.19 Rail (M4, 2026-10-20)

Files: `RailNetwork.cs`, `RailTuning.cs`, `Simulation.Rail.cs`, `Simulation.Trains.cs`, `Simulation.Crossings.cs`; `data/tuning.json` section `rail`; tests `RailNetworkTests`, `TrainTests`, `SignalTests`, `CrossingTests`, `RailBehaviourTests`, `RailEconomyTests`, `MixedNetworkTests`, `RailDeterminismTests`. The decisions are `docs/m4-plan.md` and ADR 0004.

- **Network.** `RailNetwork` (owned by the simulation, `Simulation.Rail`; it uses the road network's terrain) holds nodes, tracks and signals. A `Track` is one piece between two nodes, straight or an arc (`RoadGeometry`), single and used both ways, with a speed limit made from `rail.speedLimit` reduced for the curve (`curveLateralAcceleration`) and the grade. Building (`BuildTrack`) follows the road rules: it joins nodes, splits the tracks it ends on or crosses (a junction, never a diamond), refuses what is too short, too steep (`maxGrade` 4 %), too tight (`minRadius` 90 m), in water or with a junction too close to another, puts bridges and tunnels where the road rules would (`maxFillDepth`, `maxCutDepth`) and carves the ground with cut and fill. A node connects two of its tracks when the turn is at most `maxTurnAngle`; `TurnSpeed` is the limit through the bend. Splitting a track keeps its signals and remaps the trains on it.
- **Boundaries and reservation.** A node is a boundary when a signal, a station or a depot stands there. A train holds tracks and nodes (`_trackOwner`, `_nodeOwner`) and moves only on what it holds. It asks for the stretch beyond the end of what it holds when its front is within braking distance plus `reserveLookahead` of it: a **block** request takes every track reachable from the boundary node up to the next boundaries (the train's route or not), a **path** request only the tracks and nodes of the route up to the next boundary. The kind comes from the signal that faces the track the train arrives by (none counts as block; a stretch that starts at a depot or a station is a path). The boundary node where a stretch ends is not part of it (a train waiting at a signal has not entered the node), except the node of the station the train is stopping at. What a train holds is released piece by piece as its rear passes (`Hold.Hi`). A train that cannot get the stretch stops `stopMargin` short of the boundary and waits; after `RerouteAfterSeconds` it looks for another way to its station over track nobody else holds (`TryReroute`), which is how two trains pass at a loop.
- **Movement.** Per step a train works out the speed it may have (`Drive`): the vehicle's top speed; the stop at the end of what it holds (a station exactly, a red signal `stopMargin` short), braking at `brakePlanShare` of the service brake; the limit of every track and turn any part of the train is on or will reach (a limit applies until the rear has passed); a crossing it must not enter. It then accelerates (the smaller of the tractive effort at standstill and power over speed, less rolling, air and grade resistance, for the mass with the load) or brakes (service, emergency when too fast by 3 m/s). A train can never pass the end of what it holds. Route positions are distances along the planned route (`RouteStep` list) and shift when steps behind the rear are dropped.
- **Routes.** `FindRailPath` is Dijkstra over (track, direction) by travel time at the posted limits, with the turn rule at each node, ties by track number. All trains are push-pull: standing still with the whole train on its route it may reverse (`ReverseRoute`, which mirrors route, holds and coordinates) when that reaches the next station sooner than carrying on, with a 25 s penalty; a station at a dead end needs no search at all.
- **Stations, depots, lines.** `Station.Mode` is `Road` or `Rail`; a rail station has `RailNode` and a map position (`Simulation.PointOf`), `Node` is -1. Coverage, acceptance, queues, pay and the trip modes work from the position, so towns and industries serve a rail station like a road one. A `Line` has a mode (`CreateLineCommand` reads it from its first station; mixing is refused). `BuildRailStation` splits a track to make a node; `AddRailDepot` needs a rail node. `BuyTrain` (the depot is `BuyTrainCommand.NearestDepot` from the game) takes a locomotive and a wagon by id and a count, needs the way from the depot to the line's first station (a depot on the first station starts the train loading there); the train stands in the depot (its body behind the node) and drives out once the depot's node is free (see 5.21). A train carries one wagon type: passengers (the queues of 5.13, the same boarding and alighting code as buses) or freight (the cargo with most waiting that the next stop accepts, as a truck). Upkeep is charged daily per train and per rail station.
- **Deadlocks.** Every 2 s of simulated time trains that have waited for track for `blockedSeconds` are followed from the train that holds what they need; a ring is recorded once as a `RailWarning` (the game shows it as a message and paints the trains red). Selling one of the trains ends it. Two trains turning round at the ends of one single track meet head on and deadlock sooner or later: that needs a passing loop with path signals, as in the tests.
- **Level crossings.** A `LevelCrossing` is found (`AddCrossingsFound`, after a track or a road is built) where a track and a road cross within `rail.crossings.clearance` in height, and is re-resolved to its current track and road whenever either network changes. It is refused (`TrackCrossingsAllowed`, `RoadCrossingsAllowed`) closer than `minFromNode` to the end of the road, of the track or a junction, or at less than `minAngle`; moving a road junction so that its roads would cross a track is refused too; towns do not extend their streets within `RailClearance` of a track. States `Open`, `Lowering` (`lowerSeconds`), `Closed`: a train that will cross (the crossing is inside what it holds) and will reach it within `warnSeconds + lowerSeconds` closes it; it opens `clearSeconds` after the last train has cleared. Road vehicles get a stop line `roadStopMargin` before the band the rails take (`CrossingInteraction`, in the step loop beside the junction rule): while the barriers are coming down or down, or while the vehicle ahead is standing so close beyond the crossing that this one would stop on the rails; one that can no longer stop goes on. A train brakes for a crossing whose barriers are not down only if it would otherwise reach it (emergency braking profile), and for one with a wreck or a standing vehicle in it from far off. A train in the band and a car in it is a collision (`IncidentKind.LevelCrossing`): the car is wrecked, a crew is called as for any wreck, the train halts for `collisionHalt` seconds, and the crossing is blocked for trains until the wreck is cleared.
- **Hash.** The rail mixes into the state hash only when a track, depot or train exists, so road-only worlds keep their hashes. `RailDeterminismTests` pins a scenario (railway with a loop and path signals, a road across it, two trains, a bus line, ambient traffic; 20 minutes): The pinned value has moved with every change that touched the hashed state or the cargo list (the prices of 5.22, C1, C2, C4, C7); the current value is the one in 5.30, the same on macOS and Linux.
- **Numbers.** In the shipped tuning a diesel locomotive (18 m, 90 t, 1.6 MW, 220 kN, 36 m/s, 120 000, upkeep 2000 a day) pulls up to 8 coaches (48 people, 20 m, 36 000, 400 a day) or freight wagons (40 units, 16 m, 30 000, 300 a day): a four-coach train costs about 3600 a day to keep; track costs 110 a metre, a rail station 20 000, a rail depot 30 000 (the balance pass of 5.22; the first prices were 160, 30 000, 45 000 and 140 000 for the locomotive). C8 added a steam locomotive (70 000, 26 m/s, 6 wagons, 2800 a day) and the diesel from 1942 (5.31). Pre-C1 figures, on the 600 s day: upkeep was half (1000 for the locomotive, 200 and 150 for the coach and wagon, 1800 a day for four coaches against a bus line of the same capacity for about 2500); a four-coach line between two towns 750 m apart earned about 4000 a day against 1800 upkeep in `RailEconomyTests`; a two-wagon freight train from a forest to a sawmill 1.2 to 1.5 times its upkeep. Rail pays better the more there is to carry: the first forest alone barely feeds one train.

### 5.20 Traffic lights (M4, 2026-10-20)

Files: `Simulation.Lights.cs`, `TrafficLightTuning` (`roads.trafficLight`), `TrafficLightTests`. `BuildTrafficLightCommand(node)` works at a node with at least three roads, once, for `price` (12 000, 120 a day). The approaches (lanes running into the node) fall in two groups by the axis they come along (within 45 degrees of the first lane's axis, either way, or the other group); the groups take turns at green for `greenSeconds` (18) with `clearSeconds` (4) of red for both between; the cycle is a function of the tick and the node (an offset of 37 ticks a node id), so there is no state to replay. `JunctionInteraction` returns the red-light stop first: a vehicle that can still stop stops at the edge of the junction; others go on. Signs for priority, yield and stop are 5.22.

### 5.21 Leaving the depot freely, and the garage (2026-10-21)

Files: `Simulation.Garage.cs`, ghosts in `Simulation.Fleet.cs`, trains in `Simulation.Trains.cs` and `Simulation.Rail.cs`; tests `GarageTests`.

- **Nothing refuses a purchase for lack of room.** A road vehicle bought for a line comes out of the depot on the lane with the quickest way to the line's first station. When the road at the depot is clear it is an ordinary vehicle at once (as before). When another vehicle stands in the doorway or one is about to arrive, it is a **ghost** (`IsGhost`) that waits **inside the depot** (`IsInDepot`; not on the road, not drawn) until there is room: nobody within `SpawnClearance` of its place, nothing about to arrive at the node (`TrafficNear`), and no ghost ahead of it in the queue (vehicles bought together come out one after another, in the order of purchase). It then drives out at up to 6 m/s, out of the lane ordering (like a vehicle in a bay), so cars do not brake for it; it never goes where another vehicle is (it stops short of any vehicle ahead on its lane) and becomes an ordinary vehicle once its whole length is out and the lane has room (`StepGhosts`). It stops short of the junction if there never is room. Cars are not made to stop for a ghost (it only exists at a depot exit); a car that reaches the doorway while a ghost is out can still drive through it, and a ghost is not seen by level crossings (decided, `m4-review.md`). A train bought while another still stands on the depot's node waits **in the depot** (`Train.InDepot`, status `InDepot`, cars not drawn): it holds nothing, and takes the depot node, by train number, when it is free. Only a missing way from the depot to the first station, a closed depot or no money refuses a purchase.
- **The garage.** A vehicle or train can be bought for no line (`BuyParkedVehicleCommand`, `BuyParkedTrainCommand`; `AnyDepot` takes the first open depot): it is paid for and waits in the depot as a `ParkedVehicle` (`Simulation.Garage`; it pays `economy.parkedUpkeepShare` (10 %) of its daily upkeep while it waits, charged to the company and to no line, `ChargeGarageUpkeep`). `AssignParkedCommand(parked, line)` puts it on a road or rail line of its own kind: it comes out of its depot like a bought vehicle (any open depot with a way to the line when its own is closed); `WhyNotAssign` says in words why it cannot (wrong kind of line, no road or track from the depot, closed station). `SellParkedCommand` sells it for the usual share.
- **Sending back.** `ParkVehicleCommand` takes a road vehicle off its line (it stops loading, drops what it carries, and drives to the nearest depot it can reach by the same routing trip cars use, `_returnDepot`; at the depot it goes into the garage; people on board get off). `ParkTrainCommand` does the same for a train (`Train.ReturnDepot`: `PlanLeg` then plans to the depot's node instead of a station; a train still in its depot is parked at once). A returning vehicle no longer counts in its line's fleet. The old one-step commands (`BuyVehicleCommand`, `BuyTrainCommand`) remain and buy and assign in one go.
- **Hash.** Ghost, returning and garage state are mixed in only when present, so the old golden hashes are unchanged.
- **Game.** The Vehicles window has a depot choice and buy buttons (road depot: buses and trucks; rail depot: trains), a list of vehicles waiting in depots (a choice of line of the right kind, **Put on line**, Go to, Sell), and a **To depot** button on every vehicle on the road. Buying from the Lines window still sends the vehicle straight out and now says exactly why it could not.

### 5.22 Rail balance, road signs and the benchmark job (2026-10-21)

Files: `Simulation.Signs.cs`, `RoadSignTuning` (`roads.signs`), `game/SimHostSigns.cs`, `RoadSignTests`, `StepBenchmarks`, `RailBalanceProbe`, `.github/workflows/sim-tests.yml`.

- **Rail balance.** `RailBalanceProbe` (`RAIL_PROBE=1 dotnet test --filter RailBalanceProbe`) lays a four-coach line against six buses (and two freight wagons against four trucks) between every pair of towns on eight maps and prints capital, income, upkeep and payback. Findings: a railway cost about 2.5 times the buses (track 160 a metre against a road at 40, plus a 140 000 locomotive) and paid back later on every pair a bus line was already good on; freight earns the same by rail and by truck (the industry's output is the limit, and pay only falls after 240 s, which no distance in reach comes to) so rail never paid back sooner. Prices lowered: track 160 to 110 a metre, rail station 30 000 to 20 000, rail depot 45 000 to 30 000, locomotive 140 000 to 120 000. Now a railway costs 1.9 to 2.3 times the buses for the same seats and earns more a day on the pairs where the buses fill up; `RailEconomyTests.ARailwayCostsAboutTwiceWhatBusesCostForTheSameSeats_AndEarnsMore` pins that. Not changed, and worth a decision: no passenger line earns anything between towns more than about 1.8 km apart, because trips never go beyond `trips.workRadius`, so rail's speed buys nothing on a long line; freight is production-limited, so a railway pays only when it carries several industries or a long haul.
- **Signs.** `BuildRoadSignCommand(x, z, kind)` and `RemoveRoadSignCommand(x, z)` act on the approach nearest the click at a junction of three roads or more without lights. Kinds: priority, yield, stop; a priority sign also goes on the approach across the junction. A sign is found again by the heading of its approach (`RoadSign.Angle`, tolerance 0.3 rad), so it survives a split of the road and goes when no road comes that way. Every lane running into a junction has a rank (priority 0, no sign 1, yield or stop 2). In `JunctionInteraction` a vehicle that has not entered the junction ignores the lower-ranked vehicles that have not entered either, and waits for higher-ranked ones whatever the order of arrival unless it can be through before they arrive (the existing test); equal ranks keep the arrival order. A vehicle that gives way looks `approachRange` (90 m) back for cross traffic and starts to brake for a stop line from as far. A stop sign holds the vehicle until it has stood still (below `stopSpeed`) within `stopLine` of the edge for `stopSeconds` (`_stopHold`, zeroed on every new lane and mixed into the hash only when signs exist). Ranks are a partial order, so signs cannot make a ring of vehicles wait for each other; a vehicle that cannot stop in time still goes on, as with lights. Prices 700 (priority, each), 500 (yield, stop); no upkeep. Signs at a junction with lights are dormant. Road-only worlds without signs keep their hashes.
- **Game.** Tools Priority road, Yield sign, Stop sign, Remove sign in the Modify category (`build_tool.gd` `SIGN_TOOLS`, a marker on the post), plates drawn as boxes on a post at the right of each approach (`SimHostSigns.DrawSigns`). Compiled and the GDScript parsed; not played.
- **Benchmark job.** `StepBenchmarks` (only with `BENCH` set) measures the step in a Release build: 1000 vehicles on a grid 0.3 ms, 20 trains of 8 wagons on parallel lines 0.01 ms, a map in a rush day with 8 buses and 6 trains 0.02 ms (Apple M4). Each world has an average budget about seven times that and all share a 99th-percentile budget of 6.25 ms (a step at 8x speed). The CI job `benchmark` runs it on Ubuntu and writes the table to the job summary. Trains cost almost nothing next to road traffic, so there is nothing to optimise in them.

### 5.23 The campaign time scale (C1, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`economy`, `towns`, `industries`, `cargo`, upkeep fields), `Simulation.Economy.cs`, `EconomyTuning.StartYear`, `hud.gd`. No new mechanism: the time scale is data, so C1 is a retune plus a calendar that starts in January 1938.

- **The rule.** Every flow keeps its value per second. A day is 1200 s (twice as long): every per-day amount is doubled (upkeep of vehicles, trains, stations, bays, traffic lights; `interestPerDay` 0.004 to 0.008; `supplyPerDay`; `bankruptcyDays` 73 to 36). A month is 3 days = 3600 s (a fifth of what it was): every per-month amount is divided by five (the `perMonth` of the seven industries; the `sustain` and `construction` amounts of the nine building tiers, for instance a tier 1 home eats 4 food a month instead of 20 and a tier 1 building needs 8 building materials instead of 40). Ratios per month (stock against need, deliveries against need) are therefore the same as before, so growth, satisfaction and abandonment run at the same pace per month, which now takes a fifth of the clock time. Satisfaction points, thresholds and the month counts (`inactiveMonths`, `recoveryPerMonth`) are not scaled: they are outcomes of those ratios.
- **What was left alone, and why.** Pay decay (`freeSeconds`, `zeroSeconds`) and the other numbers in seconds (trip time budget, waiting, crews, signals) are physical times and do not depend on the day. The windows of the trip schedule are hours of the day, so they stretch with it (the morning rush is now 100 s long instead of 50). `walkSpeed` stays 2.5 m/s.
- **What moves because of it.** People still make four trips a day, so passenger traffic per second is half what it was; measured on eight maps the bus line's income per hour fell by about 10 percent on average (the buses fill more evenly over a longer rush), so passenger fares were not touched. Freight is unchanged per second. The campaign's budget (C4) is set against these figures.
- **Calendar.** `economy.startYear` is 1938: day 0 is 1 January 1938, day 35 is 3 December 1938, day 287 is 3 December 1945 (the last day of the campaign). The HUD shows "2 Mar 1938   14:30".
- **Tests.** 445 (a calendar theorem grew to six cases). Tests that carried a literal number of the old scale now read it from the tuning (upkeep, building needs); the balance and rail economy tests measure in periods of 600 s (`Worlds.Period`) so their windows and thresholds keep their meaning; a few runs that waited for a half day wait a quarter. Pinned hashes: the rail scenario hash moved to `0x6B1D2292DC4DE3EF` (since moved again, see 5.30) and is the same on macOS and Linux; the road golden hashes did not move.

### 5.24 Condition, maintenance and full incidents (M5, 2026-10-21)

Files: `MaintenanceTuning.cs` and the `maintenance` section of `data/tuning.json`, `Simulation.Condition.cs` (wear, hazard, policy, commands), `Simulation.Repairs.cs` (jobs, bays, service trips, towing, depot upgrade), `Simulation.Signals.cs`, `Simulation.Consequences.cs`, `Simulation.RoadCondition.cs`, `Simulation.Events.cs`; the game layer is `SimHostMaintenance.cs`, `hud.gd`, `main_menu.gd`. Plan `docs/m5-plan.md`, decision ADR 0005.

- **Condition (5a).** 0 to 100 for every road vehicle, every train (one value for the consist) and every parked entry of the garage; ambient cars have none. It starts at 100 and falls with the distance driven (`metresPerPoint` 1500), 30 % faster with a full load and 1.5 times as fast after a Patch until the next Service or Overhaul. `StepCondition` runs after movement and draws from the one RNG in a fixed order (vehicles by slot, then trains by id): a breakdown has a hazard per metre that is zero from 60 up, rises as the square to `hazardPerKmAtFloor` at 20 and linearly to `hazardPerKmAtZero` at 0, times the frequency scale and, on a worn road, `RoadRiskFactor`. Brakes fade below 60 (to 0.6 of the braking at 0), which feeds rear-end crashes; top speed never falls.
- **Breakdown.** A vehicle becomes a Wreck of an `IncidentKind.Breakdown` incident with `FromCondition`; the cargo is lost; once the crew has cleared it, it is towed to the garage of the nearest depot (by road, else by straight line) with `wreckCondition` 5. Only wrecks of that kind are towed: a company vehicle wrecked in a collision still vanishes as before. A train that breaks down stands `trainBreakdownSeconds` holding its track (a crew is abstract), then drives to the nearest rail depot as towed (it cannot break down again on the way) and waits in the garage. A breakdown below Condition 15 is negligence and fined.
- **Policy.** `MaintenancePolicy(Mode, EveryDays, BelowCondition, Kind)` with modes Inherit, Off and On, set for the Company (default Service every 60 days or below 40), for a Line, for a vehicle or for a train by `SetMaintenancePolicyCommand`; `EffectivePolicyOf` says which applies and where from. `RequestRepairCommand` asks for a repair at the next stop (or, for a parked vehicle, takes it into a bay of its depot). `SetFrequencyCommand` and the `Simulation` constructor argument set the frequency option (Off, Low, Normal, High; scales 0, 0.5, 1, 2); it scales breakdowns and signal failures only, not the accidents that come out of the driving.
- **A due vehicle.** At a stop (`BeginStop`, `BeginTrainStop`) it is due when the policy says so or a repair was asked for. It unloads what the stop takes, puts the rest down (people make their own way, freight is lost), loads nothing, and on leaving drives to the nearest depot that can do the repair (`ServiceDepotFor`; for a train `NearestRailDepotFor`). A vehicle goes inside as a ghost that waits in the depot (`_servicing`, not drawn, never leaves the queue of `StepGhosts`) and a train leaves the track (`TrainStatus.Servicing`, route cleared). If no depot can do it, the vehicle keeps running and the game warns once (`SimEventKind.NoDepot`).
- **Repairs and bays (5b).** A `RepairJob` takes a bay for its time: Patch +15 in 20 s for 3 % of the price, Service +50 in 60 s for 12 %, Overhaul up to 95 in 180 s for 40 % (the shares are higher than the first plan's 2, 6 and 25 % because the balance asked for it, see below). Bays are 1, 2 and 3 by level; the rest wait in the order of the jobs. The price is paid when the repair is done (ledger `Maintenance`, and on the line's books). Patch is done by any depot, Service from a Workshop, Overhaul only in a Complex (`CanRepair`). `UpgradeDepotCommand` raises a road or rail depot in place for the difference in price (a road depot gets its crews too); a rail depot has a level now (`AddRailDepotCommand` takes one; `rail.depotWorkshopPrice` and `depotComplexPrice`). A job whose depot closes is dropped and the vehicle comes out as it went in; selling a vehicle or a train, deleting its line, or parking it ends its job or trip.
- **Signals (5c).** A rail signal has a Condition that falls 0.3 with each train let through. A train that comes up to a signal draws its failure once per approach (the same curve, one passage counting as 500 m); a failed signal shows red to everybody, and trains wait at it (`BlockedBy` is -2), until a crew from the nearest depot (road or rail, by the crew speed and detour) has repaired it. A failure that takes longer than 600 s (no depot near) is negligence and fined. With the company policy on, a crew also services a signal under the floor or due by days (checked at each passage and at the turn of every day).
- **Consequences (5c).** A company vehicle wrecked in a collision pays compensation to a share of the passengers aboard (30 % at 900 each; the injured are a number, `Company.Injured`); so does a train that hits a vehicle at a crossing. The town the incident happens in (inside its blocks plus 60 m) loses 2 satisfaction once per incident, and 3 more if a company incident has blocked a road for over an hour. There is no insurance. `LedgerEntry` has `Maintenance`, `Compensation` and `Fines` now.
- **Road Condition (5f).** Every road has a Condition that falls with the vehicles that drive its length (4000 passes for a point; ambient cars count). Above 80 it does nothing; below it drivers keep less of the speed limit (down to 70 % at 0) and the breakdown risk of company vehicles rises (up to 3 times). A road that replaces another (a junction in its middle, a node moved) takes over the Condition. `ResurfaceRoadCommand` restores a road for 60 a metre; it is closed while the crew works (2 m/s of road), with debris in every lane that has room, like a wreck. The Condition of roads enters the state hash only when the company has lines, so road-only runs keep their golden hashes.
- **The garage.** At the turn of a day a vehicle or train waiting in a depot gets the company policy: one that is due goes into a bay of its depot when the depot can do the repair (a vehicle towed in after a breakdown is mended this way); one the depot cannot repair waits for the player. The policy set on a vehicle itself is lost when it is parked (it is a garage entry then).
- **Event feed.** `Simulation.Events` keeps the last 300 things the player should see (breakdowns, crashes with their injured and compensation, signal failures, a repair no depot can do, an unhappy town) with a place for the camera; it is not in the state hash. The HUD shows them top left for 45 to 90 s, a click moves the camera, serious ones also show as a message.
- **Hashes.** Condition, the time of the last repair, the patched mark, the policies, the frequency, the repair jobs and the signals' Condition are in the state hash; the rail golden was `0xFC2176B37CC8EA48` and the new maintenance scenario's `0xFE19620F8B25AE46` (both superseded, current values in 5.30); the road golden hashes did not move.
- **Balance.** `MaintenanceBalanceProbe` (env `MAINT_PROBE`, `DAYS`, `KINDS`, `SEEDS`, `MPP`, `SERVICE`) printed what a line earns, how far a bus drives (about 8 km a day) and what the default policy costs. The plan's first numbers (12 km a point, 6 % for a Service) gave under 1 % of revenue; with 1500 m a point and 12 % the policy costs 7 to 14 % of a four-bus line's income on four maps (mean 10 %), and a fleet with the policy off breaks down within a month and the line stops paying (`MaintenanceEconomyTests`, `MaintenanceNeglectTests`). The risk at the 40 floor is small (0.0125 per km at the first numbers, 0.00125 now) so a policy that works is almost never broken down. A truck line is poorer per vehicle than a passenger line (a sawmill's input fills in a few days), so maintenance is a larger share of its income; that is the line's economics, not a different number.
- **Tests.** 514 (69 more). `MaintenanceWorlds` builds the small worlds and a `Calm` tuning with the plan's first slow numbers so that the unit tests do not move when the balance does; the long fleet tests that are not about maintenance (`FleetTests`, `IndustryTests`) run with the frequency Off, because a Simple depot cannot Service and their buses would otherwise wear out in a dozen days.

### 5.27 Budget and standing (C4, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`campaign`; `debtLimit` and `bankruptcyDays` are gone from `economy`), `CampaignTuning.cs`, `Simulation.Campaign.cs`, `Company.cs` (`Standing`, `LowStandingMonths`, `Dismissed`, `Finished`, `Grade`, `Score`, `DeliveredValue`, ledger heading `Allocation`), `Simulation.Economy.cs` (the month's turn), `Simulation.Gateways.cs` (`NoteExport`), `SimEventKind.Standing`; the game layer is `SimHost.GetCompany`, `hud.gd` (top bar, Company window, end panel) and `main.gd` (pauses at the end). Tests in `CampaignTests.cs`.

- **The turn of a month** (`CloseMonthForCampaign`, from `CloseDay` on the last day of a month) does, in this order: the standing moves; a standing under `dismissBelow` (20) for `dismissMonths` (6) months in a row dismisses the committee; the last of the 96 months brings the grade; otherwise the allocation of the next month is paid (`LedgerEntry.Allocation`). `LastMonth` keeps what happened for the Company window and `StandingHistory` the standing of every month.
- **Allocation.** `phases[next month].allocation` times `allocationBase + allocationPerStanding * standing` (0.5 at a standing of 0, 1.0 at 50, 1.5 at 100). Phases start at month 0, 20, 48 and 84: Rearmament (Jan 1938 to Aug 1939), Mobilisation (Sep 1939 to Dec 1941), Total war (1942 to 1944) and The end (1945), at 45 000, 60 000, 80 000 and 50 000 a month at a standing of 50. A vehicle's upkeep is about 2 400 a month, so the first allocation keeps some twenty vehicles running.
- **Standing** starts at 60 and moves each month by three terms, then stays in 0 to 100. Deliveries: the value delivered to the gateways that month (units times the cargo's pay per unit and km, so a truck counts for more than a log) minus what the phase expects (1 500, 4 000, 8 000, 6 000), over `exportScale` (4 000) points, within plus or minus 3. Deficit: minus 2 when the money is below zero at the turn. Fines: minus one point per 2 000 of fines that month, at most 5. A committee that delivers nothing therefore slides by 0.4, 1 and 2 points a month and is dismissed around the 55th month, in 1942. The orders of C5 will add their own bonuses and penalties; the delivery term can then be turned down in the tuning.
- **Dismissal and the end.** Both set a flag on `Company` and stop the allocation and the standing; the simulation keeps running and the game layer pauses it and shows the end panel. Dismissal gives no grade. At the end the score is 0.5 times the standing, plus 0.3 times the production (value delivered over `productionTarget`, 600 000, at most 100), plus 0.2 times the average satisfaction of the towns; the grade is the first of "Distinguished service" (75 or more), "Creditable service" (55), "Adequate service" (35) and "Unsatisfactory service".
- **The advance.** The loan keeps its mechanics and code names (`TakeLoanCommand`, `Company.Loan`, interest per day, limit 1 000 000); the windows and messages call it the ministry's advance. The country (Veldmar) and the region (Marren Valley) are in `campaign` and shown in the Company window.
- **HUD.** The top bar shows the money and the standing (red below 30 or when the money is below zero) and the date. The Company window has the country, the phase and the month, the books, the standing with its allocation factor and the months left to dismissal, the next allocation, what was delivered this month against what the phase expects, and last month's three terms. The end panel (dismissal or grade, with the score, the standing, the value delivered and the satisfaction) has a button to the main menu. Run in a headless Godot with made-up data; the 3D game was not played.
- **Hashes.** Standing, the low-standing months, the end flags, the grade, the month's delivered value and fines and the total delivered are in the state hash, in place of the bankruptcy flag, so all four goldens moved: rail `0x179999302AAEC508`, maintenance `0xAE5BB21CD9A5921F` (both superseded by 5.30), road `GoldenHashMac` `0xB355B85994C6C85A` and `GoldenHashLinux` `0xFC5DC844B33778E9`, and `AvenueGoldenHash` `0x8A86D85566559152`.
- **Not done.** Orders (C5) are the main thing that will move the standing; fines from other sources than negligence (none exist yet); a standing graph (the history is kept); the balance of the allocation against real play was not measured, only reasoned from upkeep.
- **Tests.** 571 (24 more; the bankruptcy test was replaced).

### 5.28 Orders (C5, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`orders`), `OrderTuning.cs`, `Order.cs`, `Simulation.Orders.cs`, `Company.cs` (`OrdersMet`, `OrdersLate`, `OrdersFailed`, ledger heading `Orders`), `SimCommand.cs` (`AcceptOrderCommand`, `DeclineOrderCommand`), hooks in `Simulation.Gateways.cs` (`Export`) and `Simulation.Industries.cs` (`Distribute`), `SimEventKind.Order`; the game layer is `SimHostOrders.cs`, `hud.gd` (Orders window), `main.gd`. Tests in `OrderTests.cs`.

- **Three kinds.** *Quota*: deliver N units of a cargo to one gateway, whatever its source (it counts when a vehicle unloads at a station that covers that gateway, in `Export`). *Civil*: deliver N units of a cargo to a town (counts what the buildings of that town take in `Distribute`, so only what they have room for). *State work*: when accepted the state founds the industries of the template (the military truck's plant and the three works that feed it) at a place drawn by the seed, and wants N trucks delivered to any gateway. A state work is offered once per game, in Mobilisation (months 20 to 47), with 33 to 42 days to deliver 16 to 24 trucks.
- **Offers.** `StepOrders` runs every tick, only on maps with gateways (the orders draw from their own random stream, `seed ^ OrderSeedMix`, so other maps keep their hashes). The first order is offered on the first step: the tutorial (60 wood, falling back to grain or stone, to the first gateway, 15 days, a reward of 6 000 and 4 points). Then the next offer comes after a gap of `gaps[phase]` days (4 to 8 in Rearmament, 3 to 6 in Mobilisation, 2 to 5 in Total war, 3 to 6 in The end) plus a random moment of the day, so offers do not follow the beat of the month. A template is drawn by weight among those with a weight in the current phase and that fit the map (something makes the cargo, a town needs it, the state work has not been done); the same template, cargo, gateway and town as the offer before is drawn again up to `repeatRedraws` times. At most 5 offers are open and an offer lapses after 6 days (two months). Units, days and standing are drawn inside the template's ranges; the reward is units times `rewardPerUnit`.
- **Accepting.** `AcceptOrderCommand` needs a station that covers the gateway (or the town) and one that covers a producer of the cargo, and fewer than 3 active orders; `WhyNotAccept` says what is missing ("Build a road station beside the North Road Gate first"). The deadline runs from acceptance. `DeclineOrderCommand` drops an offer with no penalty. A state work only needs a station at a gateway: its works are founded on accepting (`IndustriesVersion` and `BuildingsVersion` move, so the map redraws).
- **Closing.** Progress reaching the units meets the order at once: the reward goes to the budget (`LedgerEntry.Orders`), the standing rises by the drawn points and a civil need raises its town's satisfaction by `townGain`. At the deadline an order with at least half delivered is *late*: it pays the reward in proportion and gains the standing in proportion, no penalty. Under half it *fails*: it still pays the share delivered, a fine of a quarter of the whole reward is charged (heading Fines, so it also counts toward the month's fine term), the standing falls by 1.5 times what meeting it would have given, and a town loses `townLoss` satisfaction. Everything is an event on the feed.
- **Window.** The Orders window lists the offers and the orders under way, then the last five closed; each offer has Accept (disabled with the reason shown), Decline and Go to (the gateway, the town or the first work); the button in the top bar shows how many offers are open. The Company window gets a line of totals. Run in a headless Godot with made-up data; the 3D game was not played.
- **Decisions the player has not seen.** Cargo counts whatever its source (the plan said loaded at a station covering the source): simpler and more forgiving, the producer check stays at acceptance. A quota names one gateway so road and rail gateways are asked for in turn. Rewards are first guesses, around 60 to 170 a unit for raw and processed cargo and 1 400 to 1 500 for a truck, on top of the pay for hauling. The standing from orders is added directly; the monthly delivery term of C4 is untouched and will need turning down when real play shows how much orders add.
- **Tests.** 591 (20 more). No golden moved: the test worlds of the goldens have no gateways.

### 5.29 Electricity (C6, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`power`; `demand` and `supply` on every industry type; the `powerplant` type), `Tuning.cs` (`PowerTuning`), `Simulation.Power.cs`, `Simulation.Industries.cs` (the production step), `Town.cs` (`Industry.Load`), `Company.cs` (`PowerPurchase`, ledger heading `Power`), `SimCommand.cs` (`SetPowerPurchaseCommand`), `SimEventKind.Power`; the game layer is `SimHostPower.cs`, `SimHost.GetIndustries`, `hud.gd` (the power panel at the top of the Industries window) and `main.gd`. Tests in `PowerTests.cs`.

- **Abstract capacity.** Electricity is one number for the whole region, not a cargo and not a network. Every industry type has a `demand` in megawatts (always, working or not): 0 for forest and farm, 1 for quarry, sawmill and food plant, 2 for cement works, factory and the two mines, 8 for the steel mill, 5 for the refinery, 3 to 6 for the truck's works. The supply is the *free grid*, plus what is bought, plus the power plants that are burning.
- **Free grid.** At the start the grid supplies the demand of the industries that stand, times `freeGridMargin` (1.1), so the first game runs at full power on any setting. Industries founded later (the state works of C5, 16 MW) are not in it: they need a plant or power from outside, or every industry slows.
- **Power plant.** An industry type with `supply` 20 and `output` "" (it makes no cargo, `OutputOf` is -1). It does not show as stopped for a full output.
- **Shortage.** When the demand is above the supply every industry (plants excepted) makes its output times supply over demand, and consumes its inputs in the same proportion. A change of state (a shortage begins, or ends) is an event. The supply is computed from the load of the step before, so a plant that runs dry takes effect one step later.
- **Buying.** `SetPowerPurchaseCommand(mw)` sets the megawatts bought from outside, 0 to 40, and they are charged at the turn of every month at 1 500 a megawatt (ledger `Power`) until set back to 0. No transport. The panel shows the supply, the demand, the factor, the free grid, the plants burning and two buttons to buy 5 MW more or less.
- **Hash.** `Industry.Load`, the free grid and the purchase are in the industry hash; the other goldens did not move (their worlds have no industries).
- **Decisions the player has not seen.** The numbers (demands, 20 MW and 300 coal for a plant, 1 500 a MW and month: 14 MW for the truck works costs 21 000 a month, about a third of the allocation in Mobilisation, against hauling 300 coal a month to a plant) are first guesses. Towns do not use power. A plant is part of the map at the start, not an order.

### 5.30 Workers (C7, 2026-10-21)

*Reversed by C11 (5.37): the `workers` cargo, the workers' bus, the own-staff formula, the `workers` tuning and the sending of workers described below are gone, and residents staff the industries through their trip groups. The text is kept as the record of what C7 decided and why; the jobs per industry type and the output by staffing still stand.*

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`workers`; `jobs` on every industry type; the cargo `workers`; the vehicle `workerbus`), `Tuning.cs` (`WorkersTuning`, `IndustryTypeTuning.Jobs`), `Simulation.Workers.cs`, `Simulation.Industries.cs` (production, `IndustryRoom`), `Simulation.Fleet.cs` (freight leaves workers alone), `Simulation.Towns.cs` (acceptance), `Town.cs` (`Industry.OwnStaff`); the game layer is `SimHost.GetIndustries` and `hud.gd`. Tests in `WorkerTests.cs`.

- **Jobs and staffing.** The steel mill (40 jobs), engine works (30), truck plant (60) and power plant (20) have jobs; every other industry has none and is always staffed. An industry with jobs makes its output times its *staffing*, 0 to 1 (a power plant's load too, so an unstaffed plant gives less power), on top of the power factor. Staffing is the industry's own staff plus the workers delivered over its jobs, at most 1.
- **The people's own movement** staffs part of it for free: `ownShare` (0.5) times `1 - d / ownReach` (2500 m), d being the distance to the nearest town's middle (`Industry.OwnStaff`, set when it is founded). A steel mill 1000 m from a town has 30 %; one 1500 m away 20 %. The rest has to be carried.
- **Workers are a cargo, not trip groups.** This is a departure from the plan, which said residents fill the jobs through the existing trip groups: those go from home building to a workplace *building*, with a cached mode choice per pair of buildings and the stations that cover them, and an industry is not a building, so making it one would have touched the whole trip model. Instead `workers` is a cargo (index 18) that only a vehicle of cargo `workers` carries (the freight truck and the freight wagon skip it, as they skip passengers). At the turn of every day each road station that covers homes gets workers waiting, `homeShare` (0.35) of the residents it covers, refilled by `refillPerDay`, when an industry with jobs within `reach` (3000 m) of the station is not fully staffed. The new *workers' bus* (capacity 30, the bus's price and upkeep) takes them from a station beside homes to one beside the industry; the delivery fills the industry's staff stock (room: jobs minus staff) and pays the bus line a fare of 40 a unit and km like any cargo. The workers present leave at `staffLossPerDay` (1.0, an exponential with a time constant of a day), so they must keep coming; a bus that brings 30 a day keeps a 40-job mill at about 75 % with its own 30 %. A map without towns has nobody to work: staffing 1.
- **Not done.** Trains do not carry workers (the coach carries passengers only); a wage; shifts (the plan's open point about a day of 1200 s); people leaving for the war (C9 conscription will lower the supply). The Industries window says "Staff 45% of 40 jobs (the town's own people 30%, 6 workers brought)" and turns the status line to a warning when an industry works below full staff.
- **Hash.** (C7, before C11 undid it: the cargo was taken out again and the goldens moved to the values in 5.37.) The workers sat in the industry stock arrays, already hashed; the extra cargo moved the rail golden to `0x5684A444B74001F2` and the maintenance golden to `0x3BBFFDDB1FC34F61` (checked on Linux in a container). The road goldens did not move.

### 5.33 The call-up (C9b, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`war.conscription`), `WarTuning.cs` (`ConscriptionTuning`, `WarWave`), `Simulation.Conscription.cs`, `Trips.cs` (`TripGroup.PendingCall`), `Town.cs` (`Town.CalledUp`), hooks in `Simulation.Industries.cs`, `Simulation.Workers.cs`, `Simulation.Campaign.cs` and `Simulation.Economy.cs`; the game layer is `SimHost.GetCompany` and `hud.gd` (a line in the Company window). - **Waves.** `conscription.waves` lists month indices and shares: 6 % in month 22 (November 1939), 8 % in 34, 10 % in 50 and 62, 8 % in 74. At the turn of the month before, `ConscriptionForMonth` fires every wave whose month has come: each trip group is marked to lose that share of its people.

Every industry makes its output times `1 - outputLoss * gap` (outputLoss 0.7), and, since C11, the called-up people are simply missing from the trip groups that fill the big industries' jobs, so their staffing falls with them (5.37). After the first three waves the gap is about 20 % and industry makes about 86 % of its output.

- **Hash.** The call-up state (next wave, gap, total, the people of every group, and since C15c the capacity factor of every town) is in the state hash once a wave has fired, so no golden moved.
- **A note on tests.** With a day of 30 s the walks of a trip group last longer than the day and the groups are always on the road, so the call-up tests use a day of 300 s.

### 5.36 Access points (C10, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`access`), `Tuning.cs` (`AccessTuning`), `Simulation.Access.cs` (the rule, `JoinRoad`, `AccessNodeOf`, the build of a road with its spurs), `Town.cs` (`Industry.AccessX/AccessZ`, `Gateway.AccessX/AccessZ`), small hooks in `Simulation.Commands.cs` (`BuildRoadCommand` goes through `BuildRoadWithAccess`), `Simulation.Industries.cs`, `Simulation.Orders.cs` (a state work founded later gets its point) and `Simulation.Gateways.cs`; the game layer is `SimHost.cs` (`RoadJoin`, `EstimateCurveCost`) and `build_tool.gd`. Tests in `AccessPointTests.cs`; `Worlds.TruckScenario` builds its road between the access points.

- **The point.** An industry, and a road gateway, has an access point on the edge of its square (half-side `size`, turned by its angle) that faces the nearest town, `access.edgeMargin` (3 m) outside the edge; with no town on the map it is straight ahead of the industry's heading. It is worked out once, when the industry or gateway is founded, from the plan, so it is not state and not in the hash. A rail gateway has a point too but no road is joined to it (it is a track matter, and nothing carries workers or road cargo there).
- **The node.** The point becomes a node of the road network when a road reaches it, and not before: the generator places industries without any road, so a map starts with no extra nodes and nothing moves (no golden hash changed). `Simulation.AccessNodeOf(industry | gateway | AccessRef)` reads it: the node within half a metre of the point, or -1 while no road has reached it. Nothing is stored apart from the network, so a node moved away from the point is simply no longer the access node, and the next road built near the point is drawn to it again. C11 (5.37) reads the node this way for the people who drive to work.
- **The rule** (`JoinRoad`, applied by `BuildRoadCommand` itself and used for the preview, so the simulation stays the authority and a replay builds the same):
  - An end of the road that does not stand on a node and lies within `access.snapRadius` (60 m) of an access point is moved onto it, so the road ends at the point node (created if need be, or the existing one). When both ends would go to the same point only the nearer does. An end already on a node is never moved: stations and depots stand on nodes, and a road built to a station beside an industry stays a road to the station. If the road cannot be built with an end moved (too steep, in water, too short), that end is left where it was asked.
  - A road that passes within 60 m of a point (any point it does not end at) gets a spur: a straight road of the same type and speed limit from the nearest point of the road to the access point, joined to the road by a junction. No spur is made when the road already passes within 10 m of the point (a road piece is at least 10 m, and the road is at the door; no node is made there), when the nearest point is within 10 m of an end of the road or of a junction or bridge of its plan (no junction fits), or when the spur itself cannot be built.
  - The spur is built after the road, as a build of its own with the same checks (buildings in the way are demolished and paid for, a level crossing is refused). The road is refused if it cannot be paid for together with the spurs as estimated; a spur that has become impossible or unaffordable when its turn comes is left out and the road stays.
- **The bill.** A spur is charged like any road, as construction, and its demolitions as demolition. The preview of the build tool adds the spurs to the price and to the demolished buildings (`SimHost.EstimateCurveCost` gives `spurs`, `spur_length`, `spur_money`); the spur prices are estimates against the network as it is before the road is built, so on rough ground the spur can cost a little more or less than shown.
- **The build tool.** `SimHost.RoadJoin` returns the road's ends once drawn to the points, what each end was drawn to and the spurs with their points on the ground. `build_tool.gd` draws the road to the moved end and each spur as a strip in the road's own preview style, and adds "joins the Steel Mill" and "spur to the Forest (47 m, 1,880)" to the cost line; after a click it goes on drawing from the moved end. Compiled and parsed here, the strips tried with fake data; unseen in 3D.
- **What does not change.** A station still covers an industry within `stations.coverageRadius` (150 m) of the industry's middle; a station at the point is the direct line and one farther off, inside the radius, is a hub as before. Vehicles can now drive to the door. The streets a town grows (`Simulation.Growth.cs`, `TownGenerator`) are built straight on the network and are not drawn to points.
- **Tuning.** `access.snapRadius` 60 m, `access.edgeMargin` 3 m.
- **Not done.** (Workers driving to the point were done by C11, 5.37.) A spur for a rail gateway or for a track; a marker for the point on the map before any road is there; the first click of a road (the start) is not shown moved until the road is drawn; a depot with stock (after M6).
- **Hash.** No state was added, so no golden hash moved (macOS and Linux unchanged).
- **Tests.** 687 (24 new in `AccessPointTests`): the point faces the nearest town, follows the turn of the square, falls back to the heading, exists on a map without roads with no node and an unchanged hash, lies outside the square on generated maps; an end within reach is drawn to the point, one beyond is not, one on a node is not moved, the nearer of two ends goes, an end is not moved when the road could not be built; a passing road gets a spur (of its type and speed, a junction on both sides), not beyond reach, not at the door, not within 10 m of an end, a curved road gets one too, a later road joins the existing node; the price is in the bill and agrees with the estimate, a road is refused when its spur is unaffordable; a road gateway is joined and a rail gateway is not; what a station covers does not change; the same commands give the same world and the log replays; the preview is what is built.

### 5.37 Residents as workers (C11, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (the `workers` section, the cargo `workers` and the vehicle `workerbus` are removed; `jobs` per industry type stays), `Tuning.cs` (`WorkersTuning` removed), `Simulation.Workers.cs` (staffing, rewritten), `Simulation.Trips.cs` (places, `PickPlace`, `Arrive`), `Simulation.Transit.cs` (`TransitSeconds` and `WalkSeconds` between places), `Trips.cs` (`TripGroup.Work`, `WorkIndustry`), `Town.cs` (`Industry.Staffing`, `WorkersToday`, `WorkersYesterday`; `OwnStaff` removed), `Simulation.Industries.cs`, `Simulation.Towns.cs` (acceptance), `Simulation.Fleet.cs`, `Simulation.Economy.cs` (`CloseDay`), `Simulation.Orders.cs` (a state work founded later); the game layer is `SimHost.GetIndustries` and `hud.gd`. Tests in `WorkerTests.cs`, `WarTests.cs` (`ConscriptionTests`) and `OrderTests.cs`.

- **An industry is a workplace.** `PickPlace` now looks, besides the buildings with jobs, at the industries with jobs whose middle lies within `trips.workRadius` (1800 m) of the home, weighted like a building: the jobs not yet taken times the nearness. The capacity of an industry in `_jobsTaken` is its `jobs`; when every job near is taken the people crowd in anyway, as they did for buildings. The industries come after the buildings in the candidate list, so the choice stays in a fixed order.
- **Places.** A group's `Work` is a *place*: a building id, or `Simulation.IndustryPlace` (1 << 24) plus the industry's id. Building ids never get near that number, the place is what `TripHash` hashes (`Work + 1`) and what the cached mode choice is keyed by, so nothing collides; `TripGroup.WorkIndustry` gives the industry id or -1. Homes and leisure places are always buildings. `Depart`, `ChooseMode`, `ComputeMode`, `CarSeconds`, `TransitSeconds`, `WalkSeconds`, `ModeFor` and the walk from the station all take places; `PlacePoint`, `PlaceAccessNode` and `PlaceStations` say where a place is, which road node a car goes to and which stations cover it.
- **How they come.** On foot when the industry's middle is within `walkRadius` (150 m) of the home or both reach the same node. By car to the industry's access node (C10): the node at its access point, so a car needs a road that has been drawn to the point; with none the car trip is impossible. By a line: the person walks to a station that covers the home, rides to a station that covers the industry (within `stations.coverageRadius` of its middle, like cargo) and walks from there to the industry; a line ends at that station. A group with no way (no road to the point, no car, no line) cannot go and stays at home that day, exactly as for a workplace in another town; `Complete` already treats a trip nobody arrived at as not made. A station that covers an industry with jobs now accepts passengers, so a bus can be sent to it.
- **Staffing.** `Industry.WorkersToday` counts the people of groups that reach the industry on their way out to work (`Arrive` on the `WorkOut` leg; a car that never found room or a way counts as arrived, as it always did). `CloseDay` calls `CloseDayForStaffing`: `WorkersYesterday` becomes today's count, today's is cleared and the staffing becomes `min(1, WorkersYesterday / jobs)`. An industry with jobs makes its output times that staffing until the next day closes (`Simulation.Industries.cs`, unchanged apart from where the number comes from), so a power plant's load and the power it gives follow it too. There are no shifts and people do not stay: a group goes to work in the morning window and home in the afternoon, every day. **Before the first day closes staffing is 1** (a grace day for the start of a campaign and for a state work founded in the middle of a day); a map without towns has staffing 1 always; an industry without jobs is always 1.
- **A state work founded later.** The groups already have their workplaces, so `StaffNewIndustry` gives a state work with jobs the nearest groups within `workRadius` that are at home between trips (not travelling, not already working in an industry, in order of distance and then id) until its jobs are taken; they leave the building they had. Groups made after it (a new home) choose among the workplaces with it in the list. Industries that exist at the start are in the lists from the first group.
- **The call-up** (C9b, 5.33) works on top: the called-up people are missing from the groups, so fewer reach the industry and its staffing falls with them; the output cut `WorkforceFactor` (1 - 0.7 * gap) still applies to every industry as before. The old rule that multiplied the own staff by `1 - gap` is gone with the own staff.
- **Removed.** The `workers` cargo (the cargo list is 18 entries again, passengers first), the `workerbus` vehicle (the game no longer offers it, because the vehicle list is the tuning's), the `workers` tuning section (`ownReach` 2500, `ownShare` 0.5, `staffLossPerDay` 1.0, `homeShare` 0.35, `refillPerDay` 1.0, `reach` 3000), `SendWorkers`, `SetOwnStaff`, `Industry.OwnStaff`, `StaffKeepPerTick`, the room for workers in `IndustryRoom`, the decay of the staff stock and the skip of the workers cargo in `CargoToLoadFor`. No new tuning number was added: staffing uses `trips.workRadius`, `walkRadius`, `carShare`, `peoplePerCar` and the rest of the trip tuning as they are.
- **The window.** The Industries window shows "N of J jobs filled yesterday   staffing P%" (`SimHost.GetIndustries` gives `jobs`, `staffing` and `staff`, the people who reached it on the last day that closed; `own_staff` is gone) and the status line turns to a warning "only N of J jobs were filled yesterday (give the towns a road or a line to it)" when an industry works below full staff. Compiled and parsed here, not seen in 3D.
- **Not done.** A group keeps its first workplace, so a town does not move people to an industry that is founded later except for a state work; the mix of jobs between buildings and industries is the weights of `PickPlace` and was not tuned (an industry near a big town may get less than its jobs, an unreachable one gets none; first guesses to play); no wage and no shifts; staffing is the previous day's, so a road or line built today shows in the staffing tomorrow; a station on a rail line covers an industry like a road one and the rail passengers use the same trip model.
- **Hash.** `IndustryHash` adds `Staffing`, `WorkersToday` and `WorkersYesterday` of every industry and the trip hash covers the places, so the state is hashed. The cargo list is one shorter, which moved the rail golden to `0x179999302AAEC508` and the maintenance golden to `0xAE5BB21CD9A5921F` on macOS and the same two values on Linux (checked in a linux/amd64 container). - **Tests.** 692 (the suite had 687). `WorkerTests` is rewritten (17 tests, 13 before), `ConscriptionTests` has the call-up test for staffing in place of the own-staff one, `OrderTests` has one for the state work taking groups, `TuningTests` and `ModelYearTests` lost the cargo and the model.

### 5.38 Dynamic raids (C12, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`war.bombing`, rewritten), `WarTuning.cs` (`BombingTuning`, `RaidAircraftTuning`, `RaidIndustryValue`), `Simulation.Raids.cs` (rewritten: `Flight`, `Bomb`, `Impact`, `RaidTargetKind`, `FlightPhase`, the launch, `StepFlights`, `Land` and the blast), `Station.cs`, `Simulation.Incidents.cs` (`Depot`) and `Simulation.Rail.cs` (`RailDepot`) for `ShutUntil`, hooks for shut stations and depots in `Simulation.Fleet.cs`, `Simulation.Trains.cs`, `Simulation.Industries.cs`, `Simulation.Gateways.cs`, `Simulation.Garage.cs`, `Simulation.Repairs.cs`, `Simulation.Rail.cs` and `Simulation.Incidents.cs`; the game layer is `SimHostRaids.cs`, `SimHost.cs`, `SimHostGarage.cs`, `hud.gd`, `main.gd`. Tests in `RaidTests.cs` (moved out of `WarTests.cs`).

- **A raid is a flight.** The event that used to resolve at once now launches a `Flight` (a small class in `Simulation.Flights`, like `Train`). `Simulation.Step` calls `StepFlights` right after `StepRaids`; both return at once when nothing flies. The gaps, `fromMonth` 48 and the frequency option are as in 5.35 (`Raids` now counts flights launched; `Bombs` counts bombs dropped). A launch with nothing to bomb is skipped.
- **The side.** One of eight compass directions (0 north to 7 north-west, clockwise; north is toward negative z, east toward positive x) is drawn once per game from its own stream (`seed ^ RaidSideMix`, drawn in the constructor, so it does not depend on when raids begin) and is `Simulation.RaidDirection`. The entry point of a flight is where a ray from the middle of the map, turned by up to `entryArc` (0.6 rad) either way from that direction, meets the map edge pushed out by `entryMargin` (300 m); the exit is where the straight line through the target leaves the same box.
- **Aircraft.** Between `min` and `max` of the phase (Total war 1 to 4, The end 2 to 6), drawn from the raid stream. They fly straight at `speed` 75 m/s at `altitude` 320 m (crossing the 4096 m map takes about 55 s, the diagonal 77 s). In a formation: the leader flies on the line, the others trail it by `trailSpacing` (45 m) each and sit in pairs `wingSpacing` (40 m) to either side (`Simulation.AircraftPoint`).
- **The target** is picked by weight from everything a flight could go for, in a fixed order (industries, gateways, towns, road stations, road depots, rail depots, signals): the weight is the value (industry by type: steel mill, power plant, truck plant 14, engine works 12, refinery 9, body works 6, tyre works and factory 5, coal and iron mines 4, cement works, sawmill and food plant 3, other 2; gateway 6, town 3, station 2, depot 2, signal 3) times `damagedFactor` (0.15) when it is down already or `idleFactor` (0.4) for an industry that lacks an input, times `DefenceFactor(x, z)`, a method that returns 1 until C13 weighs anti-aircraft cover into it. One roll from the raid stream picks it. A town with no standing building, a failed signal or a signal a crew is coming to are not candidates; without any signal a flight never goes for the rail.
- **The stick.** Every aircraft drops `bombsPerAircraft` (5) bombs `bombSpacing` (30 m) apart along its path, centred on the target; the whole stick is off across the path by up to `aimError` (45 m, drawn once per aircraft) and each bomb by up to `scatter` (12 m) across and `scatterAlong` (8 m) along. These come from the flight's own `SimRandom` (seeded from one draw of the raid stream at launch, its state in the hash), in a fixed order: the aim of each aircraft at launch, then two numbers per bomb at its release. A bomb is let go when its aircraft is as far before the impact point as the bomb carries it forward while it falls (`Lead` = speed times the fall time of `sqrt(2 h / 9.81)`, 8.08 s = 162 ticks), so the middle bomb of the leader lands when it is over the target, as the warning said. The bomb then flies from the release point to the impact point (`Simulation.BombPoint` gives the place and the height above the ground at any moment, with a fraction of a step for drawing) and lands `FallTicks` later. A bomb dropped before the aircraft has even reached the map (a target near the edge) is dropped at once.
- **What a bomb does** (`Land`, at its impact tick, in this order, `blastRadius` 35 m):
  - *Buildings* within the radius (`BuildingsNear`): destroyed (`Demolish`) with a chance of `buildingChance` (0.9) times (1 - distance / radius), one draw per building in id order. A building that was standing counts `casualtyShare` (35 %) of its people (a home's residents, another's jobs) as casualties (`Simulation.Casualties`, `Town.Casualties`; since C14 the share is taken from the people really inside, 5.40) and the town loses `townLossPerBuilding` (0.6) satisfaction for each.
  - *Industries* whose middle is within the radius plus half their size: down for `industryDownDays` (1) more per bomb, counted from the end of the damage they already have, but never more than `industryMaxDownDays` (8) from now; each bomb takes `inputLoss` (20 %) of the input stock and the output it holds; a power plant gives no power meanwhile (its load is zero). Told once, when it first goes down; "is working again" when the time is up.
  - *Gateways* (same reach): shut `gatewayDownDays` (2): no shipment lands, stations that cover it accept no export. Not added to by the next bomb.
  - *Stations* (`PointOf` within the radius): shut `stationDownDays` (2). A shut station accepts no cargo, nothing is unloaded or loaded (a vehicle or train that stops there stands the time of a stop with what it carries and goes on), no output of an industry or stock of a gateway is put into it. People already in its queue lose a share of themselves to a bomb that lands within the radius, and take cover at the siren (C14, 5.40).
  - *Depots*, road and rail (the node within the radius): shut `depotDownDays` (2): no repair work goes forward (jobs wait), no crew is sent to a wreck from a road depot, a vehicle or train cannot be bought into it or a parked one sold.
  - *Signal:* the nearest working signal within the radius fails (`FailSignal`, the failure of M5: trains wait until a crew has repaired it); with none in the radius nothing happens.
  - *Vehicles* whose middle (`LanePoint` of the lane position minus half the length) is within the radius, ambient or the company's, that are moving cars: become wrecks (one `Bombing` incident per bomb, `MakeWreck`) that a crew clears like any wreck (a company vehicle is scrapped). People counted as casualties: `carPeople` (2) for an ambient car, the passengers aboard for a company vehicle (an event "Bus on Line 1 was destroyed in the raid: 12 casualties"); no compensation and no fine, a bomb is nobody's fault. Trains are not hit by the blast (the signal stands for the line).
  - *The road:* with a chance of `craterChance` (0.5) the nearest road within `craterRadius` (30 m) gets a crater (a `Bombing` incident, debris on the lane nearest the impact), cleared by a crew.
  - A stick that misses the target still hits whatever lies in its blasts.
- **Events and the picture.** When a flight is launched: "Air raid warning: N aircraft from the north-east, arriving in 41 s" (Serious, with the entry point, clamped to the map edge, as its place). In the last `revealSeconds` (10) before the leader is over the target: "Air raid: the aircraft are heading for the steel mill, over it in 9 s" (Serious, at the target). When the flight has left and its last bomb has landed: "Air raid over: 15 bombs dropped on the steel mill and around it: 3 buildings destroyed, 12 casualties, 2 industries hit ..." (or "and nothing was hit"). First hits on an industry, gateway, station or depot are events, as are the reopenings; a lost company vehicle is an event. The sim exposes `Flights` (id, `AircraftPoint`, `Heading`, `Altitude`, `Phase`, `Aircraft`, `TargetRevealed`, `SecondsToTarget`, the entry and exit), `BombsFalling` and `Impacts` (the last 300 landed bombs with an id, `ImpactCount`, `ImpactsDropped`). The game layer draws the aircraft (a box body, wing and tail fin, one per aircraft, moved from the sim state each frame with the step fraction), each bomb falling from the flying height to its exact impact point on sim time, and for each new impact a flash and a dark scar the size of the blast that age with the sim's time (a pause holds them). `GetRaidWarnings` feeds a HUD banner ("Air raid warning: 3 aircraft from the north-east, arriving in 32 s   Target: steel mill"; "over the target now" while bombs fall). The Stations and Depots windows say "Bombed: shut for N more days".
- **Hash.** `RaidHash` (still gated on `_nextRaid >= 0`, so no golden moved) mixes the counters, the damage and shut times of industries, gateways, stations and depots, every flight (id, aircraft, bombs dropped and in the air, target, whether it is told, the floats of its path and aim with `SingleToUInt32Bits`, its stream) and every bomb in the air.
- **Tests.** 714 (the suite had 692): `RaidTests.cs` has 33 tests (the 11 raid tests of `WarTests.cs` are rewritten and moved): none before January 1942 or with the frequency off, the gaps and the frequency option, the shipped numbers (crossing time), the side drawn once per game and the entry within the arc, the warning text and its time, a straight path at the speed through the target to the exit, the target told in the last 10 s only, the middle bomb landing when the warning said, the number of aircraft and the formation, the stick (count, spacing, centring), the fall (time, height, the impact point), the scatter drawn in a fixed order, the target chosen by value (weights, idle, damaged, the share over 400 flights), no flight when nothing is worth bombing, each effect of a bomb by geometry (buildings and casualties, the chance falling with distance, accumulated industry damage with the cap, an industry out of reach and a miss that hits the road, the power plant, the gateway, a station, a bus at a shut station, a depot, vehicles, a bus with passengers, the signal, craters), a flight aimed true and one aimed off, the end of a flight, determinism and the hash, and a bad tuning refused in twenty ways. Tests that run into Total war still raise `War.Bombing.FromMonth`.
- **Not done.** (Casualties were a number here; C14 made them lower the population, 5.40.) No anti-aircraft (C13 multiplies its cover into `DefenceFactor` and can shoot aircraft down, `Flight.Aircraft` is then the number flying); no rebuilding and a raid can destroy the last building of a town (both done in C15a, 5.41); (people queueing at a hit station were untouched here; C14 changed it) trains are not hit by the blast; the 3D side (aircraft shapes, bombs, flash, banner) is compiled and the GDScript parsed here, and a throwaway script checked the banner text, but nobody has seen it; the numbers (a flight every 6 to 12 days with up to 4 aircraft and 5 bombs each, blast 35 m, value table) are first guesses and were not played.

### 5.39 Anti-aircraft (C13, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (the `ammunition` cargo, the `antiaircraft` industry type, `gateways.imports` with a `fromMonth` on every offer, `war.rationing.cargo`, `war.bombing.industryValues`, and the new `war.antiAircraft`), `WarTuning.cs` (`AntiAircraftTuning`), `Tuning.cs` (`ImportTuning.FromMonth`), `Simulation.Defence.cs` (new: the build command, the shots, the cover and the fire pressure), `Simulation.Raids.cs` (the downed aircraft in `Flight`, `FallingAircraft`, the changes to `StepFlights`, `ReleaseBombs` and `Crater`), `SimCommand.cs` (`BuildBatteryCommand`), `Town.cs` (`Industry.OutOfAmmo`), `Simulation.Industries.cs`, `Simulation.Gateways.cs`, `Simulation.Campaign.cs`; the game layer is `SimHostRaids.cs`, `SimHost.cs`, `build_tool.gd`, `hud.gd`. Tests in `DefenceTests.cs`.

- **A battery is an industry.** The type `antiaircraft` is the last of `industries.types` (index 16; `counts` has a 0 for it, so no map is made with one): 14 m half-side, 6 m high, no output, `inputs` of 1 unit of ammunition, `maxInput` 120, 20 jobs, no electricity demand. Because it is an industry it is hashed with the others, has an access point (C10: roads join it), a spot among the workplaces of the trip groups (C11) and a coverage by stations like any other, and is bombed by the rules of C12. `IndustryHash` also mixes its `OutOfAmmo` flag (for batteries only). `StatusOf` no longer calls an industry whose `maxOutput` is 0 "output full".
- **Ammunition** is the last of the cargo list (index 18): `payPerUnitKm` 30, `maxWaiting` 200, made by nothing (so `IsImported`) and not in `gateways.exports`. It is one more offer in `gateways.imports`: 60 units a shipment at 90 a unit, from month 20 (`ImportTuning.FromMonth`, a new key on every offer, 0 for the others; `WhyNotImport` says "The government sends that from month 20" before). It is in `war.rationing.cargo`, so Mobilisation makes it 1.25 times dearer in shipments of 85 % (51 units at 112), Total war 1.7 times and 65 %, the end 2 times and 50 %, scaled by the frequency option as for fuel. A station that covers a battery accepts it and `Distribute` fills the battery up to `maxInput`, as for any input; nothing about the trucks is new. There is no ammunition factory (after M6).
- **`BuildBatteryCommand(x, z)`.** Result: the id of the new industry, -1 when refused. `WhyNotBattery(x, z)` gives the reason, null when it can be built: the campaign is over; the month is before `war.antiAircraft.fromMonth` (20, the start of Mobilisation, September 1939: "Anti-aircraft guns are available from month 20"); the ground is not level and dry (the generator's `LevelAndDry` over a square of 1.42 times its half-side with a height range of at most `maxHeightRange` 3 m, the map edge included); another industry or gateway (any battery included) is less than `clearance` 120 m from its middle; a building is within its reach plus `buildingClearance` 30 m; a road (or, with rail, a track) is under it; or the price is not there. A refused command changes nothing. Built, it costs `price` 45 000 (the `Construction` entry of the ledger) and `upkeepPerMonth` 3 000 charged at the turn of every month (`Upkeep`), takes its access point, is given to the nearest trip groups within reach like a state work (`StaffNewIndustry`, so its staffing is 1 until the first day closes and then the share of its 20 jobs that groups reached) and an event tells it. It cannot be sold or removed.
- **News.** At the turn into month 20: "Anti-aircraft guns can now be built, 45000 each and 3000 a month. A battery needs people to man it and ammunition, which the government sends through the gateways" (Warning); at the turn into month 40 (not with the frequency option off): "Enemy raids are expected: ..." (Serious), eight months before the first flight.
- **Firing** (`Defend`, in `StepFlights` after a flight has moved and before it lets bombs go; the aircraft of each flight in index order, the batteries in id order). A battery fires at an aircraft within `range` 700 m (on the ground, the aircraft are 320 m up) if it is not damaged, somebody mans it (staffing above 0) and its stock holds the ammunition for a step. Every aircraft in range costs it `ammoPerSalvo` (1) a second of firing, taken a step at a time. The chance an aircraft is brought down in a second is `1 - product(1 - hitChance * staffing)` over the batteries that fire at it (`hitChance` 0.12: an aircraft that flies through the middle of the 1 400 m circle of one battery manned in full, 19 s at 75 m/s, is brought down nine times in ten); a step has the second's chance to the power of 0.05, and one draw is made per aircraft and step in which some battery fired and could hit, from a stream of its own (`seed ^ DefenceSeedMix`, a new constant, so no stream of before shifts). The shot-down aircraft: `Flight.IsDown`, `ShotDown`; its remaining bombs are not released (`BombsLost`; `BombsPlanned` falls by them, so the flight ends with what it dropped, and a flight whose aircraft are all down ends when its last bomb has landed); an event "Anti-aircraft shot down an aircraft" (Warning, at the place it was hit); a `FallingAircraft` that falls for as long as a bomb takes from the flying height (8.1 s), carrying on `fallCarry` 0.3 of the way it would have flown, and on landing an `Impact` (a flash and a scar in the picture) and, with `fallCraterChance` 1, a crater in the road within `craterRadius` of the place (`Crater` now takes the stream and the chance). The end-of-flight event counts "N aircraft shot down". A battery that finds its stock empty with an aircraft in range (staffed and working) says "Anti-aircraft battery out of ammunition" (Warning, at the battery), once until it has fired again.
- **Under fire** (`FirePressure(x, z)`): the staffing of the batteries that can fire at a point, added up (a manned battery with ammunition in range is 1). An aircraft that is going to let go of bombs reads the pressure at its place: the scatter across and along of its bombs is multiplied by `min(scatterCap 3, 1 + scatterGrowth 1.5 * pressure)` and the stick is let go `earlyRelease` 150 m times `min(1, pressure)` early and falls that far short of the target (the impact point moves back by the same distance). The draws are the same two per bomb, so with no pressure (no battery, none firing) every bomb falls exactly where it did in C12.
- **The enemy weighs the target** (`DefenceFactor(x, z)`, which C12 left at 1): the cover is the staffing of the batteries that are not damaged and reach the place (within `range`), over `coverSaturation` 2, at most 1, and the weight of the target is multiplied by `1 - (1 - strongCover 0.5) * cover`: half at two batteries in full staffing, three quarters at one. The enemy cannot see the stock of ammunition (an empty battery covers as much), and a battery is never "idle" to it. A battery is itself a target of value 8 (`industryValues`), covered by itself. Defence therefore moves flights toward the places not covered, as the tests count over hundreds of launches.
- **Hash.** `RaidHash` (still gated on `_nextRaid >= 0`) adds, only once a shot has been drawn: the number of draws and of aircraft shot down, the falling aircraft and the state of the stream, and for every flight with an aircraft down its mask and `BombsLost`. The battery's stock and staffing and the damage are in the existing industry and raid hashes. The cargo list is one longer and the industry list one longer, which moves the hashes that include industries: `MaintenanceGolden` 0xAE5BB21CD9A5921F to 0x3BBFFDDB1FC34F61 and `RailGolden` 0x179999302AAEC508 to 0x5684A444B74001F2, the same on macOS and in the Linux container (checked in a `mcr.microsoft.com/dotnet/sdk:8.0` container, linux/amd64: all 751 pass); the road golden hashes (`DeterminismTests`) did not move because those worlds have no industries.
- **The game layer.** The `battery` tool is in a Defence category of the toolbar; while it is chosen a green or red marker follows the mouse, with the circle of the range (700 m) drawn on the ground, and the message line says what it costs, the upkeep and what it needs, or why it cannot be built, or that the guns are available "from month 20". The click is not snapped to a junction. - **Decisions to know about.** Upkeep is charged by the month, as the brief said, not by the day as for stations. ### 5.40 Civilians (C14, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`war.civilians`), `WarTuning.cs` (`CiviliansTuning`), `Simulation.Cover.cs` (new: the siren, the shelters, the all-clear and the casualties), `Simulation.Trips.cs` (`TripEvent` carries the walk, `ScheduleWalk`, the hold at `Depart`, the hash), `Simulation.Transit.cs` (the walk from a station goes through `ScheduleWalk`), `Simulation.Raids.cs` (`Flight.FirstImpactTick`, the siren at the launch, the all-clear when the last flight is gone, `BlastBuildings`, `BlastVehicles`, `Land`); the game layer is `SimHost.cs` (`GetTowns`), `SimHostRaids.cs` (`GetRaidWarnings`) and `hud.gd`. Tests in `CoverTests.cs` (and `RaidTests.cs`, where the test of the casualties of a building is rewritten).

- **The siren.** When a flight is launched (`Raid`) the warning event is followed by `SoundSirens`. It sounds on the whole map: people do not know where the bombs will fall (the target is told only in the last ten seconds), so everybody who is outdoors runs, wherever the flight is going. The time they have is the flight's lead time: `Flight.FirstImpactTick` is the earliest tick a bomb of the flight can land, the tick of the launch plus the time the leader needs to fly to a half stick (60 m) short of the target, at least one fall (8.1 s), less the early release of 150 m when a battery exists, less one tick for the step the flight has already flown. The people need `civilians.reactionSeconds` (5 s) to start, so the seconds they have to reach cover are the lead time less that, at `trips.walkSpeed` over `walkDetour` (2.5 m/s and 1.3: 1.9 m of straight line per second). With bombs already falling nobody can reach anything.
- **Who runs, who stays.** *People on foot* (the groups that walk, or walk to a station, or walk from one to their destination) and *people waiting in a station queue* take cover. *People in cars and buses* keep driving. *People already in a building* (a group between trips is at home, at work or at leisure, as its next trip says) stay in it. A walk that ends in a standing building before the bombs can come is finished, not interrupted (the people are indoors at the end of it); a walk to a station is not (the queue is out of doors), and neither is one that would still be going on when the first bomb could land.
- **Where.** The nearest standing building (`BuildingState.Active`, any type, the lowest id on a tie) within the reach worked out above of where the person is. A walker is on the straight line from where the walk began to where it ends, by the share of the walk done (`TripEvent` now carries the start tick and the two points, so nothing has to be worked out from the group); a queue is at its station. People with no building in reach walk on in the open and are exposed; the feed says how many ("The sirens sound: 84 people on foot or waiting at stations are taking cover, 6 cannot reach a building in time", at the place the flight enters).
- **State.** A group can be in several states at once (some of its people walking, some in a queue, some on a bus), so the cover is kept in lots, not per group. A `Shelter` is one lot: the people, the building, the tick they went in, and either the walk to resume (the event and the ticks it still had to go) or the station and the destination of a queue. A walk in cover is taken out of the queue of trip events (the queue is rebuilt in the order it had) and the group stays `Travelling` with its `Pending` unchanged; the people of a station queue are taken out of `Station.Queue` (the count on the station is refreshed). `Simulation.Shelters`, `ShelteredOf(group)` and `PeopleSheltered` report them; `SirenOn` is true while a flight is in the air.
- **The all-clear** is when the last flight in the air is over (its aircraft left or shot down and its last bomb landed; `EndFlight`). Everybody in cover comes out: a walk is put back in the queue of events to end `Remaining` ticks after the all-clear, so it is late by exactly the time spent in cover (a test checks it to the tick against a world with no flight); a station queue rejoins at once with its time of waiting restarted ("All clear: 120 people come out of cover"). Two flights overlapping keep the siren on until the last is over. A group that is to leave on foot or for a station while a flight is in the air stays in and looks again every `civilians.recheckSeconds` (3 s); one that goes by car leaves.
- **Casualties lower the groups.** `Kill` takes the dead from `TripGroup.People`, frees the jobs they held in `_jobsTaken`, adds them to `Simulation.Casualties`, `Flight.Casualties` and `Town.Casualties` of the town the group lives in; a group left with nobody ends (`Retire`), and one whose trip is left with nobody to wait for completes it (`FinishHurt`, at the end of each bomb). The rules for who dies:
  - *A destroyed building* (the old `casualtyShare`, 35 %, now of the people really inside; `KillInside`): the groups whose next trip puts them there (at home when the next trip is out, at the work or leisure place when it is back), the people of a group about to set out by car from it and the people who took cover in it. Each lot loses `floor(n * share + u)`, where `u` is drawn from the group's own seed, the bomb and the lot without using any random stream, so a group of ten loses 3 or 4. A shop with nobody in it at night costs nobody; a home at night costs its residents. The survivors of a lot in cover stay in cover until the all-clear.
  - *Outdoors* (`BlastOutdoors`): walkers whose position is within the blast radius when a bomb lands lose the same share of their lot, and so does every lot of a station queue when the station is within the radius. People in cover are not outdoors: cover outside the blast loses nothing, and cover inside a destroyed building loses the share as above.
  - *Vehicles*: a wrecked trip car takes `carPeople` (2) of its people from the group (the rest of the five in it arrive when the wreck is cleared, as before); a wrecked bus takes all its riders from their groups (the number is as before, the event says so); other ambient cars, and a bus on a map without queues, are still only counted, at the town under them.
- **The homeless.** The groups of a home that is destroyed retire at the turn of the next day as before. The people of such a group who were not in the home when it fell (at work, at leisure, on the road) are not counted as casualties: they are homeless and leave the Region, and `Retire` frees the jobs they held. A group on a trip when its home falls finishes the trip first.
- **Towns.** `Town.Casualties` and `Simulation.Casualties` are counted from the groups now. `StatsOf(...).Population` is still what the standing buildings have room for (the growth rules and their tests read it); `Simulation.ResidentsByTown()` gives the people the trip groups actually have. The Towns window shows the residents as "N people", "The homes have room for M" when they are fewer, and "K killed in raids"; the banner of an air raid warning adds "84 people are taking cover" while anybody is.
- **Tuning** (`war.civilians`): `reactionSeconds` 5, `recheckSeconds` 3. Everything else is already in `trips` (walking speed and detour) and `war.bombing` (`casualtyShare`, `carPeople`, `blastRadius`).
- **Hash.** The people of every trip group are in `TripHash` once a flight has been launched or a casualty counted (before that nothing changes, so no golden moved: macOS and Linux unchanged, and all 772 tests pass in a `mcr.microsoft.com/dotnet/sdk:8.0` linux/amd64 container), and the shelters when there are any (group, people, building, since, ticks left, whether queued, the stations). The flight's `FirstImpactTick` and the positions in a trip event are worked out from hashed state and are not hashed. No random stream was added or shifted.
- **Tests.** 772 (the suite had 751; the casualties test of `RaidTests` was rewritten to the new rule). `CoverTests` has 21 tests: the lead time of a flight and the first bomb landing then; a walker at 0.3 of its walk runs to the nearest building with time to spare and its walk is held (the lot, the building, the tick, the trip still under way); one with 20 s of lead cannot reach any (39 m away against 29 m) and the feed says so; one that would be indoors at its destination before the bombs goes on and arrives; people in a station queue take cover (the queue and the count empty) and rejoin it with the same number at the all-clear; people in cars are not sheltered and a group leaves by car with the siren on; a group due to leave on foot stays in and looks again, and leaves within a few seconds of the all-clear; the arrival of a sheltered walk is late by exactly the all-clear tick less the siren tick; the hash covers the shelters; a destroyed home takes 6 to 8 of its 20 people (each group 3 or 4 of ten), the groups shrink, the residents of the town fall while `StatsOf` keeps counting the room, the jobs are freed and the trip hash changes; a bomb on a workplace takes the share of those present and nothing from those who work there but are at home; a bomb on a walker takes 3 or 4 of ten, the survivors arrive and `Pending` follows; cover outside the blast loses nothing; cover in a destroyed building loses the share and the survivors stay in until the all-clear and then arrive; a bombed station queue is shorter by about its share and the station is shut; a bombed trip car takes two people and the rest arrive; a bombed bus takes every rider from the groups; a raid on a town with trips in progress over three days kills people for real, sends people to cover, keeps `People` and `Pending` sensible at every step, leaves nobody in cover and nobody who never gets where they were going; determinism; the people are not in the trip hash before the first siren; a bad civilians tuning is refused.
- **Decisions to know about.** The siren is on the whole map, not along the path of the flight, because the target is secret until ten seconds before the bombs; the cost is that every walker on the map is held for the time of a flight (a minute or so of a 1200 s day, and a flight comes every 6 to 12 days). People who cannot reach cover are not given a second chance during the raid. A queue lot in cover rejoins with its waiting time restarted (it loses its place in the line). The walk resumed from cover goes on over the time it still had to go, not over the distance, so a person who ran to a shelter and back is not delayed beyond the time inside. Shops and offices are empty at night and cost nobody their life then, so the number of casualties of a raid is very different at noon and at night (the old rule killed 35 % of the jobs of a shop at any hour). Cars and buses: the number of dead in them is what it was (2 per car, every rider); only the books changed.
- **Not done.** People in cars are not stopped by the siren; people in a building that is not destroyed are safe whatever its size (no better or worse shelters, no fire after a hit, nobody hurt by the building next door); industries do not hurt their workers when bombed; a walker who is not in the open at the siren but would pass a blast later has no warning of the place; the 3D side shows nothing new for people (they are not drawn), only the text of the banner and of the Towns window, which were checked with a throwaway script and fake data and not seen in the game; the numbers (5 s of reaction, 3 s of recheck) are first guesses and were not played.

### 5.41 Rebuilding (C15a, 2026-10-21)

Files: `Simulation.Raids.cs` (`IsLastOfItsTown`, `BlastBuildings`, `Flight.Spared`), `Simulation.Growth.cs` (`CanBuildOn` is now internal, for the tests); no tuning, no command, nothing in the game layer. Tests in `RebuildingTests.cs`; `GrowthTests` made its helpers (`Quick`, `RunMonths`, `Fill`, `BlockTown`) internal so the new tests can use them.

- **What was already true.** A building a bomb destroys goes through the same `Demolish` as one the town pulls down for a road or after abandonment: it leaves the cell index (so it no longer crowds a lot) and frees its lot (`Lot.Building` is -1 again). Growth (5.14) already builds on any free lot with a road, so nothing in it had to change: **there is no rebuild command and the state pays nothing**. A town regrows only as it always grew: at the turn of each month, when satisfaction is at least 50 and connectivity at least 0.6, each standing and active building whose construction stock is full spends it, goes up a size or, at the top size, founds a new smallest building on the free lot nearest to it, and only when no lot is free a new block of streets is built. The lots a raid emptied are free lots, so they are used before any new block, and they are used in the order of nearness to the building that pays. The new building gets the kind its ring draws (not the kind that was lost), is Active when a road reaches it (the access rules of 5.11 and `CanBuildOn` still apply: a lot whose street is gone is not built on), and a home gets its people as new trip groups from the next day.
- **Slow on purpose.** Without construction stock nothing happens, however long it takes (a test runs six months and nothing is founded); satisfaction, which a raid lowers by `townLossPerBuilding` (0.6) for every building it destroys, must be 50 or more (it recovers by 1 a month, `recoveryPerMonth`); a survivor that is not yet at the top size spends its stock on going up a size before it founds anything, as it did before. A town whose buildings are all small therefore rebuilds in more steps than a big one.
- **The last building is spared.** A bomb that would destroy a building (the roll against `buildingChance` already won) does nothing to it when `IsLastOfItsTown` says so: no casualties, no demolition, no loss of satisfaction, and the flight counts it in `Flight.Spared` ("1 spared as the last of their town" in the end-of-raid feed line). The roll is made first, so a spared building uses the same random numbers as a destroyed one and no stream shifts. "Standing" means not `Demolished`, so Active, Inactive (no road) and Abandoned buildings all count as standing. A building is spared when no other standing building of the town would be left, **or** when it is Active and no other Active building would be left: a town grows only from its active buildings, so a raid always leaves it one to rebuild from, even when cut-off or abandoned buildings are still standing in it. The count is of the moment, building by building, so two buildings of one town in one blast cannot both fall if they are its last two. An inactive building that is the last standing one is spared as well. Only raids are bound by the rule: the town still demolishes buildings the roads cut off (5.11) and the player still demolishes buildings for roads, and that can leave a town empty, as before.
- **Accounts stay consistent.** The people of a destroyed home retire at the next turn of a day and are homeless (5.40), the casualties stay what they were, and a rebuilt home adds its people as new groups: after the retirement `ResidentsByTown` is the room of the standing homes, and after a rebuilding it is that plus the new home's room. The towns window needed no change.
- **Hash.** Nothing new is hashed (the buildings, lots and groups already are, and `Flight.Spared` is a tally for the feed like `Flight.Buildings`); no golden moved on either system (macOS run; the Linux values did not move and the CI run on Ubuntu checks them again).
- **Tests.** 785 (13 more): a destroyed building leaves a free lot that can be built on; a lot whose street is removed is not built on and whatever is founded has a road; a bombed town founds a building on a lot the raid emptied when a survivor has the stock; a station delivering materials brings the standing buildings back to eight; the town stays destroyed with no stock over six months; the state pays nothing (the money is the same with and without the stock); the homeless leave, and over twelve seeds a home that is founded brings exactly the people of its size as new groups and another kind of building brings none, with `ResidentsByTown` equal to the room at each step; casualties are unchanged by rebuilding; the last building is never destroyed over 40 seeds of 30 bombs each; the last building without a road is spared; the last active building is spared while an inactive one is not; a town reduced to one building is rebuilt from it; determinism.
- **Not done.** A priority for the lots a raid emptied over upgrading survivors (the rule is the growth of 5.14, unchanged); the kind lost is not the kind rebuilt; a rubble stage or a rebuild time; the towns window does not say how many buildings were lost (the feed says how many a raid destroyed); the enemy still counts a town with a single building as a target of full value.

### 5.42 Requisition rule (C15b, 2026-10-21)

Files: `Simulation.Requisition.cs` (`RequisitionRule`, `SetRequisitionRule`, `CanChooseRequisitionRule`, `RuleText`, the sort in `RequisitionCandidates`), `SimCommand.cs` (`SetRequisitionRuleCommand`), `Simulation.Commands.cs`, `Simulation.cs` (the hash); the game layer is `SimHost.GetCompany`, `SimHostPower.cs` (`SetRequisitionRule`), `hud.gd` (the buttons of the Company window) and `main.gd`. Tests in `RequisitionRuleTests.cs` (`RequisitionTests` in `WarTests.cs` made its helpers internal to share them). No tuning key.

- **The rules.** `RequisitionRule.FreightFirst` is the rule of C9c and the default: freight vehicles before passenger ones, then the biggest by capacity. `OldestFirst`: the model that came on sale in the earliest year first (`FromYear` of the vehicle model, of the locomotive for a train, or of the model of a parked entry; the same year the repair surcharge of old vehicles uses, 5.31), because the sim keeps no purchase date of a vehicle. `PassengersFirst`: passenger vehicles before freight, then the biggest. Whatever the rule, vehicles that are equal by it are ordered by the rest of the default (freight first among equal years, then the biggest, then road before rail before the garage, then by id), so the pick is total and the same for the same fleet. The number taken (`round(share * N)`) and the price (80 % of the price new, whatever the Condition) do not depend on the rule. Buying back is not added.
- **When it can be chosen.** `SetRequisitionRuleCommand(rule)` is accepted (result 1) only while a wave is announced and has not yet come: from the turn of the month of notice (`noticeMonths` before the wave, 1 now) until the wave fires, so `CanChooseRequisitionRule` is `NoticeMonths > 0 and MonthIndex >= wave.Month - NoticeMonths and MonthIndex < wave.Month`. Before that, after the wave, with no wave left, and for a value that is no rule, it returns 0 and changes nothing. Choosing again in the same month replaces the choice (the last counts); the command is in the log and replays.
- **One wave at a time.** The rule goes back to the default when a wave fires, so each wave starts at freight first unless the player chooses again in its month of notice. The notice in the feed names the rule then in force ("about 6 vehicles, freight first, the biggest first ... you can choose the order until then").
- **Hash.** `RequisitionHash` adds the rule times 1000003 (zero for the default, so every existing value holds), and it is mixed in once a wave has fired or the rule is not the default, so a rule chosen before the wave is in the state. No golden moved.
- **The game layer.** The Company window shows the rule in the requisition line ("next in April 1938: 20 % of the fleet, about 6 now, the oldest first") and, only while the choice is open, a row "The army takes:" with three toggle buttons (Freight first, The oldest first, Passengers first) that send `requisition_rule_set` to `main.gd`, which calls `SimHost.SetRequisitionRule` and says in the message line what was set or that it cannot be set now. `GetCompany` gives `requisition_rule`, `requisition_rule_text` and `can_choose_rule`. The panel was checked with a throwaway GDScript run with a fake company dictionary (the row is visible and the pressed button follows the rule in the notice month, a click emits the signal, and the row is gone outside it); it has not been seen in the game.
- **Tests.** 799 (14 more): the default takes the heavy and the light truck; the oldest first takes the 1930 bus and the 1932 light truck; among models of one year freight goes first; passengers first takes the large bus and the bus and pays 80 % of their price; on the lines passengers first takes five of six buses and leaves all four trucks; a train of an older locomotive goes before a newer one; the rule cannot be set before the month of notice or with no wave announced; nor after the wave has fired; the last choice in the month counts and an unknown rule is refused; the choice is for one wave and the next starts at the default; a later wave can be given its own rule; the notice names the rule; the hash differs by rule before and after the wave and is the same for the same choices; the choice replays from the command log.
- **Decisions to know about.** "Oldest" is the oldest model, not the vehicle that has served longest (there is no purchase date), so two buses of one model are told apart only by the default order; the choice is dropped after each wave rather than kept; with `noticeMonths` 0 the rule can never be chosen (there is no month of notice); the choice is not scaled or priced.
- **Not done.** A preview of which vehicles a rule would take; choosing vehicles one by one; a buy-back; the feed does not say which rule was chosen when it is chosen, only in the notice and in the window; the buttons were not played.

### 5.43 Call-up capacity (C15c, 2026-10-21)

Files: `Town.cs` (`Town.CapacityFactor`), `Simulation.Conscription.cs` (`CallUp`, `ConscriptionHash`), `Simulation.Towns.cs` (`StatsOf`, `NeedsOf`, `ScaledBy`, `Scaled`), `Simulation.Industries.cs` (`StockBuilding`, `BuildingRoom`, `GiveToBuilding`, `ConsumeNeeds`), `Simulation.Trips.cs` (`CreateTripGroups`, `PickPlace`, now internal for the tests), `Simulation.Growth.cs` (`GrowBuilding`, `FoundBuilding`); the game layer is `SimHost.GetTowns` (`capacity`) and `hud.gd` (a line in the Towns window). Tests in `CapacityTests.cs` (`ConscriptionTests` made its helpers internal to share them). No tuning key: the factor comes from the waves of 5.33.

- **The factor.** Each town has a `CapacityFactor`, 1 at the start. Every call-up wave (5.33) multiplies the factor of every town by `1 - share`, once, when it fires, and nothing ever raises it: **nobody returns before 1945**, and neither does the room they left (the workforce gap of 5.33 does close by `recoveryPerMonth`; the room of the buildings does not). The five shipped waves (6, 8, 10, 10 and 8 %) leave 64 % of the nominal capacity at the end. It is per town because the rule is per town, though every wave still takes the same share from every town.
- **Where it is applied** (one rule: the nominal figure of the tier in `towns.buildingTypes`, times the town's factor):
  - *`StatsOf`.* `Population` and `Jobs` of a town are the sums of its standing buildings' nominal people and jobs times the factor, rounded once for the town. Homes, shops and the other counts are numbers of buildings and do not change.
  - *Needs.* The monthly sustain need of every building (`towns.buildingTypes[].tiers[].sustain`) is times the factor: what `NeedsOf` says is missing, what is consumed at the turn of the month (and so the town's `UnmetShare` and its satisfaction), the room a building has for a delivery (`BuildingRoom`, `GiveToBuilding`) and the starting stock of a building founded later. The construction cost of a size is not scaled: building is paid for in full whatever the people it will hold, which is part of how the call-up hurts. What a station accepts does not change (the need is still above zero).
  - *Jobs as workplaces.* `PickPlace` weights a building by its nominal jobs times the factor of its town less the jobs already taken, and a shop as a place for leisure by the same lowered size, so after the call-up a town with fewer jobs draws fewer workers than a neighbour with more (the test sets one town's factor on its own to see it). **Industry jobs are not scaled** (`industries.types[].jobs`, the staffing of 5.37): a factory still wants its whole staff and the workers who are left fill less of it, which is the workforce gap's job.
  - *Trips.* The groups already lose their share of people at the wave (5.33), so the trips fall with them as before; what is new is that the room the buildings report now agrees with the people who are left. After a wave `ResidentsByTown` and `StatsOf(...).Population` are close (the groups draw their losses at random, the capacity is exact): the Towns window shows "The homes have room for N" only when the residents are fewer than the room, as in 5.40, and that still says something about raid casualties and homeless; a new line says "The call-up has left its buildings room for 75% of their people and jobs" once a wave has fired.
- **Growth does not refill.** A building that goes up a size brings `round(P(new) * f) - round(P(old) * f)` new people as trip groups (it was `P(new) - P(old)`), and a new building brings `round(P(0) * f)`. A town that was called up and then fed with construction stock therefore grows in buildings and sizes but its people stay at the factor of what its buildings would hold at nominal size (a test feeds every building every month for four months and checks the residents against `0.5 * nominal` at each step). Rebuilding after a raid (5.41) brings the people back at the lowered figure too, and the casualties already counted stay counted. The groups of homes that existed when a wave fired are not touched by the factor again.
- **Hash.** The factor of every town is in `ConscriptionHash`, which is mixed in only once a wave has fired, so no state before the first wave changes and no golden moved (macOS; no golden scenario reaches month 22). Before any wave every figure is multiplied by exactly 1 or taken through the old integer path, so behaviour and hashes before the first wave are identical to 5.42.
- **Tests.** 813 (14 more; `GrowthTests.ATownWithNothingDelivered_KeepsItsSize_ForYears` now runs with no call-up, because its 24 months reach the first wave): the factor starts at 1 and each wave lowers it by its share, `StatsOf` people and jobs following; it never rises, even as the gap closes; the residents are within a few of the room after a wave; the monthly need falls by the factor; a building holding the lowered need is not short, and the month leaves a town with no unmet share where the same stocks would leave half unmet without the call-up; a town with a tenth of its room draws far fewer workers than an equal one (12 or more of 16 picks go to the other); industry jobs are unchanged; a building that grows brings the lowered number of people; a new building does (over twelve seeds, homes only); a called-up town fed with stock every month does not regrow its people; a destroyed home is rebuilt at the lowered figure and the casualties do not change; the factor is in the hash and a run repeats; before a wave the numbers are those of the nominal tiers; the news says what is left of the room. Mutation check: with the growth lines left unscaled, four of these tests fail.
- **Decisions to know about.** The factor multiplies the nominal figures of a tier instead of changing the buildings (their size, look and construction cost are the same); sustain needs are scaled but construction costs and the cargo a building accepts are not; people are rounded per building when they are created and per town when reported, so a few people of difference between the two exist; the factor is one number per town, not per building, so a building destroyed and rebuilt and one that never fell have the same room; the minimum factor of the shipped waves is 64 %, so no building ever has zero people, but a tier with a handful of jobs can round to nothing in a very deep call-up (jobs as workplaces are not rounded for that reason).
- **Not done.** Per-building or per-kind capacity (only homes losing people, say); a way to raise the room again (there is none by design); the sustain stock a building already holds is not trimmed to the lower room; the conscription of the front is still not modelled; the new Towns window line was checked with a throwaway script and a fake town and not seen in the game.

### 5.44 Save and load (M6a, 2026-10-21)

Files: `StateGraph.cs` (the snapshot), `SaveGame.cs` (the file, `SaveInfo`, `SaveFormatException`), `[NotSaved]` on the command log in `Simulation.Commands.cs`; the game layer is `SimHostSaves.cs` (save in the background, load), `SaveSlots.cs` (the files for the menus), `save_menu.gd` (the list), `pause_menu.gd` and `main_menu.gd` (Save, Load, Continue), `main.gd` (autosave, F5 and F9, the screenshot, the camera and the feed, the question on leaving) and `hud.gd` (`feed_snapshot`, `restore_feed`). Tests in `SaveLoadTests.cs`; the timing in `SaveTimingProbe.cs` (gated by `SAVE_PROBE`). Plan: `docs/m6-plan.md`.

- **The snapshot is the whole object graph.** `StateGraph` writes every instance field of every object reachable from the `Simulation`, private and readonly ones included, by reflection: the road and rail networks, the terrain, towns, buildings, trip groups and their queue of events, the fleet arrays, lines, stations, depots, incidents, orders, every random stream, the war (flights in the air, bombs falling, the siren, batteries and their ammunition, the call-up and requisition indices, the requisition rule, the capacity factor), power, caches, and the `Tuning` itself, which is reached through the network. Nothing has to be listed by hand, so a field added later is saved without anyone remembering it. Shared references stay shared and cycles are fine (objects are numbered as they are met and written breadth-first, so deep chains do not overflow the stack). Arrays of plain values and of structs without references are copied as their bytes. The collections of the base library are written by their own fields, so a loaded `Dictionary<int, …>` is the same down to its free slots and later inserts go where they would have; dictionaries and sets keyed by tuples or strings (two caches of `RoadNetwork`, the deadlock keys, the order weights of the tuning) are written as their entries and filled again, because those hash codes change from one process to the next. A delegate, a pointer or a reflection object in the graph stops the save with the field's name.
- **A save of another version is refused, never misread.** The snapshot starts with a table of every type in it and its fields (name and type, no assembly versions), enum values included; on load each type is looked up and its fields compared first, and a difference gives "This save was made by another version of the game (X has changed)". `SaveGame.FormatVersion` (1) covers the file layout. No migrations. The base library's collections are part of the table, so a save made on one .NET runtime may be refused by a game on another whose internals differ; the game ships with one runtime.
- **The file.** A magic word, the format version, `SaveInfo` as JSON (name, tick, month index, year and month, money, standing, real time), the game layer's block, then the snapshot gzipped (fastest level). `ReadHeader` reads the list's info without loading. The file is written to a `.tmp` and moved over the name, so a crash midway leaves the old save. The command log is `[NotSaved]`: a loaded game's log is empty.
- **Measured** (`SaveTimingProbe`, Release, macOS M4, 8 towns of many industries, 1 208 vehicle slots, 624 groups, two months run): the snapshot takes 20 ms on the main thread, compression and writing 17 ms on another, loading 22 ms; 3.1 MiB of snapshot, 1.7 MiB of file. In the game a save of a five-town map held the main thread 16 ms. The target was 0.3 s.
- **The game layer.** `SimHost.SaveGameTo(path, name, block)` takes the snapshot and starts a task that writes the file (a second save waits for the first; leaving the game waits for it too); a failure in the background comes back as a message through `TakeSaveError`. `LoadGameFrom(path)` replaces the world as a new game does and does not tell again the news and rail warnings from before the save. `SaveSlots` lists `user://saves/*.tmsave` newest first with the info and the screenshot (`<file>.png`, 320 pixels wide, beside it), deletes, and names the files: `slot-<name>` for a named save (the same name overwrites), `auto-1` to `auto-3` (a missing one first, else the oldest) and `quick`. Loading always starts the game scene again with `Session.load_path`; a save that cannot be loaded sends the player back to the main menu with the reason.
- **What the player sees.** The pause menu's Save game page offers the month as a name (typed over, or Overwrite on a named save) and its Load game page lists every save with its screenshot, the month, money, standing and when it was saved, with Load and Delete (click twice). The main menu has Continue (the newest save that loads) and Load game. F5 saves quickly, F9 loads the quick save. An autosave is written at the start of each month (not after the end of the campaign, not in the self-tests). A manual save hides the menu and the HUD for two frames for its screenshot; the autosave and the quick save take the screen as it is. The camera and the news feed come back with a loaded game, which starts paused with "Game loaded. It is paused: pick a speed to go on." Leaving for the menu, quitting, closing the window or loading another save with progress since the last save (a tick or a command) asks first.
- **Tests.** 822 (9 more; the same on Linux in the container): an empty map, buses and ambient traffic in generated towns, trucks on a chain with incidents drawn at the High option, a train on its line, and a generated campaign map with gateways, a bus line, a battery, a call-up and a requisition in month 1 and then a flight saved with its bombs in the air and the siren on, are each saved and loaded; the loaded game's snapshot has the same bytes as the original's, and after running both on (60 to 900 s) they have the same state hash and again the same snapshot bytes. The command log of a loaded game is empty and the info and block come back; a loaded game keeps the tuning it was started with; a save of another format, cut in half, or not a save is refused with a `SaveFormatException`. No golden moved: the state hash is not changed by M6a.
- **Played** (the .NET editor, through the godot-ai bridge, not by hand): a towns game was built on, run a month (the autosave came), saved by name from the pause menu, run on, loaded from the pause menu and from Continue of the main menu (tick, money and camera as saved, paused, the feed with its one entry), the Save and Load pages looked at, F5 written and the window's close button asking. The test saves were deleted afterwards.
- **Not done.** A save made while a save is being written waits for it; there is no cloud or export; the list does not sort or filter; the screenshot of the autosave includes the HUD; a save does not keep which windows were open or the speed (decided).

### 5.46 Campaign balance (M6c, 2026-10-21)

Files: `sim/TransportMadness.Sim.Tests/CampaignBots.cs` (the bots), `CampaignBalanceTests.cs` (the targets), `.github/workflows/campaign-balance.yml` (a manual workflow; `sim-tests.yml` now filters out `Category=Balance`), `data/tuning.json` (campaign numbers), `Simulation.Junctions.cs` (a deadlock the bots found), `JunctionYieldTests.cs`.

- **The bots.** Three players drive a whole campaign of 96 months on a generated map of five towns (`WorldBuilder.Towns`, normal industries) through commands only, about a minute each in Release. *Idle* builds nothing. *Buses without maintenance* joins each town to its nearest neighbour with a road (if the road costs under 150 000), runs four buses on each and turns the company's maintenance policy off. *Competent* takes a loan of 400 000, sends every primary industry the road gateway takes (forest, farm, quarry, the two mines) there by truck, cheapest road first (each road is the cheaper of straight or bent once round a hill, as estimated by `EstimateCost`, at most 300 000 a link, 900 000 in all, keeping money for three trucks a line), builds Workshop depots (a Simple depot cannot do the default Service), roads from its industries with jobs to the nearest town and buses between towns while money is left, then every couple of minutes of the game accepts the quota orders of the cargo it already sends to that gateway and declines the rest, and once a month repays the loan when it is rich, sells vehicles towed in under Condition 60 and puts the rest back on a line, keeps three trucks on every line (four more where cargo waits) and grows by the cheapest of another raw source or a processor with one input fed and sent to the gateway. It never builds rail, never imports, never supplies a town.
- **What the shipped numbers gave** (before this slice): the idle committee was dismissed at month 56 (seeds 1, 2); buses without maintenance at months 43 and 53; the competent bot reached standing 100 within a year on every map that did not break it and ended with the best grade (score 80), because two raw lines to the gateway delivered 20 000 to 30 000 of value a month against an expectation of 1 500 to 8 000.
- **Tuning chosen** (numbers only, no rule changed): `campaign.exportScale` 4 000 to 8 000; `phases[].expectedValue` rearmament 1 500 to 16 000, mobilisation 4 000 to 22 000, total war 8 000 to 26 000, the end 6 000 to 20 000; `grade.thresholds[0]` 75 to 85. An idle committee now loses 2 points a month from the first month, and the best grade needs a score of 85, which standing 100 and full production (80 points) do not reach without the towns' satisfaction: exporting alone gives the second grade; the best asks for supplied towns too.
- **Measured with them:** idle dismissed at month 25 (seeds 1, 2, 4; the idle run draws nothing that depends on the map); buses without maintenance at month 20 (seeds 1, 2); competent on seeds 1 to 6: all six end with the second grade, scores 74.5 to 80.2, final standing 88 to 100, 2.3 to 4.7 million delivered. The targets of the plan were idle 20 to 30 (met), buses without maintenance about 28 (now 20, see below), competent second grade (met).
- **Buses without maintenance come out earlier than idle**: buses earn no standing (passengers are not exports) and their breakdowns bring negligence fines that cost up to 5 points a month, so with the steeper drift they are out at month 20, against 28 before. Making them last longer than idle would need passengers to count toward the standing, a rule change left to the developer. The test pins them between 15 and 30.
- **A deadlock the bots found and fixed** (`JunctionInteraction`): at a four-way junction 50 m from a station, trucks waiting at the edge for room in a full exit lane were still waited for by the trucks whose path crossed theirs, and the queue that would have made the room was one of those: 21 trucks stood still for good with no incident (seed 3, month 41), the exports fell to nothing and the committee was dismissed. A vehicle standing outside the junction whose exit is full is now not waited for (it is not coming); `ACarStandingAtTheEdgeWithItsExitFull_IsNotWaitedFor` fails without the line. This moved `GoldenHashMac`, `GoldenHashLinux` (checked in the Linux container) and the maintenance golden (the latter through the new standing, the same value on both).
- **Findings for the developer.** Roads over the generated hills cost 200 000 to 500 000 for 700 m of straight road (earthworks), against 1 000 000 to start and 45 000 a month: the bot's routing round hills halves that, and a player who follows the valleys does better. A Simple depot (the cheapest) cannot do the default policy's Service, so a new player's vehicles are never serviced and break down with negligence fines of up to 5 points a month; the feed says so, but it is easy to miss. The competent bot ends with millions unspent on two maps of six.
- **Tests.** 827 (3 more in the default run): the deadlock test, the short probe that a year of doing nothing costs 2 points a month (checks `expectedValue / exportScale` of rearmament against the 20-to-30 target), and the nine whole-campaign balance tests, which pass on macOS (run by hand with `BALANCE_BOTS=1 dotnet test -c Release --filter Category=Balance`).
- **Not done.** Bots that build rail, import, supply towns or defend against raids; more than six maps; a bot that is merely mediocre (one line) to set the third grade.

The options and `_loadTotal` are in the state hash only when some line has options, so the pinned hashes did not change. They apply to road vehicles; trains read the load option through `CargoToLoadFor` but have no share.
- **Competition for stock**, as decided: an industry with a station covering it and a stop of its own splits its output between both every tick; no reservation.

The preview draws each leg as the vehicles would drive it (`RouteBetween`: the quickest lane route to the second stop, blue; red when no route runs), the leg back from the last stop to the first, and the message gives the round trip's length and time and what each leg carries. The Lines window has the options for each stop of a road line (load, cargo, unload, a share box) and offers industries a road reaches as stops to add.

### 5.52 Pads, rotation and placing freely (M6g-b, 2026-10-22)

### 5.56 Coverage along the streets (M6h-3, 2026-10-22)

Sim only (`Simulation.Reach.cs`); the map view and the build preview that draw it are the next slice. Decided with the developer: the distance is walked along the streets; coverage is full to `stations.coverageRadius` (150 m) and falls in a line to 0 at the new `stations.reachRadius` (300 m); the number is a chance each Trip group draws once; a building reached by two stations takes the better. The circle stays exactly as it was (cargo, needs, the accepted cargo, connectivity, industries and gateways, rail stations): **only buildings outside it and only road stations get a reach**, so no golden hash moved. `RefreshCoverage` ends with `RefreshReach`: for each road station a Dijkstra over the roads from its node up to the reach (`WalkReach`), then each standing building outside the circle whose access node (`AccessNodeOf`, a node within `trips.carAccessRadius`) was reached gets `walk = distance to the node + straight walk to the door` and `CoverageAtWalk(walk)`; the lists are `ReachOf(station)` and `ReachingStationsOf(building)`, and are made again when the network changes (`_reachNetworkVersion`), not only when coverage is dirty. In a trip, `ChooseMode(from, to, hasCar, group)` gives the group the stations that cover each end plus the reaching ones whose draw `Unit(group seed, station * 1,000,003 + place, 53) < coverage` passes (`StationsFor`), so the same group always draws the same; a choice for a drawn set is cached in `_reachChoices` keyed by the place pair and the set; a choice with no group (`ModeFor`, the UI) knows only the circle. The walk to and from such a station is the street distance, divided by `trips.walkDetour` because the callers add the detour to a straight line (`WalkMetres`). `ReachRoads` and `BuildingsReached` give a map view and a preview what to draw for a station that stands or one that would. A building with no access node is not reached. 8 tests in `ReachTests` (873 in all). **Not done:** the map view and the preview; the Balance category was not run (more people can reach a stop, so the bots' numbers may move); what the stations window counts (homes covered) still means the circle.

- `game/terrain.gdshader` replaces the vertex-colour material of the ground: the vertex colour (grass, upland, rock, snow, sand, mud) is the tint and its alpha is how sandy the spot is (`BuildTerrainChunk`); the grain is grass or dirt (sand) on level ground and rock (triplanar) on steep ground, each sampled at two scales multiplied to hide the tiling, fading to flat colour from 500 to 2200 m. A map overlay dims it through the `tint` parameter (`DimMap`).
- Roads: `SimHost.Textured` gives the road (asphalt, 4 m a tile), the stone access road (paving), the avenue median (grass) and the track bed (gravel) a world-projected (triplanar) texture, so ribbons and boxes without UVs take it. The water has a normal map of drifting noise (`Uv1Offset` moves it each frame).
- Trees, `game/SimHostTrees.cs`: visual only, not in the sim, the save or the hash. A fixed jittered spot every 16 m (a hash of the towns' positions and the cell) with its own random number; a spot has a tree when that number is below the density (a few on open ground, patches of wood from two octaves of value noise, thick within 55 to 130 m of a Forest industry), the ground is dry (1.2 m above the water), under a ragged tree line (55 to 85 m) and not steeper than about 30 degrees, and nothing stands there: no road (11 m), track (10 m), building (20 m), industry or gateway (40 m) or depot (26 to 28 m). Worked out again at most every 3 s when the roads, tracks, ground, buildings, industries, gateways or depots changed; the first run took about 73 ms (6000 trees on the test map, which includes the baking of the Models). One MultiMesh per variant of the `tree` Model (5 variants, scale 0.65 to 1.15), hidden under a map overlay. `ModelDraw.Add` takes a `scale`.
- Seen in screenshots of `--selftest-industries` and `--selftest-models`: grass grain, trees (sparse and in patches), a lake with its sand shore, roads with asphalt. Not seen: rock and snow grain on mountains, the road texture close up on a curve, paving, the gravel bed, the water ripples moving, the dimmed map views, trees around a built-up town after growth. No frame times compared (`--bench` not run). The Forest industry's own composite looked nearly empty in one shot (the trees of its Model stand within 30 m); the trees around it come from the new scatter.
- Not done: ground blended by height with painted tiles (M8), tree species other than Kenney's, the cutaway sides still use flat colours, shadows (M7f).

### 5.67 Performance panel and sweep (2026-10-22)
- The developer saw 30 to 40 fps after M7e (60 before, Mac mini M4). Nothing had LODs: every Model is one MultiMesh with its full mesh (the trees one for the whole map, so never culled), industries are nodes of many meshes, only the name labels have a visibility range. This slice measures before changing any of that.
- `game/perf_overlay.gd` (F10, or `--perf`): FPS, frame time, worst 1 % and max, render CPU time, draw calls, objects, triangles, video, texture and buffer memory, process CPU % and resident MB (from `ps`, once a second), node and orphan node counts, counts of what is on the map, and the CPU time of each section of `SimHost._Process` (`SimHostPerf.cs`: `PerfTimings`, `PerfCounts`). Switches (`PerfSetLayer`) hide terrain, the grain of the ground, water, the ripples, the cutaway, roads and tracks, trees, town buildings, industries and gateways, vehicles, trains and names; plus vsync, MSAA, 3D scale and an overdraw or unshaded view.
- **Log:** the "Log to file" box writes `user://perf/perf-<time>.jsonl` (on macOS `~/Library/Application Support/Godot/app_userdata/transport-madness/perf/`): a `session` line (Godot, OS, CPU, GPU, driver, renderer, whether run from the editor or a debug build, window size, screen scale, refresh rate, switches, arguments), a `sample` line a second (stats, memory, process, sections, counts, camera), a `toggle` line for each switch changed (name, value, all switches) and `mark` lines from the button.
- **Sweep:** "Run the sweep" or `--perf-sweep [--perf-scale=1.2] [--towns]` measures each switch taken away alone with a baseline between steps (vsync off, 1.5 s warm-up, 3 s measured), prints the table, writes `sweep_step` and `sweep_table` lines to `user://perf/sweep-*.jsonl` and quits when started from the command line.
- **What the first sweeps on this Mac showed (1600 x 900 window at screen scale 2, debug build run from the project):** the game's own CPU work is small: all of `_Process` about 0.4 ms a frame, render CPU about 1 ms, process CPU 11 to 15 % of one core... so the cost is on the GPU or in how frames are paced. The frame time was **not a usable measure of content cost**: it slid from 5.9 ms to 7.0 ms (a plateau) and then to 16.7 ms or 30 ms during a run whatever was switched off, so macOS pacing or GPU clock changes hide what the content costs (an idle-ish GPU is clocked down until the frame fills the interval). The one clean early pair: the terrain took about 2.5 ms of 5.9 ms. Metal reports no GPU time to `viewport_get_measured_render_time_gpu` (always 0). **The sweep therefore needs to be run on the developer's machine at the real window size, and the numbers read with that in mind**; Apple's own HUD (`MTL_HUD_ENABLED=1 ./Godot ...`) shows GPU time and Xcode can capture a frame.
- Noted by the panel: about 290 orphan nodes (nodes outside the tree) on the test map; not looked into.
- **The developer's log (2026-10-22, window 4471 x 2515 pixels at screen scale 2, about 11 million pixels, debug build, vsync on, 60 Hz):** 35 fps (28 ms) with everything on. Terrain off: 56 to 60 fps; with vsync off 82 fps (12 ms), and then water off 118 fps (8.4 ms; the water is one quad over the whole map, so with the terrain gone it covers the screen and that figure is an upper bound). Terrain back on: 32 fps (31 ms). Buildings, roads and the grain switch changed nothing. **So the ground costs about 19 ms at that size, and the grain switch did nothing because it only multiplied the result after the texture reads were done.** CPU work stays at 0.4 ms a frame.
- Fix: `terrain.gdshader` reads the textures only inside branches (none beyond 1500 m, grass or dirt only on level ground, rock only where steep), without anisotropic filtering, the grain fading from 350 m; the switch now skips the reads. To be measured again by the developer.
- **Second log, after the fix (same window):** everything on 62 to 66 fps (15.5 ms) with vsync on, 84 fps (12 ms) with it off, against 35 fps before. Grain off changed nothing more (the reads were already skipped at this camera distance, 1900 m). Terrain off with water on: 85 fps; terrain on with water off: 95 fps (10.5 ms); both off: 118 fps (8.5 ms). So the ground now costs about 2 ms, the water about 1.5 ms (one quad over the whole map), and 8.5 ms is left for everything else at 11 million pixels, which only an all-off run can split from the fixed cost of the frame (post-processing and presenting such a window). The panel has "All layers off" and "All on" for that.
- **Third log (camera 1100 m, same window; "terrain" was off during most of the switching, which hides the cutaway's floor and walls behind nothing and makes the water cover the screen, so those steps over-state the cutaway (2.1 ms) and the water):** all on 16.2 ms with vsync off but 21.7 ms with it on (46 fps: at 60 Hz a frame that needs 16.2 ms misses the sync now and then); everything off 8.5 ms with vsync off (the floor of an empty frame at this size; 15.3 ms with vsync on). With the terrain and the water off: town buildings 0.9 ms, industries 0.2, roads 0.3, vehicles about 0, **trees about 0 although they are 1.1 million triangles**, names about 0. So the content other than ground and water is about 1.5 ms; the ground and the water are most of what is left above the floor (about 6 ms at 1100 m).
- Changes from it: the water is a quad for each 64 m block with water in or beside it (not one over the map; rebuilt when digging changes chunks); the grain reads one texture beyond 300 m, two within, rock only in the directions that matter, fading from 300 to 1100 m. To be measured again; keep the terrain on while switching the other layers.
- **Fourth log (same window, camera 1100 m, vsync off):** all on 16.4 to 17 ms; the water and grain changes made no visible difference at this distance (terrain grain off 0, water off 0.6 ms, terrain off about 2.4 ms net). **The 3D scale moves it the most:** 85 % 14.6 ms, 75 % 13.2, 50 % 9.8 (floor 8.5); unshaded 14.5 to 16 against 16.6 normal (lighting about 1.5 ms). The game is bound by the number of pixels it shades, not by what it draws.
- **3D resolution setting** (`Settings.render_scale`, Settings > Display): Automatic (default) draws the 3D picture at the largest size that stays within 8 million pixels (0.85 of 4471 x 2515; 1.0 for a 1920 x 1080 window at scale 2 and smaller), or a fixed 100, 85, 75, 66 or 50 %; below 100 % it is scaled up with FSR 1, the interface stays sharp. Re-applied when the window is resized; not applied on command line runs, which keep the project's own window.
- Not done: LODs for Models, culling of the tree MultiMesh, the water as one quad, a 3D scale in Settings.

### 5.68 M7f: the lit scene (2026-10-22)
- `game/lighting.gd` (made by `main.gd`, replaces the fixed sun and flat environment): a sun that follows the hour of the simulation (`GetCompany()["hour"]`, every 0.25 s): up at 6, down at 19, noon height 62 degrees, from the east through the south to the west, warm and low at dawn and dusk (colour ramp by height, never lower than 4 degrees for the shadows), no seasons yet. Below the horizon a dim blue moon and a dark sky keep the night readable. Sky and ambient colour follow the sun's height; mild exponential fog (density 0.00006, about 10 % at 1900 m); ACES tone mapping, saturation 0.92 and contrast 1.04 for the earthy, slightly desaturated look.
- **Shadows** (Settings > Display, Off / Low / High, default Low): Low is a 2048 atlas, 2 splits, 450 m from the camera; High 4096, 4 splits, 900 m. Cast by town buildings, vehicles, trains, crews, industries and the terrain (hill shadows); **not** by roads, the cutaway, water, overlays or the trees (the tree MultiMesh is one object for the whole map, so it would be drawn whole into every cascade: 1.1 million triangles). **SSAO** (Ambient occlusion, default off). Both are in `settings.cfg`.
- `--hour=<0 to 24>` holds the light at an hour (screenshots). Checked in the real .NET game with screenshots at 7, 12, 17.5 and 23 h; **the frame time was not measured** (the shadow pass is the likely cost; measure with the performance panel, F10, shadows Off against Low). A new game starts at midnight, so the first minutes of a game are night; the sim's start hour was not changed.
- Not done: seasons, street lights at night, window lights, shadows from trees, a sky drawn at low camera angles.

## 8. Tuning values

`data/tuning.json` is the only place the behaviour numbers and prices live. It is embedded in the simulation assembly (so the sim stays free of file paths and Godot) and read by `Tuning.Load()` into typed records; the network owns the `Tuning` (`RoadNetwork(terrain, tuning)`, default is the shipped file) and the simulation reads it from there. Every key is required and unknown keys are rejected, so a typo fails at load. Sections: `driving` (IDM, braking, junction range, conflict distance, crossing safety time, exit queue speed, turn speed), `traffic` (spawning, driver speed spread and ambient cars per town), `manoeuvres` (decision interval, lateral rate, stall), `laneChange`, `overtake` (every threshold and driver-trait formula), `crews` (timings, crews per depot level, the depot snap radius, how many cleared incidents are kept), `roads` (speed limit and price per type, earthworks, demolition, upgrade refund, downgrade and re-signing prices, bridge and tunnel thresholds and prices, crossing clearance, `trafficLight`), `economy` (starting money, loan limit, day length, days per month, start year and months per year, interest, sale refund, the share of upkeep a parked vehicle pays, depot prices), `campaign` (country and region, the months, starting standing, dismissal, allocation and standing formulas, the four phases, the grade), `war` (`rationing`, `conscription`, `requisition` and `bombing`), `power`, `workers`, `orders`, `stations` (price, upkeep, coverage radius, stop geometry), `towns.growth` (thresholds, time budget, lot clearance, whether to extend the grid), `trips` (group size, car share, walking, nearness, windows and jitter, cost weights, the limits on cars and waiting), `towns` (names, satisfaction rules, unmet-needs penalty, starting and maximum stock, access radius, abandonment months, `generation`: attempts, margins, spacing, grid size, `rings`, and `buildingTypes` with three tiers each: size, height, people, jobs, demolition price, passengers per day, `sustain` and `construction` lists), `industries` (generation margins, `chainReach` and deposit rules, counts for few, normal and many, and the sixteen types: size, output, rate, stock limits, inputs, jobs, demand and supply of power, colour), `gateways` (placement, import offers with prices, shipment and interval times, exports), `maintenance` (wear, hazard, repair prices and times, the old-vehicle rule, signal wear, compensation and fines, road Condition), `cargo` (19 entries: name, pay rate, delay tolerance, supply on empty maps and waiting limit per cargo type), `vehicles` (the roster: model year, cargo, capacity, length, top speed, price, upkeep, dwell times; bus, light truck, workers' bus, heavy truck, large bus) and `rail` (track prices and limits, signals, station and depot prices, `movement` (braking, resistance, how far ahead a train asks for track, when a wait is a block), `crossings` (barrier timing, minimum distances, collision halt) and the `locomotives` (steam and diesel, each with a `fromYear`) and `wagons` rosters). A change to a number is a behaviour change: the golden hash in `DeterminismTests` will move and the traffic tests must still pass (see section 7).

Still in code, on purpose: step 0.05 s (ADR 0003); a car's 4.5 m and every vehicle's 1.9 m width, and lane width 3.5 m; the 12 m click radius and 5 m step for stations and the 40 m watch distance when a vehicle leaves a depot; the lateral thresholds `HomeLaneLat` 0.5 and `OncomingSightLat` 0.3 (they define when a vehicle counts as being in the other lane); road geometry rules in `RoadNetwork` (minimum length, maximum grade, minimum radius, maximum sweep, curve comfort). Eras were dropped (ADR 0006): road models and locomotives carry a `fromYear` in the data instead.

## 9. Findings worth remembering

- Anything that lets a vehicle appear in front of traffic that cannot see it (lane change right after a junction) produces rear-end pile-ups; the fix is margins, not braking.
- A head-on standoff between an overtaker and stopped oncoming traffic is a permanent deadlock unless something forces a resolution; queues then spread across the grid.
- Strong braking hides failures: with instant full braking almost no overtake ended in a crash. Reaction delay (jerk limit) and attention are what make rare failures happen.
- The old chord approximation of junction paths ignored every pair of moves that shared an arm, including a left turn against the oncoming straight traffic. Real paths found those conflicts; the extra waiting built long crawling queues at junctions, and overtakers then tried to pass them and sideswiped oncoming cars. Fix: no overtake starts with a queue (vehicles under 2 m/s) within 100 m ahead of the target.
- A right of way that is decided pair by pair is not transitive: when each pair compares arrival at its own conflict stretch, four vehicles at a four-way junction can each wait for the next, and a 1000-car grid locked into such rings. Rank vehicles by one number that does not depend on the pair (time to the junction edge) and let them wait outside the junction.
- Centre lines that never cross are not enough to call two moves compatible: a vehicle reaches half its length ahead of its path and half its width beside it, so paths closer than 3.3 m conflict. The exact box test inside the junction (`IncidentKind.Junction`) is what showed it.
- A junction path needs a length: lanes meet at the node point, so paths start and end 8 m from it.
- Junction conflicts depend on the size of the bodies: the stretch where two paths meet and the 3.3 m "paths too close" test were derived for cars. A 10 m bus makes a crash out of cases that are clear for cars (box corners sweep further on a turn), and widening the stretch by a guessed margin changed nothing. The fix was a second set of tables, built with larger distances, for any pair with a long vehicle.
- A vehicle put on the road at a junction is hit from behind by one that is just leaving the junction onto the same lane, so a purchase waits until nothing is about to arrive there.
- Test helpers that turn days into ticks must round: `60f / 0.05f` is just under 1200 and a cast truncates it, which dropped a day of upkeep and made a replay stop one tick short.
- A boundary node cannot belong to the stretch that ends at it: if the train waiting at a signal holds the node, it blocks every other train that wants to start a stretch there, and two trains waiting nose to nose at the two ends of one block each hold the node the other needs. A stretch holds its start node, not its end node (except at the station a train is stopping at).
- Block signals cannot make a passing loop work: a block request takes the whole loop, both tracks. Passing needs path signals (only the train's path) and a train that changes its route around held track.
- Putting a level crossing close to a junction makes cars stop on the rails: they come out of the junction too fast to stop at the line, brake as hard as they can, and stand in the band. 24 m from a junction or the end of a road leaves room to stop; 14 m did not (seven cars in eight days on one map).
- A speed profile that reads "slow down for a barrier that is not down yet" makes every train slow for every crossing, since the barrier comes down only a few seconds before it is needed. The hold has to use the emergency brake: it bites only when the train would otherwise arrive before the barrier, which is exactly when it is needed.
- `Worlds.Run(sim, days)` takes days and `RailWorlds.Run(sim, seconds)` seconds; a 1200 in the wrong one runs 1200 days.
- Tests that run into Total war (from month 48) need the raids switched off or `War.Bombing.FromMonth` raised, or a raid will stop an industry or fail a signal in the middle of the test.
- Tests that buy the heavy truck, the large bus or the diesel locomotive, or that buy a locomotive at all, need `Worlds.Late`, since a model is refused before its `fromYear` (5.31).
- Trip tests need a day of at least 300 s: with a day of 30 s the walks of a trip group last longer than the day and the groups are always on the road (5.33).
- The rail, maintenance and road golden hashes move with every change to the cargo list or to an industry; re-pin them on macOS and on Linux (a container, or the CI log) (5.25, 5.30).
- Staffing is the residents' trip groups reaching an industry (5.37). A group chooses its workplace once (a building or an industry) and keeps it; it does not move to a new industry unless it is a state work founded later, and a mill nobody can reach by road, line or on foot stops after the first day.
- A message built at the turn of a phase must read the phase that is starting, not the one that ended: the rationing news showed the old factors until a full-campaign run found it (5.32).
- `unproject_position` returns viewport coordinates and synthetic input events need window coordinates; with a stretch mode the two differ, so the self-tests convert through `get_final_transform()`.

## 10. Known limitations and technical debt

Behaviour:
- Ambient traffic that is not a trip car (the few cars `SeedTraffic` puts on a map) is a random walk; drivers only steer away from blocked roads. Company vehicles and trip cars are routed.
- Only tier 0 fidelity exists (ADR 0002 tiers 1 and 2 are not built, deliberately left out of M2); 1000 vehicles are all fully simulated.
- Accident causes are overtaking, rear-ending, meeting inside a junction, level crossings, the craters of raids (`IncidentKind.Bombing`, 5.35) and, for the company's own fleet, breakdowns from low Condition (ambient cars do not break down); rail signals fail from wear or from a raid (5.24, 5.35). No weather.
- Crews are abstract (a straight line from the depot, no return trip) and, for signals and trains, only a time; the depot level sets crews, bays, what the depot repairs and whether it can be upgraded (5.24).
- Removal and moving leave the terrain as it was carved, on purpose: demolishing a road does not rebuild the hill (decision, not a gap).
- Junctions have traffic lights (5.20) and signs (5.22), and a vehicle that cannot stop in time goes on into the junction; the lane length is the centre-line length for every lane of a curve.
- Lakes are not exactly level (the water surface has one gentle slope for the whole map), and there are no tides or boats (decision, 5.8).
- An overtaker alongside its target at a lane end can reappear overlapping it.
- Tiny dense networks (a few roads, dozens of cars) still produce occasional pile-ups at junctions.

Rail (M4):
- Trains cannot collide with each other (reservation) and there is no derailment; the only incidents on the rail side are trains hitting vehicles at crossings. Signal failures, breakdowns and wear came with M5 (5.24).
- All trains are push-pull and reverse in a station wherever it is quicker; there is no run-round loop and no timetable. A train longer than the track behind its station cannot reverse until it is fully on its route (it carries on instead).
- Unsignalled track is one block per stretch between stations and depots: a second train waits for the first. Two trains on one single track turning round at its ends deadlock when they meet; passing loops need path signals and the re-routing of 5.19. Re-routing only changes the part of the route that has not been given to the train and does not look at where other trains are going, only at what they hold.
- A signal faces the track it was built on (the direction chosen by the click side); it cannot be changed except by taking it down. Block and path signals are the only kinds; there is no one-way signal that lets trains pass the other way unchecked.
- Level crossings: a crossing is refused near a junction or road end (24 m), so a track cannot go through a town block that is shorter than 48 m between junctions; the street between two crossings of one railway can hold only as many cars as it is long. Moving a road junction so that its roads would cross a track is refused rather than priced. Towns do not grow within 14 m of a track, so a railway through a town stops it on that side.
- Rail stations are only at nodes (the track is split), with a platform drawn on both sides of the node; a station that is an end of the line needs a dead-end node. Platform capacity and a train too long for its platform are not modelled (the front stops at the node; the rest hangs back along the track).
- Trains are boxes: no pantograph, no smoke, a locomotive is a taller box. Stations, signals, barriers and tunnel portals are boxes too.
- Rail balance had a first pass (5.22) and long-distance passenger rail has nothing to earn because trips stop at `workRadius`; the price of track, the locomotive roster and the pay rates are first numbers.

Maintenance (M5):
- A company vehicle wrecked in a collision is still scrapped, not towed (a breakdown is towed). Resurfacing a road with traffic on it traps vehicles behind the debris until it is done. A parked vehicle follows the company policy only (a policy set on the vehicle is lost when it is parked); rail track has no Condition. Condition of roads is in the hash only when the company has lines. Signals have no age, so an old model rule does not apply to them (5.31).

Economy and fleet (M2):
- Stations produce a fixed amount of each cargo and accept anything only on a map with neither towns nor industries (the demo grid and the older tests); otherwise coverage decides what a station serves and accepts (5.11, 5.12).
- A vehicle stops in its lane at a station built without bays: a loading bus holds up everything behind it. A station with bays (5.15) takes it out of the lane, on the right-most lane only, and a second bus beyond `bayCapacity` loads in the lane again. A bay is drawn beside the lane, as a slab of paving (5.17), not as a built pocket in the road.
- Passengers have a destination (queues per destination station, M3c); freight does not: a truck unloads everything at the first stop that accepts it, and a line of three or more stations pays by the hop. Pay uses straight-line distance.
- A wrecked company vehicle is removed with its cargo when the crew clears it (a breakdown is towed instead, 5.24); compensation for injured passengers replaces insurance. A purchase is never refused for lack of room (5.21).
- Vehicle width is 1.9 m for all; models differ in length and height.
- Lines can be edited, named and coloured, and stations renamed, closed and given bays later (5.15, 5.17); a station that was never renamed is named for the nearest town or industry. A closed station cannot be reopened (build another at its node). The Coverage overlay shows what each station covers (5.45).
- Pay rates, prices and supply had balance passes (5.15, 5.22); upkeep and the chains were set in the campaign slices (section 11 lists what was not measured), and the final balance is M6.

Towns (M3a):
- Satisfaction falls by demolition, abandonment and unmet needs and recovers on a timer; it gates growth (5.14), moves civil orders (5.28) and enters the grade (5.27).
- Streets are all straight lines on a square grid, axis-aligned to the contours; a town does not follow rivers or hills beyond staying on level dry ground. A town extends its grid only by whole blocks (see Growth below).
- Road access is "a road centre line within `accessRadius` of the building's middle", not a tie to a particular street, so a player's road that passes near an abandoned lot revives its building.
- A road that ends on a lot, or widening a street (upgrade), is not checked against buildings: lots are set back far enough that an avenue never reaches one, and a station is a node, not a footprint.
- A town's name is drawn by a `Label3D` with a guessed fixed size; the buildings are boxes.
- Generation takes about 0.15 s for five towns on a default map; it runs on the main thread when a game starts.

Industries and cargo (M3b, C2):
- Production is a fixed rate, reduced by a shortage of power (5.29), by missing staff (5.30), by the call-up (5.33) and for days by a raid (5.35): no boost for good service, no seasons, no permanent closure. Processors use their inputs by the ratios of the tuning (for instance steel: ore 1 and coal 0.5 for 1).
- A freight truck carries one cargo type per trip, the one with most waiting that the next stop accepts, and it will carry on with a load a stop does not accept. A line whose destination is saturated wastes the excess (it is lost, not paid).
- The generator keeps each processor within `chainReach` (1500 m, straight line) of a producer of each input and of a town, but only as the crow flies: the ground between may be a lake or a hill, and it does not build roads to industries. On about 1 map in 50 a processor does not fit (the map says so, 5.16). The engine, body and tyre works and the truck plant are state works, founded by an order (5.28).
- Industries are one box each; the names above them are `Label3D` with guessed sizes.

Trip groups (M3c):
- The cost of a car trip uses the empty-road time times the congestion estimate of the hour (5.15), learned from the trips that ended: it only moves people when there is something to move to (a line) and it lags a day or two behind a new jam. The rush was measured before C1 (5.16): with 5 people to a car and a cap of 200 the buses of the probe maps average 3 to 9 m/s through it; a town of 4000 to 5400 people still jams its centre at 8 to 9 and 17 to 19 o'clock, and one seed of six (721 m between its towns) still has buses at 3 m/s and a line that barely pays.
- A group is made once per home, when the world is added, and again for new people when a home grows (5.14); the groups of a demolished home are retired (5.16); the call-up lowers them (5.33).
- Public transport is direct lines only, the wait is a share of the line's real round trip over its fleet (5.17), and the fare in the cost is the passenger pay rate. Buses carry whatever queue they find, in the order people arrived; there is no capacity planning and no way to see a line's load.
- A car is taken off the road 30 m before its destination node (it is not parked or drawn at a building) and appears at the node nearest the origin; walkers and passengers are not drawn at all (pedestrians are a visual for later).
- Walking and leisure follow the 1200 s day since C1 (the windows are hours of the day): the numbers (`walkSpeed`, windows) are first guesses.
- Workplaces are drawn once; a group does not change job when a better one appears (only when its own is gone). Inter-town work trips need a road or line between the towns, otherwise the group cannot go and is counted as staying home.

Growth (M3d):
- Growth and the construction stock were measured and the pace pinned in `BalanceTests` (5.16); the costs per size are the first numbers of M3a, and deliveries are limited only by the trucks the player runs. The town window shows what happened last month and what is still missing, there is no forecast.
- A town extends its grid only by whole columns and rows on four sides, with straight streets and no regard for the terrain beyond dry ground; steep ground makes `BuildRoad` refuse, and then that side is skipped. New blocks are not checked against the player's roads (a road through the strip just gets junctions) or against other towns beyond buildings and industries. The roads are redrawn from scratch when a town builds streets, which takes a moment on a big map.
- A home never gets smaller (a town does not shrink) except by the call-up, which removes people from trip groups but not from the buildings (below); a group that is away or travelling when its home goes is retired a day later. Workplaces are not drawn again when a bigger one appears.
- Every building in id order gets its turn each month, so older buildings found new ones first; with scarce stock the centre grows before the edge.

Campaign (C1 to C12, 5.23 to 5.38):
- The balance was played by the developer and is ok for now, but it was not measured: the allocation, the rewards, fines and the grade thresholds, the pace of offers, the power numbers, how many people an industry draws against the buildings' jobs (5.37), the rationing factors, the size and months of the call-up and requisition waves, the gap and damage of raids, and the import prices are all first guesses in `data/tuning.json` (`docs/campaign-plan.md`, "Open").
- Raids have no defence the player can build (C13), casualties lower the people of the groups (C14) and a town rebuilds from the construction stock it is delivered (C15a, 5.41), but only slowly and only from a standing building. Trains are not hit by a blast. The presentation of the flights (aircraft, bombs, flash, scar and the banner) and the numbers of C12 were not played.
- The call-up lowers the trip groups, not the buildings: the population `StatsOf` reports comes from the buildings and is not lowered, nobody returns, and the call-up is not scaled by the frequency option.
- The requisition cannot be refused, the army does not choose by the player's need (a line's last vehicle goes as readily as a spare), and nothing can be bought back.
- There is no wage and no shift. A state work is offered once per game. The standing history is kept but not shown as a graph; the Orders window shows only the last five closed orders. News without a place (the call-up, requisition, rationing, standing and power) carries no place and a click on it in the feed does nothing; order news goes to the gateway, the town or the first planned works of its order (planned when it is offered).
- The road signs and sign tools (5.22) were not played.

Engineering:
- `GoldenHash` in `DeterminismTests` has one pinned value for macOS (`GoldenHashMac`) and one for Linux (`GoldenHashLinux`, the same on x64 and arm64); a system with neither (Windows) only has the two-runs-agree check. `AvenueGoldenHash` is the same on both. `MathF.Sin/Cos/Sqrt` come from the platform's maths library, so a change that moves the hash must be pinned twice (the Linux value comes from the container run or from the CI log). The rail and maintenance goldens are pinned the same way.
- A save is a snapshot (5.44), and the command log, in memory only, starts again with a loaded game: a bug can be replayed from a save and the commands after it only if those commands are kept by hand. `DemoWorld` builds its grid outside the log.
- Cleared incidents are remembered up to `crews.incidentsKept` (500) plus a batch of up to 16 (5.18); one very old incident that is still open holds back the dropping of the ones after it. Spawning a vehicle, clearing an incident and a network change still rebuild the whole per-lane ordering; only a lane change is local.
- Tuning is read through section records cached in `Simulation` fields rather than constants; step time at 1000 vehicles was measured 0.8 to 1.1 ms in `--bench` after that change (it was about 0.9 ms), so nothing is gained by flattening it yet. The Release `StepBenchmarks` of 5.22 gives 0.3 ms for the same world and runs in CI.
- `SpawnDebris` goes through `SpawnVehicle` (draws RNG, rebuilds order) although debris never drives.
- `SimHost` still does picking and drawing (snapping a click to a node or a road, the colours and shapes), which is the game layer's job; the rules it used to carry now live in the simulation (5.18). Both `SimHost.cs` (1713 lines, with its partials) and `hud.gd` (1922 lines) are large and will need splitting when the interface is reworked.
- `main.gd` carries the benchmark and self-test harness and, since the campaign, its state (the end of the game, the new-game note, the news of new models).
- Frame-rate numbers are manual; the step-time benchmark is in CI (5.22).

## 11. What comes next

### 5.69 M7g: interface icons (2026-10-22)
- **Pack:** game-icons.net (CC BY 3.0), chosen and approved by the developer on 2026-10-22 over Kenney's CC0 packs for its train, bus, signal and anti-aircraft shapes. `data/icons.json` maps a button key (a tool, a window or a group name, plus "pointer") to an icon; `tools/make_icons.py <unzipped game-icons folder>` copies the 54 SVGs into `game/assets/icons/game-icons/` and drops the black square each ships with. The pack itself stays in `../transport-madness-packs/game-icons/`. One row in `CREDITS.md` (so the Credits screen shows it).
- **Code:** `game/ui/icons.gd` (`Icons.put(button, key)`, a button with no entry stays text only); the pointer, group, tool and window buttons of the bottom bar call it. `ui_theme.gd` tints the icon with the text colour per state and sets `icon_max_width` 22 and a gap of 8 on Button, OptionButton and CheckBox. Icons sit beside the text, never in place of it; the settings and menu buttons have none.
- **Checked:** the real game with screenshots (the bottom bar shows the pointer and the ten groups with icons); `--selftest-models` still passes. **Not looked at:** the open tool and window rows (same call, but no screenshot had one open), tooltips. Some shapes are busy at 22 px (Network, Region, Rail); change them in `data/icons.json` and run the script again.

### Before M6: nothing is left (decided with the developer on 2026-10-21; C10 to C15 are built, 5.36 to 5.43)

Built in this order. Each has its tests and its hashes and is one commit and push; design decisions are asked of the developer first, and the tuning numbers are chosen by Claude and listed afterwards. The full decisions are in `docs/campaign-plan.md` ("Decided with the developer, 2026-10-21").
- ~~**C10 Access points.**~~ Built (5.36): an industry and a road gateway get an access point that becomes a node of the road network; a road built near one is joined to it by itself, with a preview and a price. Coverage by stations does not change.
- ~~**C11 Residents as workers.**~~ Built (5.37): an industry with jobs is a workplace of the trip groups; the `workers` cargo, the workers' bus and the own-staff formula are gone (this reverses C7, 5.30).
- ~~**C12 Dynamic raids.**~~ Built (5.38): bomber flights from one side of the map choose a target by value and drop a stick of bombs, each resolved by geometry; the instant raid of 5.35 is gone.
- ~~**C13 Anti-aircraft.**~~ Built (5.39): a battery the player builds, with jobs and imported ammunition, shoots flights down and spoils the aim of the rest; the enemy weighs targets by their defence.
- ~~**C14 Civilians.**~~ Built (5.40): a siren when a flight is launched, people on foot or in a queue take cover, casualties lower the people of the groups.
- ~~**C15a Rebuilding.**~~ Built (5.41): the town rebuilds through its growth; a raid never destroys the last standing building.
- ~~**C15b Requisition rule.**~~ Built (5.42): the player picks the rule of each requisition wave in its month of notice.
- ~~**C15c Call-up capacity.**~~ Built (5.43): each wave lowers for good the room of the buildings of every town for people and jobs, and growth cannot refill it.

The work before M6 is finished. M6 (save and load, overlays, balancing, the interface) was planned with the developer on 2026-10-21 in `docs/m6-plan.md`; art and audio moved to M7.
