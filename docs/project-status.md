## 4. Architecture

Follows ADR 0001: the simulation assembly never references Godot. `SimHost` (C#, in the Godot project) owns a `Simulation`, advances it in fixed steps against the frame clock and draws it. GDScript only emits intent (speed, tool, action) and calls `SimHost`'s PascalCase methods.

Stepping (`SimHost._Process`): accumulates `delta * speed`, runs whole 0.05 s steps while under a wall-clock budget (10 ms at 1x, 70 ms above), and drops the backlog when over budget, so effective speed degrades instead of the frame rate (ADR 0002). The wall clock only decides how many steps run, never what a step computes.

Rendering: roads are boxes with a centre line (avenues get a median); every vehicle is one instance of a single `MultiMesh` filled in bulk each frame; rescue crews use a second small `MultiMesh`. Vehicle positions are extrapolated by a fraction of a step so motion is smooth between the 20 Hz steps. The buffer starts at 2048 instances and doubles when needed.

## 5. Simulation model

### 5.1 Road network (`RoadNetwork`)
- Roads are straight or circular arcs (`RoadGeometry`, section 5.7). Nodes have x/z and a height; a `Road` joins two nodes and owns `ForwardLanes` and `BackwardLanes` (lane 0 is right-most).
- `TwoLane`: one lane per direction, opposing lanes linked as `Oncoming` (overtaking allowed). `Avenue2x2`: two lanes per direction, linked `Left`/`Right`, no `Oncoming`.
- Junction connections are rebuilt whenever a road is added: each incoming lane connects to the lane of every other road at the node, same lane index where possible; a dead end turns traffic around onto the opposite lane.
- `MovesConflict(inA, outA, inB, outB)`: two moves conflict if they enter the same lane (merge) or their paths cross. Each move has a path: a cubic curve from where the incoming lane enters the junction area (8 m before the node, at the lane's own offset) to where the outgoing lane leaves it, keeping each lane's heading. Two moves conflict when their paths cross (segment intersection on 12-point polylines) or run closer than 3.3 m apart (a vehicle reaches half its length ahead of its path and half its width beside it), so lane offsets, junction width and arcs count, and the left turn against oncoming straight traffic is a conflict. Results are cached per move pair (lane geometry never changes). The simulation keeps its own flat copy: each node has a small square table over its moves (an incoming lane with one of its next lanes), filled on first use and laid out again when the network changes (`SyncConflictTables`); a step with 1000 vehicles got about 7 percent faster.
- `GetConflictZone(inA, outA, inB, outB, distance)` gives, for a conflicting pair, the stretch of each path where the centres of two vehicles come within `distance` (5 m, `conflictDistance`) of each other, as distances from the start of the path; merges into the same lane stay in conflict to the end of both paths. The simulation caches it with the conflict bit, so the table entry for (a, b) holds a's stretch.
- `JunctionLength`, `JunctionTurn` and `JunctionPointAt(from, to, distance)` describe the same path (length, total heading change, pose at a distance; heading is blended between the polyline's pieces so it changes smoothly). `JunctionReach` is 8 m, or half a short lane. Vehicles drive along these paths, see 5.9.
- `BuildRoad` plans first (null means "cannot build"): snaps to nodes within 0.5 m at its ends, finds every road it ends on or crosses, splits those roads, then adds the pieces. Refused when shorter than `MinRoadLength` (10 m), when a piece duplicates an existing road, when a piece is too steep, or when it would cross within 10 m of another junction (unless it passes over or under that road, 5.8).
- `SplitRoad` retires the old road and lanes and records for each old lane its two replacements and the split distance. Vehicles migrate in `Simulation.SyncNetwork`.

### 5.2 Vehicles and the step (`Simulation`, partial class)
Data layout: parallel arrays indexed by vehicle id (lane, position, speed, traits, manoeuvre state, kind...). Position is the front bumper's distance along its lane. Vehicles are grouped per lane in ascending position (counting sort by lane, insertion sort by position, ties broken by id), rebuilt each step (a lane change re-files just that vehicle, `MoveInOrder`). Lane links (`Oncoming`, `Left`, `Right`, `Next`, road) are flat arrays refreshed when the network version changes. Ids are reused after a vehicle is removed.

Step order: `SyncNetwork`; `DecideManoeuvres`; `PrepareOvertakes` (observe oncoming traffic, advance overtakes); acceleration for every vehicle from the start-of-step state; integrate and advance lanes; `RebuildOrder`; `DetectIncidents` (collisions, debris, crews); `Tick++`.

Car-following is the Intelligent Driver Model, taking the most restrictive of: the leader in the lane (or the rearmost vehicle on the lane about to be entered), an oncoming overtaker, and junction yielding. Braking is capped at 9 m/s^2 and builds up at most 15 m/s^3, so drivers need a moment to react; that delay is what makes some situations unrecoverable.

Junctions are uncontrolled; see 5.9 for who waits for whom. Vehicles drive through them.

### 5.3 Manoeuvres
- **Lane change** (avenues): evaluated every 0.5 s per vehicle (staggered by id) when held up by a slower leader; gap checks ahead and behind in the target lane; returns to the right lane once the way is clear. Not within 30 m of either end of a lane. The sim swaps lanes instantly; `VehicleState.Shift` carries a 3.5 m offset that fades over 1.25 s so the renderer can show a smooth move.
- **Overtaking** (2-lane roads): after being stuck behind a slower leader for `1.5 + 6 * patience` seconds, a driver pulls into the oncoming lane if it believes the pass fits. Phases: `MovingOut`, `Passing`, `Returning`. Attention limits how far the driver sees, makes it underestimate oncoming speed and how long the pass takes; aggression shrinks the safety margin. It never overtakes a halted vehicle, a queue with a halted vehicle within 80 m, or a leader slower than 1 m/s, and not within 15 m of the start of a lane or when the lane is too short.
- **Giving up:** an overtaker aborts when oncoming time-to-collision is too short, when something is within 15 m, or within 30 m of a junction: back in behind its target if still behind it, straight in if already clear. Alongside the target it cannot back out. An overtaker standing still nose to nose with oncoming traffic for 4 s cuts back in regardless (a sideswipe if the target is still beside it).
- Oncoming traffic brakes for an overtaker through a virtual leader; overtakers out in the oncoming lane follow each other.

### 5.4 Incidents, depots and crews
- **Detection** by box overlap: follower against the vehicle in front (also across a lane end), and each overtaker against everything in its own and the oncoming lane (oncoming positions mapped into its frame, lateral offset `3.5 m * lateral`, vehicle width 1.9 m). A vehicle inside a junction is checked as a rotated box against the others in it (separating axis test on their poses on the junction paths; two on the same path are the following check's business). Kinds: `Breakdown`, `RearEnd`, `HeadOn`, `Sideswipe`, `Junction` (two vehicles met inside a junction).
- **Wrecks** are halted vehicles (`VehicleKind.Wreck`); a head-on with the overtaker out in the oncoming lane also spawns `Debris` in its own lane so both directions are blocked. A car that hits a wreck joins that incident. Wrecks count against the lane they physically block, and drivers prefer turns onto unblocked roads.
- **Crews**: `AddDepot(node, level)`. A new incident takes the nearest depot with a free crew: arrival after `10 s + straight distance * 1.4 / 16 m/s`; clearing takes 45 s, then wrecks are removed and the crew is free again (it does not drive back). If that response would take 300 s or more, or no depot exists, or every crew has been busy for 300 s, a distant fallback service arrives instead. A car piling into a wreck being cleared restarts the clearing timer.

### 5.5 Determinism (ADR 0003)
One seeded RNG (`SimRandom`, xoshiro256** seeded through SplitMix64, own implementation so it never depends on the runtime), vehicles processed in id order, no wall-clock or Godot values inside a step. `StateHash()` is FNV-1a over all vehicle state, incident and depot state, the network version and the RNG state. Roads built while running are picked up at the next step or spawn.

Player actions are commands (`SimCommand`: `BuildRoadCommand`, `RemoveRoadCommand`, `UpgradeRoadCommand`, `DowngradeRoadCommand`, `SetSpeedLimitCommand`, `MoveNodeCommand`, `AddDepotCommand`, `BuildStationCommand`, `CreateLineCommand`, `DeleteLineCommand`, `BuyVehicleCommand`, `SellVehicleCommand`, `TakeLoanCommand`, `RepayLoanCommand`, `SeedTrafficCommand`, `CrashSomeoneCommand`). Road, depot and station commands charge their price and are refused (result 0 or -1, nothing changes) when the company cannot pay. `Simulation.Execute` applies one between steps and appends it to `CommandLog` with the tick it ran at (refused commands too: they change nothing and are refused again on replay). `Simulation.Replay(log, untilTick)` runs a fresh simulation (same seed, same initial network) to a tick, executing the log at the recorded ticks. `DeterminismTests` builds its whole world through commands, so the log plus the seed alone reproduces the run. Anything that changes the network or the simulation without going through `Execute` is not replayable; the game's `DemoWorld` grid is the one such initial world (the demo's depots and traffic are commands).

### 5.6 Terrain and elevation (M1)
- `Terrain` is a heightmap centred on the origin (water, which has its own surface height at each point: see 5.8): 512 x 512 cells of 8 m (4 km square), divided into 16 x 16 chunks of 32 cells. Carving a road records which chunks it touched (`TakeChangedChunks`) so a renderer rebuilds only those. `Terrain.Generate(seed)` sums five octaves of integer-hashed value noise and shapes it so there are plains between hills (up to about 110 m); `Terrain.Flat()` is level. Same seed, same map (`Hash()` in tests). `HeightAt` is bilinear.
- `RoadNetwork(Terrain?)`: nodes have a height (terrain height when created on the ground, interpolated along the road when a junction splits it). A road's profile is the straight line between its node heights. `Lane.Grade` is rise over run in the direction of travel; the lane's `SpeedLimit` falls linearly from the posted value at 4 percent to half of it at 12 percent. Lane length is still the horizontal distance.
- `Plan` refuses a piece steeper than `MaxGrade` (12 percent), so `CanBuildRoad` and `BuildRoad` check it. A deep fill or cut does not refuse the road: it becomes a bridge or tunnel (5.8).
- `EstimateCost` returns length, cut and fill volumes and money: the volume between ground and road surface sampled every 4 m, cross section `h * width + 1.5 * h^2`, plus a price per metre by road type. These are real prices now: commands charge them (5.10).
- `BuildRoad` then carves the terrain (`Terrain.CarveRoad`): the carriageway becomes level with the road, blending back to the old ground over a 6 m shoulder; the flat zone is never narrower than 1.42 cells so every sample read under the road is level. This bumps `Terrain.Version`.

### 5.7 Arcs, removal and upgrade (M1)
- `RoadGeometry` is a straight line or a circular arc: start point, start heading, signed sweep (total turn, positive is an increasing heading) and arc length. `PointAt(s)` and `HeadingAt(s)` take distances along the centre line; `Slice` and `Reversed` give the parts and the other direction. Positions of vehicles on every lane of a road are distances along the road's centre line, so parallel lanes share a length; the lane's sideways offset is only used for drawing, junction paths and incident points.
- `AddRoad(..., sweep)`; `BuildRoad(..., sweep)`. Limits: radius at least 25 m (`MinRadius`), total sweep at most 2.8 rad. Curve speed limit is `sqrt(3 * radius)` (about 8.7 m/s at 25 m, 17 m/s at 100 m), combined with the grade factor. Splitting an arc splits its sweep in proportion.
- `Plan` intersects straight against straight analytically as before; any arc involved uses 3 m polylines with a bounding box test, then the same rules (ends within 0.5 m snap to nodes, no junction within 10 m of an end).
- The tool draws a curve from three clicks: start, a point on it, end (`RoadGeometry.SweepThrough`). Terrain carving follows the polyline of the arc (`Terrain.CarvePath`).
- Terrain is never restored: removing or moving a road keeps its cut and fill, as a real demolition would.
- `RemoveRoad(id)` retires the road's lanes with no replacement; the simulation removes vehicles on them (wrecks included, taking them out of their incident) and rebuilds the junctions at both ends, so a node left with one road becomes a dead end. Cost is `10 per metre` demolition; the terrain keeps its cut and fill.
- `UpgradeRoad(id, speed)` turns a two-lane road into an avenue on the same centre line; vehicles move to the matching new lane. Cost is the avenue price minus half the two-lane price, plus earthworks for the extra width. Only two-lane roads can be upgraded.
- `DowngradeRoad(id, speed)` narrows an avenue to a two-lane road on the same centre line (`DowngradeCostPerMetre`; the ground keeps its earthworks). Right-lane vehicles carry on in the new lane; left-lane vehicles are moved into it as well, and one that lands within a vehicle length of another is removed (`Lane.SplitMerges`, checked in `SyncNetwork`).
- `SetSpeedLimit(id, limit)` re-signs a road (`SpeedLimitChangeCostPerMetre`; refused when unchanged or not a positive finite number). Upgrade, downgrade and speed change share `Reshape`: retire the old road, add a new one on the same nodes and sweep, hand every old lane to the matching new one. Lanes are immutable, so a limit change is a new road, not an edit.
- `MoveNode(node, x, z)` retires every road at a node and rebuilds each one from the new place to its other end with the same total turn (`Sweep`), through `BuildRoad`: a rebuilt road joins the roads it ends on or crosses, and bridges and tunnels are worked out again for the new line. The price is demolition plus new construction (`EstimateMoveNode`, with the bridge and tunnel lengths). Refused when the node has no roads, the target is within 1 m of the old place, on another node or in water, a rebuilt road would be too short, too steep or bend too tightly (the same turn over a shorter chord is a smaller radius), two rebuilt roads would leave in the same direction (under 0.05 rad apart) or cross each other. Vehicles are carried over: every old lane gets a `LaneRemap` to the lanes of the rebuilt pieces, and a vehicle moves to the point at the same share of the road (`SyncNetwork`); where the new road is shorter, vehicles that end up on top of another are removed. A depot at the node moves with it (`MoveNodeCommand` re-points it).
- A depot whose node has lost every road is closed (`Depot.Active` false, checked in `SyncNetwork`): it sends no more crews, a crew on its way is lost and the incident falls back to the distant service, counted from that moment. `AddDepotCommand` is refused at a node with no road. The state hash only mixes in a closed depot, so runs without closures keep their hash.
- `RoadNetwork.Version` increases on every change; the simulation re-syncs when it differs, so a change that adds no lanes (removal) is noticed too.

### 5.8 Water, bridges and tunnels (M1)
- **Water.** `Terrain.WaterSurfaceAt(x, z)` is the height of the water surface: 0 at the middle of the map, falling by 0.0012 m per metre (about 5 m over the map) toward a side chosen from the seed (`WaterSlopeX`, `WaterSlopeZ`), so rivers run downhill and end in the sea. Ground below the surface is under water (`Terrain.IsWater`). `Generate(seed, water: true)` keeps the plains 2.5 m above the local surface, cuts lakes (a noise high) and rivers (a thin band around the middle of another noise field) into the lowlands only, up to 4 m deep, and cuts a sea along each edge the water runs toward (400 m wide when it runs straight at the edge, narrower the more obliquely, none when the water runs along the edge to within 17 degrees), hills or not; roughly 10 to 35 percent of a map is water depending on the seed. Lakes follow the same slope (a few tenths of a metre across a lake) rather than being exactly level. `Terrain.Create(cells, cellSize, shape, waterSlopeX, waterSlopeZ)` builds a hand-shaped map for tests (level water unless a slope is given). Decision: no tides and no boats. Tides would move the shore under roads while the sim runs, and ships are out of scope (game plan); bridges therefore do not check clearance for boats.
- **Rules.** A road must start and end on dry land (`Plan` refuses an end in water); nodes made by hand with `AddNode` are not checked.
- **Structures.** `Plan` walks every stretch between two meetings every 4 m and marks it `Bridge` where the ground is under water or the road would sit more than `maxFillDepth` (6 m) above it, and `Tunnel` where the road would be more than `maxCutDepth` (8 m) below it. Each run is widened by `structureMargin` (8 m) each way (a bridge wins over a tunnel where they meet), and any run shorter than `MinRoadLength` is absorbed by its neighbour. A new meeting (a plain node) is added wherever the kind changes, with the height of the straight profile at that point, so a long road over one gorge is ground, bridge, ground. Each piece is a `Road` with `Road.Structure`; the lanes are ordinary lanes.
- **Cost.** A structure costs `bridgeCostPerMetre` or `tunnelCostPerMetre` (times `avenueStructureFactor` for an avenue) instead of the road price and earthworks, and does not carve the ground. `RoadCost` carries `BridgeLength` and `TunnelLength`; the tool shows them.
- **Junctions on a structure.** A road can join a bridge or tunnel in the middle (`SplitRoad` keeps the structure on both halves) or end on its node; the junction node sits at the height of the deck or bore, so the joining road has to get there within the grade limit (usually on a bridge of its own).
- **Grade-separated crossings.** Where the planned road meets a road in the middle (not at a node), it passes over or under it, with no node and no junction, when the two surfaces are at least `crossingClearance` (5.5 m) apart in height and there is a deck or bore for it: the upper road is a bridge there, or the lower one is a tunnel (`SeparateCrossings`, `PassesOver`). The planned road's height at the crossing is its own straight profile between the other meetings, so dropping the junction also drops the height it would have forced. Otherwise the crossing is a junction as before (and a junction too close to the end of a road is refused). Crossings at a node are always junctions. Vehicles on the two roads never interact there.
- **Limits.** Upgrade, downgrade and re-signing keep the structure; upgrading a structure has no earthworks. Existing roads are never turned into structures, except that moving a node rebuilds its roads and works their structures out again.

### 5.9 Driving through junctions and right of way (M1)
- A vehicle stays on its lane while it crosses a junction: the lane has a tail past its end, the path to the next lane. The tail starts `reach` metres before the lane's end (`reach` = `JunctionReach`, 8 m) and is `P` long, the length of the path for the chosen next lane (`_moveLength`, per move). When the front bumper passes `zoneStart + P` (`ZoneEnd`) the vehicle is on the next lane, `reach` metres into it, with the overshoot carried over (`AdvanceLane`). For a straight crossing `P` is exactly the old jump (`reachIn + reachOut`), so only turns change length. `VehicleState.Position` can therefore exceed the lane length by up to the tail.
- Following: a vehicle in the tail is an ordinary leader for those behind it in the lane; across the junction the gap to the rearmost vehicle on the next lane is `ZoneEnd - pos + theirPos - reach - length` (`GapAcrossJunction`, also used by the rear-end check).
- Turn speed: each move has a speed limit from the mean radius of its path, `sqrt(turnLateralAcceleration * P / turn)` (floor `minTurnSpeed`, none under 0.05 rad of turn). `FreeSpeed` lets a vehicle approach it at `sqrt(limit^2 + 2 * comfortDecel * distanceLeft)`, so it brakes smoothly; IDM tracks that profile with up to about 25 percent overshoot at the start of the tail. At 4 m/s^2 a right-angle turn is taken at about 7 m/s.
- Drawing: `Simulation.JunctionPoseOf(vehicle, centre)` gives the pose of the vehicle's middle on the path (on the path to its next lane past the start of the tail, or on the path it came by while less than one reach into its lane); the game uses it instead of the lane's line, so cars really turn.
- **Right of way** (`JunctionInteraction`, within `junctionRange` 40 m of the end of the lane). A vehicle that has not entered the junction (front bumper before the edge, 8 m from the node) and can still stop before it (`v^2 < 2 * maxBraking * distance`) waits at the edge, as an obstacle at standstill for the IDM, for any other vehicle whose move conflicts with its own and which (a) is inside the junction or has come out of it with its tail still in, and has not yet left the stretch of its path where the two meet (5.1, `GetConflictZone`), or (b) would reach the edge sooner. Arrival time is `TimeToCover(distance, speed)` with the IDM acceleration, ties to the lower id; it is the same for every pair, so the order is total and no ring of vehicles can wait for each other (an earlier version compared arrival at each pair's own stretch, which is not transitive, and a 1000-car grid locked into four-way rings). A vehicle that would arrive second still goes when it can be through its stretch, with `crossingSafetyTime` (1 s) to spare, before the other gets to its own. A vehicle already inside the junction never waits, and one that cannot stop in time goes on.
- **Box clear.** A vehicle also stops at the edge when the rearmost vehicle on its exit lane is crawling (under `exitQueueSpeed` 3 m/s) with less than a vehicle length plus `minGap` between the start of the lane and its rear: entering would leave the car standing in the junction.
- **Collisions.** `DetectJunctionCollisions` puts every vehicle with its middle inside a junction (past the start of the tail, or less than one reach into its lane after one) on its path pose and tests rotated boxes (4.5 x 1.9 m) pairwise; overlap on different paths is an `IncidentKind.Junction` and wrecks both. With the rules above none happens in the test grids; it is what remains when a vehicle cannot stop in time, a wreck stands in the crossing, or a scenario puts cars there by hand.
- Effects seen: grids see fewer overtakes (about 4 on average per 25 minutes with 600 cars on an 8 x 8 grid, against about 10 before and 35 before turns were slowed) because everyone slows for each turn and waits for crossing traffic; 1000 cars on a 10 x 10 grid with avenues run at a mean 6.7 m/s with about one in six standing at a junction edge, against 8.6 m/s and one in thirty when crossing paths were only yielded to (cars drove through each other: 17 overlaps in 10 minutes). Conflict stretches use 5 m between centres and paths count as meeting 3.3 m apart; smaller values let collisions through again.
- Not done: left-turn waiting in the middle of the junction. Traffic lights are 5.20, priority, yield and stop signs 5.22.

### 5.10 Company, stations, lines and vehicles (M2)
- **Money** (`Company`, `Simulation.Economy.cs`). Whole currency units in a `long`. The company starts with `economy.startingMoney` and can borrow up to `maxLoan` (`TakeLoanCommand`, `RepayLoanCommand`). A command that costs money is priced first (`EstimateCost`, `DemolitionCost`, `EstimateUpgrade`, ... the same numbers the tools show), refused if `Money < price`, and charged only when the work was done. A ledger keeps a running total per `LedgerEntry` (construction, stations, depots, vehicles, income, upkeep, interest). `CanAfford(cost)` is public for the UI. The simulation's state hash includes money, loan and everything below, so the golden hashes in `DeterminismTests` were re-pinned when this landed (ambient behaviour did not change: they matched again after the vehicle-length work).
- **Days.** One day is `economy.dayLength` (1200 s, 24 000 ticks since C1; it was 60 s and then 600 s). At the turn of a day the company pays upkeep for every open station and every vehicle of the fleet and interest on the loan (the ministry's advance in the campaign), and at the turn of a month the campaign's books (5.27). Bankruptcy no longer exists: money can go below zero, which costs standing, and dismissal ends the game.
- **Stations** (`Station`). A station stands at a road node. `BuildStationCommand(x, z)` snaps to a node within 12 m, else to the road under the point, which is split there (at least `minApproach` 30 m from both ends); `FindStationSite` is the same decision without building, for the tool's preview. Vehicles stop on the lanes that run into the node and are at least `minApproach` long, with their front `stopMargin` (3 m) before the junction edge. A station whose node loses every road closes (`Active` false), a moved node takes its station along, and lines skip closed stations. Without towns or industries a station simply produces each cargo at `cargo[].supplyPerDay`, up to `maxWaiting`; with them `stations.coverageRadius` (150 m, the walking radius of the trips) decides what it serves. `BuildStationCommand(x, z, bays)` can build a station with bays (5.15).
- **Lines** (`Line`). An ordered list of at least two stations (no station twice in a row, the last may not equal the first); vehicles visit them in order and start over. `DeleteLineCommand` sells the fleet. A line keeps a name, income, upkeep and units delivered; `EditLineCommand` and `RenameLineCommand` change its stops and name (5.15), `SetLineColorCommand` its colour (5.17).
- **Vehicles** (`BuyVehicleCommand(depot, model, line)`). The roster is `vehicles[]` in the tuning file (id, cargo, capacity, length, top speed, price, upkeep, dwell times). A new vehicle appears on the right-most lane that leaves the depot's node, the one with the lower route cost to the line's first station, when the first `length + spawnClearance` of it is free and nothing is within 40 m of the end of any lane running into that node (otherwise a car would run into its tail: this was found as rear-end crashes in a 30-day grid run). Refused for an unknown model, a closed depot, a deleted line, no money or no room. Company vehicles are ordinary tier 0 vehicles with `_line >= 0`: same IDM, lane changes, overtaking and incidents. `SellVehicleCommand` pays `sellRefundShare` of the price.
- **Routing** (`Simulation.Routing.cs`). For each station a field over the lane graph: the time to drive from the start of each lane to the station's stop, found backwards from the stop lanes by Dijkstra (lane time = length / limit, plus the junction path). Made when first needed and again when the network version changes. At each junction (`AdvanceLane`, `Depart`, network sync) a company vehicle takes the turn with the lowest `field[next] + move time`; a lane with a wreck costs 600 s extra; if no turn leads there it picks at random as ambient traffic does. Ambient traffic still chooses at random (the random walk stays until trip groups exist). Dijkstra distances do not depend on queue tie order, and ties between turns go to the first in lane order, so routes are deterministic.
- **Buying** (`Simulation.Fleet.cs`). A vehicle is put on a lane leaving the depot that can reach the line's first station; a depot with no road to it refuses (`BuyVehicleCommand` returns -1), and `DepotsByDistance(line)` lists the connected depots nearest first, which is what the game uses, so a depot on a road of its own never takes a vehicle it cannot send anywhere.
- **Stops** (`Simulation.Fleet.cs`). A vehicle heading for a station brakes for an obstacle at the stop position (`StopInteraction`, same IDM term as a leader). It has arrived when it is within `arrivalGap` of the stop and slower than `arrivalSpeed`; it then unloads (pay, below), loads up to its capacity from the station's waiting cargo of the type it carries, and stands halted for `dwellSeconds + secondsPerUnit * (unloaded + loaded)`. While halted it is an obstacle to followers and the junction rules treat it as standing still outside the junction. After loading it retargets the next open station and turns toward it. A vehicle unloads everything at the next stop, so cargo has one origin (the previous stop of the line).
- **Pay.** `units * km * payPerUnitKm * share`, with `km` the straight-line distance between the two stations and `share` falling from 1 at `freeSeconds` of travel to `minPayShare` at `zeroSeconds`. Passengers and goods are separate cargo types (`cargo[]`).
- **Vehicle length.** `_length[]` per vehicle (a car is 4.5 m, a bus 10 m, a truck 8 m by the roster); gaps, collisions, lane changes, overtaking, spawning and junction poses use the right one. The junction conflict tables assume car-sized bodies, so a pair with a longer vehicle uses a second set of tables (`wideConflictDistance` 8 m and `wideBodyClearance` 6.1 m in the tuning file: a bus reaches 5 m ahead of its path). Widening the stretches by a guessed margin did not help; using the larger distances in the real geometry did (a 5 x 5 grid with 150 cars and 8 company vehicles: 3 junction collisions in 25 days became 0 over 12 seeds of 30 days). Width is still the car's 1.9 m for every vehicle.
- **Game layer.** The Lines panel (right) lists each line with its colour, stations, fleet, loading count and money, and has buttons to buy a bus or truck for it (at the first depot that has room) or delete it. The top panel shows money, loan, day and the totals, and has Borrow and Repay 100k. Tools: **Station** (hover shows the spot, the price and the coverage radius) and **New line** (click stations in order, Enter finishes). Company vehicles are drawn at their real length, taller than cars, in their line's colour. `--selftest-lines` builds a road, a depot, two stations and two lines through the host API, runs 30 days and prints the books.

### 5.11 Towns and buildings (M3a)

Files: `Town.cs` (types), `TownGenerator.cs`, `RoadIndex.cs`, `Simulation.Towns.cs`; tuning in `towns` (section 8).

- **Time.** `economy.dayLength` was 600 s so that a commute fits inside a day (a bus crossing 900 m takes about a minute); since C1 it is 1200 s and a month is 3 days (5.23). `Simulation.Today` gives year, month and day of the month from the day counter (12-month years from `economy.startYear`, HUD only), `TimeOfDay` the share of the day gone (0 at midnight).
- **Generation.** `TownGenerator.Generate(net, seed, count)` runs before the `Simulation` exists, so its roads are free and not in the command log; a replay generates the same towns again from the same seed. It draws from its own `SimRandom` (seed xor a constant), so the simulation's stream and every old golden hash are untouched. For each town it tries random places (`attempts`) that are dry (`waterMargin` above the water surface everywhere under the disc around the grid), level (`maxHeightRange`), `minSpacing` from other towns and `edgeMargin` from the map edge; the first failure ends the search, so fewer towns than asked can come out (`WorldPlan.Requested` keeps the number asked). Streets are a square grid of 3 to 5 blocks of 120 m, rotated to run along the contours of the slope (a drawn angle on level ground), the line through the middle in one direction an avenue and the rest two-lane, all built with `RoadNetwork.BuildRoad` (so they cut and fill the terrain and cross water on bridges like any road). Buildings stand on the four sides of each block, two to a side, 30 m apart and centred so that neighbours at a block's corner do not touch; a building is as far from its street as an avenue is wide plus its own half-size plus a gap, so widening a street never reaches one. Three rings out from the middle (`rings`) set how many lots are built on, the mix of home, commerce and industry, and the size (three tiers each, `buildingTypes`). A building is kept only if a road really is within `accessRadius` of it. Names come from a shuffled list.
- **Model.** `Town` (name, centre, satisfaction) and `Building` (type, tier, position, facing, state, the day it entered that state) are plain classes in id-ordered lists; ids are never reused and a demolished building stays in the list. `Simulation.AddTowns(plan)` loads a `WorldPlan` once, before the first step. A 128 m grid of building ids answers `BuildingsNear`. The state hash includes the buildings and satisfaction only when there are any, so maps without towns hash as before.
- **Access and abandonment.** When the network version changes, `EvaluateAccess` builds a `RoadIndex` (a coarse grid of road centre lines) and sets each building Active (a road centre line within `accessRadius` of its middle) or Inactive. At the turn of each day an Inactive building older than `inactiveMonths` months becomes Abandoned (the town loses `abandonedPenalty` satisfaction) and an Abandoned one older than `abandonedMonths` is demolished by the town. A road that comes back before that makes it Active again. Each month every town regains `recoveryPerMonth` satisfaction, up to the maximum.
- **Roads through buildings.** A new road (`BuildRoadCommand`) or a node move (`MoveNodeCommand`, using `RoadNetwork.PlannedMoveRoads`) demolishes every building whose footprint (1.2 times its half-size) comes within half the road's width of the road's centre line. The price of each building (by type and tier) is added to the road's own price: the command is refused if the total cannot be paid, otherwise the buildings are demolished, the price is charged under `LedgerEntry.Demolition` and the town loses `demolishedPenalty` satisfaction per building. `EstimateRoadDemolition` and `EstimateMoveDemolition` give the same figures for the build preview.
- **Stations.** A station covers the Active buildings within `stations.coverageRadius` of its node (cached, refreshed when buildings, stations or nodes change). Until M3c it also collected passengers from them at a fixed rate per building; that is gone: on a map with towns people appear at a station only when a trip group queues there (5.13). On a map without towns the fixed `supplyPerDay` of the cargo list still applies (the older tests and the demo grid use it).
- **Game layer.** `SimHost.NewTownsWorld(seed, towns)` (hills terrain, towns, 30 ambient cars per town) returns the number of towns that fit; buildings are one MultiMesh of boxes (kind colour, grey when cut off, dark when abandoned) redrawn when `Simulation.BuildingsVersion` changes, with a floating name per town. The new-game screen has a Towns map (the first on offer) with a 2 to 10 slider; the HUD shows the date and time of day, a Towns window (people, jobs, buildings by kind, satisfaction bar, Go to) and, in the build preview, the buildings a road would demolish and what they cost; the station preview shows how many buildings it would serve.

### 5.12 Industries, cargo and building needs (M3b)

Files: `IndustryGenerator.cs`, `Simulation.Industries.cs`, changes in `Simulation.Fleet.cs`, `Simulation.Towns.cs`; tuning in `industries`, `cargo` and the `sustain` and `construction` lists of each building tier.

- **Cargo.** Eight types: passengers, goods, wood, planks, stone, materials (building materials), grain, food. Chains: wood to planks (sawmill), stone to materials (cement works), grain to food (food plant), planks to goods (factory). The fixed `supplyPerDay` of a cargo only applies on a map with neither towns nor industries (`Settled` false), where every station also accepts everything; this keeps the demo grid and the older tests as they were.
- **Industries.** Seven types in `industries.types`: a primary industry has no inputs and makes `perMonth` of its output; a processor makes at most `perMonth`, each unit using the listed amounts of its inputs, and stops when an input runs out or the output stock (`maxOutput`) is full. Production is continuous (a month's amount spread over the month's ticks). Each step an industry's output goes, evenly, to the stations that cover it (centre within `stations.coverageRadius`), as far as they have room (`cargo.maxWaiting`); the rest stays in its output stock. `IndustryGenerator.Generate` runs after the towns: types in file order, `counts.few|normal|many` of each, on level dry ground (`maxHeightRange`) clear of every town's grid (`townClearance`), `minSpacing` apart, with its own random stream; a processor is first tried only where a producer of each of its inputs and a town lie within `generation.chainReach` (1500 m), and only when no such place turns up is it put anywhere that will do and listed in `WorldPlan.Unlinked`. Roads to industries are the player's.
- **Acceptance.** A station accepts a cargo when something it covers uses it: any standing building (passengers, whose destination is the station itself; the queues are per destination station, 5.13), a building whose tier lists it under `sustain` or `construction`, or an industry that lists it as an input. `Simulation.Accepts` is cached with the coverage, which is refreshed when buildings, stations or nodes change.
- **Freight trucks.** The truck's model cargo is `freight`: at a stop, when empty, it takes the freight cargo (anything but passengers) with the most waiting (at least one unit) among those the next stop on the line accepts; a bus takes passengers on the same rule. A load is unloaded only at a stop that accepts it; otherwise it stays on board and the vehicle takes nothing more. Pay is for what is taken, by the origin station's distance and the cargo's rate as before.
- **Deliveries.** `Distribute` splits the units over the buildings and industries in range that have room, by room (a building's room is what is missing from its monthly stock plus what is missing from its construction stock; an industry's is `maxInput` less its stock). Units beyond the total room are lost and not paid; when the units cover all the room every taker is filled exactly (5.16). A building fills its monthly stock first, then its construction stock.
- **Needs.** Each building starts with `initialStockMonths` months of its `sustain` amounts and holds at most `sustainStockMonths`. At the turn of each month every standing building uses up its monthly amounts; the share of the town's needs that were missing is `Town.UnmetShare`, and the town loses `unmetPenalty` times that. The construction stock fills but nothing spends it until M3d.
- **Game layer.** `NewTownsWorld(seed, towns, industries)`; industries are boxes in the buildings MultiMesh with their names above them; the Industries window lists output, stock and inputs, the Stations window what is waiting and what each station accepts, the Towns window the share of needs met; the new-game screen has Few, Normal and Many.

### 5.13 Trip groups (M3c)

Files: `Trips.cs` (`TripGroup`, `TripMode`, `TripLeg`, `PaxLot`), `Simulation.Trips.cs`, `Simulation.Transit.cs`, car routing in `Simulation.Routing.cs`; tuning in `trips`.

- **Groups.** `AddTowns` makes the groups once: the people of each home split into groups of at most `groupSize` (10; a tier 1 home of 20 people has two). A group has its home, a workplace, a commerce building for the evening and a car or none (`carShare`). The workplace is drawn weighted by the jobs not yet taken (workplaces fill up, and crowd when all are taken) times nearness `1 / (1 + (d / nearness)^2)` within `workRadius`, so most work in their own town and some in the next; leisure the same by size. All draws come from their own `SimRandom` (seed xor a constant), so maps without towns and every old golden hash are untouched. At the turn of each day a group that is at home and whose workplace or leisure place is gone (demolished, abandoned, cut off) draws a new one. A group whose home is demolished is retired at the next turn of the day it is not travelling (`TripGroup.Retired`, `People` 0, no workplace or leisure place, never departs again) and the jobs it held go back to its workplace (5.16).
- **Schedule.** Four trips a day: out to work, back, out for leisure, back. Each starts at its group's own point inside the window of the tuning file (`windows`, in hours: 7 to 9, 16 to 18, 18 to 21, 21 to 22.5) plus a fixed jitter up to `jitterMinutes` (45), both drawn once from the group's seed, so a group leaves at the same time every day. A trip never starts before the previous one is over (a slow trip pushes the day back); a leisure trip is made on `leisureChance` of the days (a pure function of group and day), stays at least `leisureStayMinutes`, and is dropped for the day when the group is already past its window. A group without a workplace skips the work trips. A day is 600 s, so a window is about 50 s long and a 100 m walk takes a tenth of a day.
- **Mode.** Within `walkRadius` (150 m) or when both buildings are reached from the same road node, walk. Otherwise the lowest generalised cost, seconds plus `moneyWeight` times money, of what the group can use: by car (if it has one, from a road node within `carAccessRadius` of each building; the field of the destination node gives the time, plus `carOverheadSeconds`; money is `carCostPerKm`), and by public transport (a station covering each building and a line that has both and at least one vehicle: walk, wait `transitWaitShare` (0.5) of the time between vehicles, which is the line's round trip over its fleet (5.17), ride at `transitRideFactor` times the car time, walk; money is the passenger pay rate). Direct lines only. Nothing usable means the trip is not made (the group stays; counted as "could not go"), except the way home, which is a walk however long. Choices are cached per pair of buildings and car flag and made again when the road network, the lines, their vehicles or the coverage change.
- **Walking** is a timer: `walkDetour` times the straight line over `walkSpeed` (2.5 m/s, quicker than life so that a short walk stays short beside a drive in a day of 1200 s), no pathfinding, no entity. The arrival of every walk, of the last leg of a bus ride and of any other timer is an event in a priority queue (tick, then order of scheduling).
- **Cars.** A car stands for `peoplePerCar` (5) people. A group's cars are queued for the road and put there one every `carDepartGapSeconds`, a few tries per step (`carSpawnAttempts`), at most `maxCars` (200) on the road at once, on the lane out of the origin's access node with room and the shortest way to the destination (`carSpawnClearance` beyond its length; not while a car is about to arrive at that node). The car is an ordinary vehicle (IDM, lane changes, overtaking, incidents) that routes at each junction by the field of its destination node (the same backward Dijkstra as stations, over the lanes into the node, cached per network version). It is taken off the road within `carArriveRange` (30 m) of the end of the lane into that node, so a queue at the destination does not block the junction; after `carTimeoutSeconds` (400) a car still on the road is taken off too and its people count as arrived, so a jam clears itself slowly; a wreck that is cleared counts as arrived as well. A group whose cars never find room is released after the same time.
- **Public transport.** A group walks to the best station and joins its queue as a lot (group, people, destination station, since when). A bus (when towns exist) unloads the lots whose destination is the station it stands at, paid by the distance between the lot's stations and the time on board, then takes on the lots waiting there whose destination is another stop of its line (queue order, lots split to fit), up to its capacity. People get off only at their own destination, walk to their building and arrive; a lot whose destination station closed is let off wherever the bus is. People who have waited `transitGiveUpSeconds` (900) or whose station closed give up. A bus that leaves the world (sold, wreck cleared) puts its people down where they set out from. `Station.Waiting` for passengers mirrors the queue (the HUD still reads it) and `Station.WaitingFor()` splits it by destination. Without towns buses still load the fixed supply as before.
- **Counts.** Each town counts the people-trips of the day (and of the day before) by mode, including those that could not be made. A group keeps the mode and duration of its last completed trip (for M3d's connectivity).
- **State.** The hash includes the groups, queues, riders and the trip stream only when groups exist. Trip cars, groups and queues replay from the seed and the command log (`TripsReplayFromTheCommandLog`).
- **Performance.** Five towns with about 3200 people and 330 groups: a mean of 0.04 ms a step in a Release build, 0.15 ms at the 99th percentile, 3 ms at worst, up to about 275 vehicles on the road in the rush; the 1000-vehicle step test is unchanged. In the morning rush a car takes two to ten times the free-flow time from the field (junctions with many cars, queues at destinations); the generalised cost does not yet charge for that.

### 5.14 Growth (M3d)

Files: `Simulation.Growth.cs`; lots in `TownGenerator.LotSites`; `Town` (grid, `Connectivity`, `LastGrowth`), `Lot`; tuning `towns.growth`.

- **Lots.** A lot is a place beside a street where a building can stand: block (column, row), side, lot on the side. Its middle is as far from the street as the biggest size needs, whatever stands on it, so a building can grow in place (generated towns moved a little when this went in). A town keeps the lots of its grid; the buildings of the plan are matched to the lots they stand on (within 1 m; hand-placed ones belong to none), and a demolished building frees its lot. A free lot can be built on when it has a road within `accessRadius` and none closer than `growth.lotClearance`, is dry and on the map, and has no standing building within 22 m or industry on it.
- **Connectivity.** At the turn of each month, a town's connectivity is the share of its active homes from which a group completed an outbound work or leisure trip within `timeBudgetSeconds` (300) that month (`TripGroup.ReachedMonth`). A town starts at 1; one whose people can reach nothing (the road is cut, the workplaces are in another town with no link) falls to 0.
- **Gates.** The same turn, a town whose satisfaction is below `minSatisfaction` (50) or whose connectivity is below `minConnectivity` (0.6) does not grow, and says which (`LastGrowth.Blocked`). It never shrinks except by the abandonment of 5.11.
- **Growth.** For each active building in id order (buildings founded this month wait): when its construction stock holds the whole `construction` list of its size, it spends it and goes up a size; a home gets the new people as new trip groups (from the next day). A building at the top size spends the same list to found a new smallest building on the free lot of its town nearest to it (kind drawn from the mix of the lot's ring, from a stream of its own, seed xor a constant); a home founded gets its groups. A town with no free lot, and `extendGrid`, first adds a column or row of blocks on the side of the grid nearest to the parent: dry ground, no building or industry in the strip, then the streets (the line of each existing street continued by one block, the main street staying an avenue, and a closing street) built with `RoadNetwork.BuildRoad`, free and outside the command log (a replay grows the same way), and the lots of the new blocks. It tries the other sides in turn; with none it waits. The sim picks the new streets up at once (access, routing); the renderer redraws the roads when the network version moves.
- **Observed.** Four generated towns, nothing delivered: no growth, and satisfaction falls about 2 a month once the starting stock is gone and the town stalls at 50 after about 7 months. With every building topped up to full construction stock every day (a cheat, to see the ceiling): people 3200 to 64000 in 8 months, with about 100 new buildings and a block or two a town each month, until the trip car cap (300) keeps people from getting where they go and connectivity falls under the gate, which slows the towns again.

### 5.15 Line editing, windows, bays and balance (M2 follow-ups, first M3 balance pass)

Files: `Simulation.Fleet.cs`, `Station.cs`, `Simulation.Trips.cs`, `game/hud.gd`, `game/SimHost.cs`; probes in `BalanceProbe.cs`, `Worlds.cs`.

- **Editing a line.** `EditLineCommand(line, stations)` replaces the stops (same rules as creating: at least two, open, none twice in a row, the last not the first). Each vehicle of the line keeps heading for its station when the new list has it and takes its new place in the list; when its station was dropped it heads for the stop at the same place (the last if the list is shorter) and takes the new turn at the next junction it has not yet entered (a vehicle inside a junction keeps its path). People on a bus whose station is no longer on the line are put down at the next stop. `RenameLineCommand` names a line (1 to 24 characters; not in the state hash). Both are commands, so they replay.
- **What stations and vehicles remember.** A station counts the people who boarded, got off and gave up waiting today and yesterday (`Station.Today`, `Yesterday`); a vehicle counts the upkeep paid for it (`UpkeepPaidFor`) next to its income, so its profit is known. None of the counters change behaviour; the vehicle upkeep is in `FleetHash`.
- **Windows.** The Lines window has a name box, the stops with up, down and remove buttons, an "add stop" choice and the buy and delete buttons. The Stations window names each station for its town or industry and number ("Aldermark 2"), lists the lines that stop there, what it covers (homes, shops, other workplaces, industries), waiting people by destination, and the counts of yesterday and today. The Vehicles window lists every company vehicle: what it is, where it is heading, its load, income, upkeep and profit, with Go to and Sell. All of it is assembled in `SimHost.GetLines`, `GetStations`, `GetVehicles`; the Godot scripts only emit intent.
- **Bays.** A station built with bays (`stations.bayPrice` more to build, `bayUpkeepPerDay` more a day) takes a loading vehicle on its right-most lane out of the lane: `_bay[i]` removes the vehicle from the per-lane ordering (`RebuildOrder` skips it, `_orderCount` is the number ordered), so followers, collision checks, right of way, spawn checks and overtaking never see it, and it is drawn 3.4 m to the right of the lane. Room is `stations.bayCapacity` vehicles at a time per station; a vehicle that finds them taken loads in the lane as before. A vehicle leaves its bay when no vehicle is within `bayExitGap` (30 m) behind it and none overlaps it or is within the spawn clearance ahead; until then it waits. `Station.InBay`, `Simulation.InBay(v)`, the bay flag is in `FleetHash`.
- **Congestion estimate.** For each hour of the day the simulation keeps how many times longer than the empty-road time a car trip took (`_congestion[24]`, 1 at the start); every car trip that ends moves the estimate for the hour it set out in by `trips.congestionSmoothing` (0.15) of the way to what it took, at most `trips.maxCongestion` (6). The car's seconds in the choice of mode are the empty-road ride times the estimate plus the overhead, so a jammed rush hour sends people with cars to the lines (or leaves their trip made on foot or not made). It is part of the trip hash, and of the mode cache key and version (in quarters), so a choice is made again when people's idea of the jam moves. Without a company line there is nothing to switch to, so on a map with no lines it changes nothing.
- **Cars that stand still.** A trip car that has moved at less than 0.3 m/s for `trips.carStuckSeconds` (90 s, 7.5 percent of a day since C1) is taken off like one that ran out of time, its people counted as arrived (they gave up and walked). The longer `carTimeoutSeconds` stays as the limit for a car that crawls.
- **Pay and coverage, measured.** Probes (`BalanceProbe`, run with `BALANCE_PROBE=1`; settings by environment variable) put a six-stop passenger line with four buses across the two closest of four towns on six seeds, ten days each, and a three-truck line from a forest to a sawmill. Before: coverage 90 m, passengers 40 per unit-km: 30 to 65 people a day carried per line, a mean income of 250 a day against 700 to 1300 of upkeep, and the trucks 930 to 2000 a day each against 450 (three to five times a bus). After: coverage 150 m, passengers 65, freight about 55 percent of its old rates (wood 16, planks 22, stone 14, materials 25, grain 15, food 28, goods 30): buses earn about 2 to 3 times their upkeep on the middling seeds (900 to 7600 a day on four buses, one seed barely pays because its buses crawl through the rush at 1.2 m/s), trucks 1.5 to 2.5 times. `BalanceTests` pins the ranges: a bus line over 1.3 times upkeep, a truck line between 1.3 and 4.5.
- **Bugs found on the way** (both wrecked buses in town traffic; 16 of 24 buses were lost in ten days on the probe maps, 7 after the first fix, 2 after both, the rest overtaking accidents): (1) the rear-end check across a junction compared a car waiting to merge with the first vehicle of the exit lane along the car's own short turn, so a 10 m bus entering that lane from another street seemed to overlap it; it now only applies to a vehicle that came by the follower's lane (`_prevLane`). (2) The room to spawn compared only the distance between fronts, so a trip car could be put on the road inside the body of a bus ahead; `IsClearFor` counts the real length of the vehicles already there.

### 5.17 The medium-priority block (2026-10-19)

Files: `Simulation.Transit.cs`, `Simulation.Towns.cs`, `Simulation.Stations.cs`, `Simulation.Fleet.cs`, `SimCommand.cs`, `game/SimHost.cs`, `game/hud.gd`, `game/main.gd`, `game/settings.gd`.

- **Transit wait from the round trip.** `trips.transitWaitSeconds` (240 s for a line with one vehicle, divided by its vehicles) became `trips.transitWaitShare` (0.5). `Simulation.LineCycleSeconds(line)` is the time one vehicle takes to go once round a line: the road time of every leg from a stop to the next (the last back to the first, as a car would drive it, times `transitRideFactor`) plus the standing time (`dwellSeconds`) at every stop; infinity when a leg has no road. The cycles are counted again when lines, fleets or roads change. A vehicle comes every cycle over fleet seconds, several lines that share two stations add their arrivals, and people wait `transitWaitShare` of the time between vehicles. A longer line therefore makes people wait longer and a line cut in two brings nobody. `TripTests` pin: the wait is the share of the round trip over the fleet (a second bus halves it), a longer line has a longer round trip, a cut line has none and brings nobody.

The buildings are redrawn when the switch moves and when a home changes (`BuildingsVersion`).

### 5.19 Rail (M4, 2026-10-20)

Files: `RailNetwork.cs`, `RailTuning.cs`, `Simulation.Rail.cs`, `Simulation.Trains.cs`, `Simulation.Crossings.cs`; `data/tuning.json` section `rail`; tests `RailNetworkTests`, `TrainTests`, `SignalTests`, `CrossingTests`, `RailBehaviourTests`, `RailEconomyTests`, `MixedNetworkTests`, `RailDeterminismTests`. The decisions are `docs/m4-plan.md` and ADR 0004.

- **Network.** `RailNetwork` (owned by the simulation, `Simulation.Rail`; it uses the road network's terrain) holds nodes, tracks and signals. A `Track` is one piece between two nodes, straight or an arc (`RoadGeometry`), single and used both ways, with a speed limit made from `rail.speedLimit` reduced for the curve (`curveLateralAcceleration`) and the grade. Building (`BuildTrack`) follows the road rules: it joins nodes, splits the tracks it ends on or crosses (a junction, never a diamond), refuses what is too short, too steep (`maxGrade` 4 %), too tight (`minRadius` 90 m), in water or with a junction too close to another, puts bridges and tunnels where the road rules would (`maxFillDepth`, `maxCutDepth`) and carves the ground with cut and fill. A node connects two of its tracks when the turn is at most `maxTurnAngle`; `TurnSpeed` is the limit through the bend. Splitting a track keeps its signals and remaps the trains on it.
- **Boundaries and reservation.** A node is a boundary when a signal, a station or a depot stands there. A train holds tracks and nodes (`_trackOwner`, `_nodeOwner`) and moves only on what it holds. It asks for the stretch beyond the end of what it holds when its front is within braking distance plus `reserveLookahead` of it: a **block** request takes every track reachable from the boundary node up to the next boundaries (the train's route or not), a **path** request only the tracks and nodes of the route up to the next boundary. The kind comes from the signal that faces the track the train arrives by (none counts as block; a stretch that starts at a depot or a station is a path). The boundary node where a stretch ends is not part of it (a train waiting at a signal has not entered the node), except the node of the station the train is stopping at. What a train holds is released piece by piece as its rear passes (`Hold.Hi`). A train that cannot get the stretch stops `stopMargin` short of the boundary and waits; after `RerouteAfterSeconds` it looks for another way to its station over track nobody else holds (`TryReroute`), which is how two trains pass at a loop.
- **Movement.** Per step a train works out the speed it may have (`Drive`): the vehicle's top speed; the stop at the end of what it holds (a station exactly, a red signal `stopMargin` short), braking at `brakePlanShare` of the service brake; the limit of every track and turn any part of the train is on or will reach (a limit applies until the rear has passed); a crossing it must not enter. It then accelerates (the smaller of the tractive effort at standstill and power over speed, less rolling, air and grade resistance, for the mass with the load) or brakes (service, emergency when too fast by 3 m/s). A train can never pass the end of what it holds. Route positions are distances along the planned route (`RouteStep` list) and shift when steps behind the rear are dropped.
- **Routes.** `FindRailPath` is Dijkstra over (track, direction) by travel time at the posted limits, with the turn rule at each node, ties by track number. All trains are push-pull: standing still with the whole train on its route it may reverse (`ReverseRoute`, which mirrors route, holds and coordinates) when that reaches the next station sooner than carrying on, with a 25 s penalty; a station at a dead end needs no search at all.
- **Stations, depots, lines.** `Station.Mode` is `Road` or `Rail`; a rail station has `RailNode` and a map position (`Simulation.PointOf`), `Node` is -1. Coverage, acceptance, queues, pay and the trip modes work from the position, so towns and industries serve a rail station like a road one. A `Line` has a mode (`CreateLineCommand` reads it from its first station; mixing is refused). `BuildRailStation` splits a track to make a node; `AddRailDepot` needs a rail node. `BuyTrain` (the depot is `BuyTrainCommand.NearestDepot` from the game) takes a locomotive and a wagon by id and a count, needs the way from the depot to the line's first station (a depot on the first station starts the train loading there); the train stands in the depot (its body behind the node) and drives out once the depot's node is free (see 5.21). A train carries one wagon type: passengers (the queues of 5.13, the same boarding and alighting code as buses) or freight (the cargo with most waiting that the next stop accepts, as a truck). Upkeep is charged daily per train and per rail station.
- **Deadlocks.** Every 2 s of simulated time trains that have waited for track for `blockedSeconds` are followed from the train that holds what they need; a ring is recorded once as a `RailWarning` (the game shows it as a message and paints the trains red). Selling one of the trains ends it. Two trains turning round at the ends of one single track meet head on and deadlock sooner or later: that needs a passing loop with path signals, as in the tests.
- **Level crossings.** A `LevelCrossing` is found (`AddCrossingsFound`, after a track or a road is built) where a track and a road cross within `rail.crossings.clearance` in height, and is re-resolved to its current track and road whenever either network changes. It is refused (`TrackCrossingsAllowed`, `RoadCrossingsAllowed`) closer than `minFromNode` to the end of the road, of the track or a junction, or at less than `minAngle`; moving a road junction so that its roads would cross a track is refused too; towns do not extend their streets within `RailClearance` of a track. States `Open`, `Lowering` (`lowerSeconds`), `Closed`: a train that will cross (the crossing is inside what it holds) and will reach it within `warnSeconds + lowerSeconds` closes it; it opens `clearSeconds` after the last train has cleared. Road vehicles get a stop line `roadStopMargin` before the band the rails take (`CrossingInteraction`, in the step loop beside the junction rule): while the barriers are coming down or down, or while the vehicle ahead is standing so close beyond the crossing that this one would stop on the rails; one that can no longer stop goes on. A train brakes for a crossing whose barriers are not down only if it would otherwise reach it (emergency braking profile), and for one with a wreck or a standing vehicle in it from far off. A train in the band and a car in it is a collision (`IncidentKind.LevelCrossing`): the car is wrecked, a crew is called as for any wreck, the train halts for `collisionHalt` seconds, and the crossing is blocked for trains until the wreck is cleared.
- **Hash.** The rail mixes into the state hash only when a track, depot or train exists, so road-only worlds keep their hashes. `RailDeterminismTests` pins a scenario (railway with a loop and path signals, a road across it, two trains, a bus line, ambient traffic; 20 minutes): 0x24A1F669E8A6208C on macOS and on Linux (0x9CABBCD32ADBD550 on both since the price change of 5.22).
- **Numbers.** (Prices changed in the balance pass of 5.22: track 110 a metre, station 20 000, depot 30 000, locomotive 120 000; the sentences below give the earlier figures where they differ.) In the shipped tuning a diesel locomotive (18 m, 90 t, 1.6 MW, 220 kN, 36 m/s, 140 000, 1000 a day) pulls up to 8 coaches (48 people, 20 m, 36 000, 200 a day) or freight wagons (40 units, 16 m, 30 000, 150 a day): a four-coach train runs for 1800 a day against a bus line of the same capacity for about 2500; track costs 160 per metre. A four-coach line between two towns 750 m apart earned about 4000 a day against 1800 upkeep in `RailEconomyTests`; a two-wagon freight train from a forest to a sawmill 1.2 to 1.5 times its upkeep. Rail pays better the more there is to carry: the first forest alone barely feeds one train.

### 5.21 Leaving the depot freely, and the garage (2026-10-21)

Files: `Simulation.Garage.cs`, ghosts in `Simulation.Fleet.cs`, trains in `Simulation.Trains.cs` and `Simulation.Rail.cs`; tests `GarageTests`.

- **Nothing refuses a purchase for lack of room.** A road vehicle bought for a line comes out of the depot on the lane with the quickest way to the line's first station. When the road at the depot is clear it is an ordinary vehicle at once (as before). When another vehicle stands in the doorway or one is about to arrive, it is a **ghost** (`IsGhost`) that waits **inside the depot** (`IsInDepot`; not on the road, not drawn) until there is room: nobody within `SpawnClearance` of its place, nothing about to arrive at the node (`TrafficNear`), and no ghost ahead of it in the queue (vehicles bought together come out one after another, in the order of purchase). It then drives out at up to 6 m/s, out of the lane ordering (like a vehicle in a bay), so cars do not brake for it; it never goes where another vehicle is (it stops short of any vehicle ahead on its lane) and becomes an ordinary vehicle once its whole length is out and the lane has room (`StepGhosts`). It stops short of the junction if there never is room. Cars are not made to stop for a ghost (it only exists at a depot exit); a car that reaches the doorway while a ghost is out can still drive through it, and a ghost is not seen by level crossings (decided, `m4-review.md`). A train bought while another still stands on the depot's node waits **in the depot** (`Train.InDepot`, status `InDepot`, cars not drawn): it holds nothing, and takes the depot node, by train number, when it is free. Only a missing way from the depot to the first station, a closed depot or no money refuses a purchase.
- **The garage.** A vehicle or train can be bought for no line (`BuyParkedVehicleCommand`, `BuyParkedTrainCommand`; `AnyDepot` takes the first open depot): it is paid for and waits in the depot as a `ParkedVehicle` (`Simulation.Garage`; it pays `economy.parkedUpkeepShare` (10 %) of its daily upkeep while it waits, charged to the company and to no line, `ChargeGarageUpkeep`). `AssignParkedCommand(parked, line)` puts it on a road or rail line of its own kind: it comes out of its depot like a bought vehicle (any open depot with a way to the line when its own is closed); `WhyNotAssign` says in words why it cannot (wrong kind of line, no road or track from the depot, closed station). `SellParkedCommand` sells it for the usual share.
- **Sending back.** `ParkVehicleCommand` takes a road vehicle off its line (it stops loading, drops what it carries, and drives to the nearest depot it can reach by the same routing trip cars use, `_returnDepot`; at the depot it goes into the garage; people on board get off). `ParkTrainCommand` does the same for a train (`Train.ReturnDepot`: `PlanLeg` then plans to the depot's node instead of a station; a train still in its depot is parked at once). A returning vehicle no longer counts in its line's fleet. The old one-step commands (`BuyVehicleCommand`, `BuyTrainCommand`) remain and buy and assign in one go.
- **Hash.** Ghost, returning and garage state are mixed in only when present, so the old golden hashes are unchanged.
- **Game.** The Vehicles window has a depot choice and buy buttons (road depot: buses and trucks; rail depot: trains), a list of vehicles waiting in depots (a choice of line of the right kind, **Put on line**, Go to, Sell), and a **To depot** button on every vehicle on the road. Buying from the Lines window still sends the vehicle straight out and now says exactly why it could not.

### 5.22 Rail balance, road signs and the benchmark job (2026-10-21)

Files: `Simulation.Signs.cs`, `RoadSignTuning` (`roads.signs`), `game/SimHostSigns.cs`, `RoadSignTests`, `StepBenchmarks`, `RailBalanceProbe`, `.github/workflows/sim-tests.yml`.

- **Rail balance.** `RailBalanceProbe` (`RAIL_PROBE=1 dotnet test --filter RailBalanceProbe`) lays a four-coach line against six buses (and two freight wagons against four trucks) between every pair of towns on eight maps and prints capital, income, upkeep and payback. Findings: a railway cost about 2.5 times the buses (track 160 a metre against a road at 40, plus a 140 000 locomotive) and paid back later on every pair a bus line was already good on; freight earns the same by rail and by truck (the industry's output is the limit, and pay only falls after 240 s, which no distance in reach comes to) so rail never paid back sooner. Prices lowered: track 160 to 110 a metre, rail station 30 000 to 20 000, rail depot 45 000 to 30 000, locomotive 140 000 to 120 000. Now a railway costs 1.9 to 2.3 times the buses for the same seats and earns more a day on the pairs where the buses fill up; `RailEconomyTests.ARailwayCostsAboutTwiceWhatBusesCostForTheSameSeats_AndEarnsMore` pins that. Not changed, and worth a decision: no passenger line earns anything between towns more than about 1.8 km apart, because trips never go beyond `trips.workRadius`, so rail's speed buys nothing on a long line; freight is production-limited, so a railway pays only when it carries several industries or a long haul.
- **Signs.** `BuildRoadSignCommand(x, z, kind)` and `RemoveRoadSignCommand(x, z)` act on the approach nearest the click at a junction of three roads or more without lights. Kinds: priority, yield, stop; a priority sign also goes on the approach across the junction. A sign is found again by the heading of its approach (`RoadSign.Angle`, tolerance 0.3 rad), so it survives a split of the road and goes when no road comes that way. Every lane running into a junction has a rank (priority 0, no sign 1, yield or stop 2). In `JunctionInteraction` a vehicle that has not entered the junction ignores the lower-ranked vehicles that have not entered either, and waits for higher-ranked ones whatever the order of arrival unless it can be through before they arrive (the existing test); equal ranks keep the arrival order. A vehicle that gives way looks `approachRange` (90 m) back for cross traffic and starts to brake for a stop line from as far. A stop sign holds the vehicle until it has stood still (below `stopSpeed`) within `stopLine` of the edge for `stopSeconds` (`_stopHold`, zeroed on every new lane and mixed into the hash only when signs exist). Ranks are a partial order, so signs cannot make a ring of vehicles wait for each other; a vehicle that cannot stop in time still goes on, as with lights. Prices 700 (priority, each), 500 (yield, stop); no upkeep. Signs at a junction with lights are dormant. Road-only worlds without signs keep their hashes.
- **Game.** Tools Priority road, Yield sign, Stop sign, Remove sign in the Modify category (`build_tool.gd` `SIGN_TOOLS`, a marker on the post), plates drawn as boxes on a post at the right of each approach (`SimHostSigns.DrawSigns`). Compiled and the GDScript parsed; not played.
- **Benchmark job.** `StepBenchmarks` (only with `BENCH` set) measures the step in a Release build: 1000 vehicles on a grid 0.3 ms, 20 trains of 8 wagons on parallel lines 0.01 ms, a map in a rush day with 8 buses and 6 trains 0.02 ms (Apple M4). Each world has an average budget about seven times that and all share a 99th-percentile budget of 6.25 ms (a step at 8x speed). The CI job `benchmark` runs it on Ubuntu and writes the table to the job summary. Trains cost almost nothing next to road traffic, so there is nothing to optimise in them.

### 5.23 The campaign time scale (C1, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`economy`, `towns`, `industries`, `cargo`, upkeep fields), `Simulation.Economy.cs`, `EconomyTuning.StartYear`, `hud.gd`. No new mechanism: the time scale is data, so C1 is a retune plus a calendar that starts in January 1938.

- **The rule.** Every flow keeps its value per second. A day is 1200 s (twice as long): every per-day amount is doubled (upkeep of vehicles, trains, stations, bays, traffic lights; `interestPerDay` 0.004 to 0.008; `supplyPerDay`; `bankruptcyDays` 73 to 36). A month is 3 days = 3600 s (a fifth of what it was): every per-month amount is divided by five (the `perMonth` of the seven industries; the `sustain` and `construction` amounts of the nine building tiers, for instance a tier 1 home eats 4 food a month instead of 20 and a tier 1 building needs 8 building materials instead of 40). Ratios per month (stock against need, deliveries against need) are therefore the same as before, so growth, satisfaction and abandonment run at the same pace per month, which now takes a fifth of the clock time. Satisfaction points, thresholds and the month counts (`inactiveMonths`, `recoveryPerMonth`) are not scaled: they are outcomes of those ratios.
- **What was left alone, and why.** Pay decay (`freeSeconds`, `zeroSeconds`) and the other numbers in seconds (trip time budget, waiting, crews, signals) are physical times and do not depend on the day. The windows of the trip schedule are hours of the day, so they stretch with it (the morning rush is now 100 s long instead of 50). `walkSpeed` stays 2.5 m/s.
- **What moves because of it.** People still make four trips a day, so passenger traffic per second is half what it was; measured on eight maps the bus line's income per hour fell by about 10 percent on average (the buses fill more evenly over a longer rush), so passenger fares were not touched. Freight is unchanged per second. The campaign's budget (C4) is set against these figures.
- **Calendar.** `economy.startYear` is 1938: day 0 is 1 January 1938, day 35 is 3 December 1938, day 287 is 3 December 1945 (the last day of the campaign). The HUD shows "2 Mar 1938   14:30".
- **Tests.** 445 (a calendar theorem grew to six cases). Tests that carried a literal number of the old scale now read it from the tuning (upkeep, building needs); the balance and rail economy tests measure in periods of 600 s (`Worlds.Period`) so their windows and thresholds keep their meaning; a few runs that waited for a half day wait a quarter. Pinned hashes: the rail scenario hash moved to `0x6B1D2292DC4DE3EF` and is the same on macOS and Linux; the road golden hashes did not move.

### 5.33 The call-up (C9b, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`war.conscription`), `WarTuning.cs` (`ConscriptionTuning`, `WarWave`), `Simulation.Conscription.cs`, `Trips.cs` (`TripGroup.PendingCall`), `Town.cs` (`Town.CalledUp`), hooks in `Simulation.Industries.cs`, `Simulation.Workers.cs`, `Simulation.Campaign.cs` and `Simulation.Economy.cs`; the game layer is `SimHost.GetCompany` and `hud.gd` (a line in the Company window). Tests in `WarTests.cs` (`ConscriptionTests`).

- **Waves.** `conscription.waves` lists month indices and shares: 6 % in month 22 (November 1939), 8 % in 34, 10 % in 50 and 62, 8 % in 74. At the turn of the month before, `ConscriptionForMonth` fires every wave whose month has come: each trip group is marked to lose that share of its people.
- **Taking people.** A group that is not on a trip loses `floor(People * share + u)` people at once, with `u` drawn from the group's own seed and the wave number, so a small group loses its fair share on average; a group on a trip loses them at the next turn of a day (`TakeCalledUp`). The people are gone for good: the jobs they held at their workplace are freed, the town counts them in `Town.CalledUp` and `Simulation.CalledUp` (a number only; there are no individuals), and a group that loses everyone is retired. Each town loses `satisfactionLoss` (3) per wave and the feed says "Call-up: 8 % of the people are called up, about 54 in all".
- **The workforce gap.** The share of the workforce that is missing goes to `1 - (1 - gap) * (1 - share)` with each wave and closes by `recoveryPerMonth` (0.012) a month (those who stayed work more). Every industry makes its output times `1 - outputLoss * gap` (outputLoss 0.7), the people's own staff of the big industries is multiplied by `1 - gap`, and the workers a station sends by the same. After the first three waves the gap is about 20 % and industry makes about 86 % of its output.
- **Not done.** The towns' own population as `StatsOf` reports it comes from the buildings and is not lowered (the trip groups are what travel and so what earns); the call-up is not scaled by the frequency option (the plan scales only rationing and the raids); nobody returns; casualties of the front are not modelled.
- **Hash.** The call-up state (next wave, gap, total, the people of every group) is in the state hash once a wave has fired, so no golden moved.
- **A note on tests.** With a day of 30 s the walks of a trip group last longer than the day and the groups are always on the road, so the call-up tests use a day of 300 s.
- **Tests.** 644 (10 more).

### 5.30 Workers (C7, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`workers`; `jobs` on every industry type; the cargo `workers`; the vehicle `workerbus`), `Tuning.cs` (`WorkersTuning`, `IndustryTypeTuning.Jobs`), `Simulation.Workers.cs`, `Simulation.Industries.cs` (production, `IndustryRoom`), `Simulation.Fleet.cs` (freight leaves workers alone), `Simulation.Towns.cs` (acceptance), `Town.cs` (`Industry.OwnStaff`); the game layer is `SimHost.GetIndustries` and `hud.gd`. - **Jobs and staffing.** The steel mill (40 jobs), engine works (30), truck plant (60) and power plant (20) have jobs; every other industry has none and is always staffed. Staffing is the industry's own staff plus the workers delivered over its jobs, at most 1.
- **The people's own movement** staffs part of it for free: `ownShare` (0.5) times `1 - d / ownReach` (2500 m), d being the distance to the nearest town's middle (`Industry.OwnStaff`, set when it is founded). A steel mill 1000 m from a town has 30 %; one 1500 m away 20 %. The rest has to be carried.
- **Workers are a cargo, not trip groups.** This is a departure from the plan, which said residents fill the jobs through the existing trip groups: those go from home building to a workplace *building*, with a cached mode choice per pair of buildings and the stations that cover them, and an industry is not a building, so making it one would have touched the whole trip model. At the turn of every day each road station that covers homes gets workers waiting, `homeShare` (0.35) of the residents it covers, refilled by `refillPerDay`, when an industry with jobs within `reach` (3000 m) of the station is not fully staffed. The new *workers' bus* (capacity 30, the bus's price and upkeep) takes them from a station beside homes to one beside the industry; the delivery fills the industry's staff stock (room: jobs minus staff) and pays the bus line a fare of 40 a unit and km like any cargo. The workers present leave at `staffLossPerDay` (1.0, an exponential with a time constant of a day), so they must keep coming; a bus that brings 30 a day keeps a 40-job mill at about 75 % with its own 30 %. A map without towns has nobody to work: staffing 1.

The Industries window says "Staff 45% of 40 jobs (the town's own people 30%, 6 workers brought)" and turns the status line to a warning when an industry works below full staff.

### 5.29 Electricity (C6, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`power`; `demand` and `supply` on every industry type; the `powerplant` type), `Tuning.cs` (`PowerTuning`), `Simulation.Power.cs`, `Simulation.Industries.cs` (the production step), `Town.cs` (`Industry.Load`), `Company.cs` (`PowerPurchase`, ledger heading `Power`), `SimCommand.cs` (`SetPowerPurchaseCommand`), `SimEventKind.Power`; the game layer is `SimHostPower.cs`, `SimHost.GetIndustries`, `hud.gd` (the power panel at the top of the Industries window) and `main.gd`. Tests in `PowerTests.cs`.

- **Abstract capacity.** Electricity is one number for the whole region, not a cargo and not a network. Every industry type has a `demand` in megawatts (always, working or not): 0 for forest and farm, 1 for quarry, sawmill and food plant, 2 for cement works, factory and the two mines, 8 for the steel mill, 5 for the refinery, 3 to 6 for the truck's works. The supply is the *free grid*, plus what is bought, plus the power plants that are burning.
- **Free grid.** At the start the grid supplies the demand of the industries that stand, times `freeGridMargin` (1.1), so the first game runs at full power on any setting. Industries founded later (the state works of C5, 16 MW) are not in it: they need a plant or power from outside, or every industry slows.

Towns do not use power. A plant is part of the map at the start, not an order.

### 5.28 Orders (C5, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`orders`), `OrderTuning.cs`, `Order.cs`, `Simulation.Orders.cs`, `Company.cs` (`OrdersMet`, `OrdersLate`, `OrdersFailed`, ledger heading `Orders`), `SimCommand.cs` (`AcceptOrderCommand`, `DeclineOrderCommand`), hooks in `Simulation.Gateways.cs` (`Export`) and `Simulation.Industries.cs` (`Distribute`), `SimEventKind.Order`; the game layer is `SimHostOrders.cs`, `hud.gd` (Orders window), `main.gd`. Tests in `OrderTests.cs`.

- **Three kinds.** *Quota*: deliver N units of a cargo to one gateway, whatever its source (it counts when a vehicle unloads at a station that covers that gateway, in `Export`). *Civil*: deliver N units of a cargo to a town (counts what the buildings of that town take in `Distribute`, so only what they have room for). *State work*: when accepted the state founds the industries of the template (the military truck's plant and the three works that feed it) at a place drawn by the seed, and wants N trucks delivered to any gateway. A state work is offered once per game, in Mobilisation (months 20 to 47), with 33 to 42 days to deliver 16 to 24 trucks.
- **Offers.** `StepOrders` runs every tick, only on maps with gateways (the orders draw from their own random stream, `seed ^ OrderSeedMix`, so other maps keep their hashes). The first order is offered on the first step: the tutorial (60 wood, falling back to grain or stone, to the first gateway, 15 days, a reward of 6 000 and 4 points). Then the next offer comes after a gap of `gaps[phase]` days (4 to 8 in Rearmament, 3 to 6 in Mobilisation, 2 to 5 in Total war, 3 to 6 in The end) plus a random moment of the day, so offers do not follow the beat of the month. A template is drawn by weight among those with a weight in the current phase and that fit the map (something makes the cargo, a town needs it, the state work has not been done); the same template, cargo, gateway and town as the offer before is drawn again up to `repeatRedraws` times. At most 5 offers are open and an offer lapses after 6 days (two months). Units, days and standing are drawn inside the template's ranges; the reward is units times `rewardPerUnit`.
- **Accepting.** `AcceptOrderCommand` needs a station that covers the gateway (or the town) and one that covers a producer of the cargo, and fewer than 3 active orders; `WhyNotAccept` says what is missing ("Build a road station beside the North Road Gate first"). The deadline runs from acceptance. `DeclineOrderCommand` drops an offer with no penalty. A state work only needs a station at a gateway: its works are founded on accepting (`IndustriesVersion` and `BuildingsVersion` move, so the map redraws).
- **Closing.** Progress reaching the units meets the order at once: the reward goes to the budget (`LedgerEntry.Orders`), the standing rises by the drawn points and a civil need raises its town's satisfaction by `townGain`. At the deadline an order with at least half delivered is *late*: it pays the reward in proportion and gains the standing in proportion, no penalty. Under half it *fails*: it still pays the share delivered, a fine of a quarter of the whole reward is charged (heading Fines, so it also counts toward the month's fine term), the standing falls by 1.5 times what meeting it would have given, and a town loses `townLoss` satisfaction. Everything is an event on the feed.
- **Window.** The Orders window lists the offers and the orders under way, then the last five closed; each offer has Accept (disabled with the reason shown), Decline and Go to (the gateway, the town or the first work); the button in the top bar shows how many offers are open. The Company window gets a line of totals. Run in a headless Godot with made-up data; the 3D game was not played.
- **Decisions the player has not seen.** Cargo counts whatever its source (the plan said loaded at a station covering the source): simpler and more forgiving, the producer check stays at acceptance. A quota names one gateway so road and rail gateways are asked for in turn. Rewards are first guesses, around 60 to 170 a unit for raw and processed cargo and 1 400 to 1 500 for a truck, on top of the pay for hauling. The standing from orders is added directly; the monthly delivery term of C4 is untouched and will need turning down when real play shows how much orders add.
- **Tests.** 591 (20 more). No golden moved: the test worlds of the goldens have no gateways.

### 5.27 Budget and standing (C4, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`campaign`; `debtLimit` and `bankruptcyDays` are gone from `economy`), `CampaignTuning.cs`, `Simulation.Campaign.cs`, `Company.cs` (`Standing`, `LowStandingMonths`, `Dismissed`, `Finished`, `Grade`, `Score`, `DeliveredValue`, ledger heading `Allocation`), `Simulation.Economy.cs` (the month's turn), `Simulation.Gateways.cs` (`NoteExport`), `SimEventKind.Standing`; the game layer is `SimHost.GetCompany`, `hud.gd` (top bar, Company window, end panel) and `main.gd` (pauses at the end). Tests in `CampaignTests.cs`.

- **The turn of a month** (`CloseMonthForCampaign`, from `CloseDay` on the last day of a month) does, in this order: the standing moves; a standing under `dismissBelow` (20) for `dismissMonths` (6) months in a row dismisses the committee; the last of the 96 months brings the grade; otherwise the allocation of the next month is paid (`LedgerEntry.Allocation`). `LastMonth` keeps what happened for the Company window and `StandingHistory` the standing of every month.
- **Allocation.** `phases[next month].allocation` times `allocationBase + allocationPerStanding * standing` (0.5 at a standing of 0, 1.0 at 50, 1.5 at 100). Phases start at month 0, 20, 48 and 84: Rearmament (Jan 1938 to Aug 1939), Mobilisation (Sep 1939 to Dec 1941), Total war (1942 to 1944) and The end (1945), at 45 000, 60 000, 80 000 and 50 000 a month at a standing of 50. A vehicle's upkeep is about 2 400 a month, so the first allocation keeps some twenty vehicles running.
- **Standing** starts at 60 and moves each month by three terms, then stays in 0 to 100. Deliveries: the value delivered to the gateways that month (units times the cargo's pay per unit and km, so a truck counts for more than a log) minus what the phase expects (1 500, 4 000, 8 000, 6 000), over `exportScale` (4 000) points, within plus or minus 3. Deficit: minus 2 when the money is below zero at the turn. Fines: minus one point per 2 000 of fines that month, at most 5. A committee that delivers nothing therefore slides by 0.4, 1 and 2 points a month and is dismissed around the 55th month, in 1942. The orders of C5 will add their own bonuses and penalties; the delivery term can then be turned down in the tuning.
- **Dismissal and the end.** Both set a flag on `Company` and stop the allocation and the standing; the simulation keeps running and the game layer pauses it and shows the end panel. Dismissal gives no grade. At the end the score is 0.5 times the standing, plus 0.3 times the production (value delivered over `productionTarget`, 600 000, at most 100), plus 0.2 times the average satisfaction of the towns; the grade is the first of "Distinguished service" (75 or more), "Creditable service" (55), "Adequate service" (35) and "Unsatisfactory service".
- **The advance.** The loan keeps its mechanics and code names (`TakeLoanCommand`, `Company.Loan`, interest per day, limit 1 000 000); the windows and messages call it the ministry's advance. The country (Veldmar) and the region (Marren Valley) are in `campaign` and shown in the Company window.
- **HUD.** The top bar shows the money and the standing (red below 30 or when the money is below zero) and the date. The Company window has the country, the phase and the month, the books, the standing with its allocation factor and the months left to dismissal, the next allocation, what was delivered this month against what the phase expects, and last month's three terms. ### 5.26 Gateways (C3, 2026-10-21)

Files: `data/tuning.json` (`gateways`), `Tuning.cs` (`GatewaysTuning`), `Town.cs` (`Gateway`, `GatewayPlan`, `GatewayKind`), `GatewayGenerator.cs`, `Simulation.Gateways.cs`, small changes in `Simulation.Towns.cs` (coverage), `Simulation.Industries.cs` (`Distribute`), `Simulation.Events.cs`, `SimCommand.cs`; the game layer is `SimHost.cs` (`GetGateways`, `RequestImport`, boxes and labels), `hud.gd` (Gateways window), `main.gd`. Tests in `GatewayTests.cs`.

- **Placement.** `GatewayGenerator` runs after the industries, from its own random stream (so the towns and industries do not move), and puts one road gateway and one rail gateway at the edge of the map, `edgeInset` (140 m) in, on two different sides when it can, on level dry ground clear of the towns and industries, at least 1500 m apart and within 3000 m of a town's middle. Without such a place it takes any that will do, and with none on any side the gateway is left out; the new-game note says "Only n of the 2 gateways fit". Names come from the side and kind: "North Road Gate", "East Rail Gate". `WorldBuilder.Towns` calls it.
- **A gateway is an industry that a station covers.** A road station covers a road gateway within the coverage radius, a rail station a rail gateway, and neither covers the other kind. The station quote counts the gateway among the industries it would reach. `Gateway` holds `Stock` (imported cargo waiting for a station), the totals imported and exported, and for every cargo the tick a shipment arrives at and the tick from which it can be asked for again.
- **Imports.** `RequestImportCommand(gateway, cargo)` charges `units` times `pricePerUnit` at once (ledger heading `Imports`; from the budget once C4 exists) and the shipment lands `shipmentSeconds` (600) later as stock, which is handed to the covering stations of its mode like an industry's output. One shipment of a cargo at a gateway at a time, `intervalSeconds` (1200) before it can be asked for again, and at most `stockLimit` (800) of it at the gateway. `WhyNotImport` says why a request is refused. On offer: coal and iron ore (200 units, 60 each), oil (100, 80), rubber (100, 110) and fuel (100, 100). These are first guesses, dearer than the 40 to 60 that hauling a unit a few kilometres pays, to be balanced with C4 and C5. Plan says "coal, ore, rubber, fuel"; oil is added because the refinery needs it.
- **Exports.** Whatever a vehicle unloads at a station that covers a gateway and that the buildings and industries there cannot use is taken by the gateway (`gateways.exports`: every freight cargo except oil and rubber), pays as any delivery and is counted in `Exported` for the orders of C5. A station that covers a gateway accepts the exports.
- **Hash and events.** Stock, totals and timers are in the state hash (only when a map has gateways, so the old goldens did not move); an arrival is a `ShipmentArrived` event.
- **Gateways window.** One block per gateway: whether a station reaches it, a row per import with price, stock, arrivals so far, state (ready, on its way with seconds left, asked for too recently, no room, no money) and a button to ask for a shipment, and what has been exported. Run in a headless Godot with made-up data; the boxes (blue for road, orange for rail) and labels in 3D were not seen.
- **Tests.** 547 (18 more).

### 5.25 The heavy chain (C2, 2026-10-21)

### 5.24 Condition, maintenance and full incidents (M5, 2026-10-21)

Files: `MaintenanceTuning.cs` and the `maintenance` section of `data/tuning.json`, `Simulation.Condition.cs` (wear, hazard, policy, commands), `Simulation.Repairs.cs` (jobs, bays, service trips, towing, depot upgrade), `Simulation.Signals.cs`, `Simulation.Consequences.cs`, `Simulation.RoadCondition.cs`, `Simulation.Events.cs`; the game layer is `SimHostMaintenance.cs`, `hud.gd`, `main_menu.gd`. Plan `docs/m5-plan.md`, decision ADR 0005.

- **Condition (5a).** 0 to 100 for every road vehicle, every train (one value for the consist) and every parked entry of the garage; ambient cars have none. It starts at 100 and falls with the distance driven (`metresPerPoint` 1500), 30 % faster with a full load and 1.5 times as fast after a Patch until the next Service or Overhaul. `StepCondition` runs after movement and draws from the one RNG in a fixed order (vehicles by slot, then trains by id): a breakdown has a hazard per metre that is zero from 60 up, rises as the square to `hazardPerKmAtFloor` at 20 and linearly to `hazardPerKmAtZero` at 0, times the frequency scale and, on a worn road, `RoadRiskFactor`. Brakes fade below 60 (to 0.6 of the braking at 0), which feeds rear-end crashes; top speed never falls.
- **Breakdown.** A vehicle becomes a Wreck of an `IncidentKind.Breakdown` incident with `FromCondition`; the cargo is lost; once the crew has cleared it, it is towed to the garage of the nearest depot (by road, else by straight line) with `wreckCondition` 5. Only wrecks of that kind are towed: a company vehicle wrecked in a collision still vanishes as before. A train that breaks down stands `trainBreakdownSeconds` holding its track (a crew is abstract), then drives to the nearest rail depot as towed (it cannot break down again on the way) and waits in the garage. A breakdown below Condition 15 is negligence and fined.
- **Policy.** `MaintenancePolicy(Mode, EveryDays, BelowCondition, Kind)` with modes Inherit, Off and On, set for the Company (default Service every 60 days or below 40), for a Line, for a vehicle or for a train by `SetMaintenancePolicyCommand`; `EffectivePolicyOf` says which applies and where from. `RequestRepairCommand` asks for a repair at the next stop (or, for a parked vehicle, takes it into a bay of its depot). `SetFrequencyCommand` and the `Simulation` constructor argument set the frequency option (Off, Low, Normal, High; scales 0, 0.5, 1, 2); it scales breakdowns and signal failures only, not the accidents that come out of the driving.
- **A due vehicle.** At a stop (`BeginStop`, `BeginTrainStop`) it is due when the policy says so or a repair was asked for. It unloads what the stop takes, puts the rest down (people make their own way, freight is lost), loads nothing, and on leaving drives to the nearest depot that can do the repair (`ServiceDepotFor`; for a train `NearestRailDepotFor`). A vehicle goes inside as a ghost that waits in the depot (`_servicing`, not drawn, never leaves the queue of `StepGhosts`) and a train leaves the track (`TrainStatus.Servicing`, route cleared). If no depot can do it, the vehicle keeps running and the game warns once (`SimEventKind.NoDepot`).
- **Repairs and bays (5b).** A `RepairJob` takes a bay for its time: Patch +15 in 20 s for 3 % of the price, Service +50 in 60 s for 12 %, Overhaul up to 95 in 180 s for 40 % (the shares are higher than the first plan's 2, 6 and 25 % because the balance asked for it, see below). Bays are 1, 2 and 3 by level; the rest wait in the order of the jobs. The price is paid when the repair is done (ledger `Maintenance`, and on the line's books). Patch is done by any depot, Service from a Workshop, Overhaul only in a Complex (`CanRepair`). `UpgradeDepotCommand` raises a road or rail depot in place for the difference in price (a road depot gets its crews too); a rail depot has a level now (`AddRailDepotCommand` takes one; `rail.depotWorkshopPrice` and `depotComplexPrice`). A job whose depot closes is dropped and the vehicle comes out as it went in; selling a vehicle or a train, deleting its line, or parking it ends its job or trip.
- **Signals (5c).** A rail signal has a Condition that falls 0.3 with each train let through. A train that comes up to a signal draws its failure once per approach (the same curve, one passage counting as 500 m); a failed signal shows red to everybody, and trains wait at it (`BlockedBy` is -2), until a crew from the nearest depot (road or rail, by the crew speed and detour) has repaired it. A failure that takes longer than 600 s (no depot near) is negligence and fined. With the company policy on, a crew also services a signal under the floor or due by days (checked at each passage and at the turn of every day).
- **Consequences (5c).** A company vehicle wrecked in a collision pays compensation to a share of the passengers aboard (30 % at 900 each; the injured are a number, `Company.Injured`); so does a train that hits a vehicle at a crossing. The town the incident happens in (inside its blocks plus 60 m) loses 2 satisfaction once per incident, and 3 more if a company incident has blocked a road for over an hour. There is no insurance. `LedgerEntry` has `Maintenance`, `Compensation` and `Fines` now.
- **Road Condition (5f).** Every road has a Condition that falls with the vehicles that drive its length (4000 passes for a point; ambient cars count). Above 80 it does nothing; below it drivers keep less of the speed limit (down to 70 % at 0) and the breakdown risk of company vehicles rises (up to 3 times). A road that replaces another (a junction in its middle, a node moved) takes over the Condition. `ResurfaceRoadCommand` restores a road for 60 a metre; it is closed while the crew works (2 m/s of road), with debris in every lane that has room, like a wreck. The Condition of roads enters the state hash only when the company has lines, so road-only runs keep their golden hashes.
- **The garage.** At the turn of a day a vehicle or train waiting in a depot gets the company policy: one that is due goes into a bay of its depot when the depot can do the repair (a vehicle towed in after a breakdown is mended this way); one the depot cannot repair waits for the player. The policy set on a vehicle itself is lost when it is parked (it is a garage entry then).
- **Event feed.** `Simulation.Events` keeps the last 300 things the player should see (breakdowns, crashes with their injured and compensation, signal failures, a repair no depot can do, an unhappy town) with a place for the camera; it is not in the state hash. The HUD shows them top left for 45 to 90 s, a click moves the camera, serious ones also show as a message.
- **Hashes.** Condition, the time of the last repair, the patched mark, the policies, the frequency, the repair jobs and the signals' Condition are in the state hash; the rail golden is `0xFC2176B37CC8EA48` and the new maintenance scenario's `0xFE19620F8B25AE46`, both the same on macOS and Linux; the road golden hashes did not move.
- **Balance.** `MaintenanceBalanceProbe` (env `MAINT_PROBE`, `DAYS`, `KINDS`, `SEEDS`, `MPP`, `SERVICE`) printed what a line earns, how far a bus drives (about 8 km a day) and what the default policy costs. The plan's first numbers (12 km a point, 6 % for a Service) gave under 1 % of revenue; with 1500 m a point and 12 % the policy costs 7 to 14 % of a four-bus line's income on four maps (mean 10 %), and a fleet with the policy off breaks down within a month and the line stops paying (`MaintenanceEconomyTests`, `MaintenanceNeglectTests`). The risk at the 40 floor is small (0.0125 per km at the first numbers, 0.00125 now) so a policy that works is almost never broken down. A truck line is poorer per vehicle than a passenger line (a sawmill's input fills in a few days), so maintenance is a larger share of its income; that is the line's economics, not a different number.
- **Tests.** 514 (69 more). `MaintenanceWorlds` builds the small worlds and a `Calm` tuning with the plan's first slow numbers so that the unit tests do not move when the balance does; the long fleet tests that are not about maintenance (`FleetTests`, `IndustryTests`) run with the frequency Off, because a Simple depot cannot Service and their buses would otherwise wear out in a dozen days.

## 8. Tuning values

`data/tuning.json` is the only place the behaviour numbers and prices live. It is embedded in the simulation assembly (so the sim stays free of file paths and Godot) and read by `Tuning.Load()` into typed records; the network owns the `Tuning` (`RoadNetwork(terrain, tuning)`, default is the shipped file) and the simulation reads it from there. Every key is required and unknown keys are rejected, so a typo fails at load. Sections: `driving` (IDM, braking, junction range, conflict distance, crossing safety time, exit queue speed, turn speed), `traffic` (spawning, driver speed spread and ambient cars per town), `manoeuvres` (decision interval, lateral rate, stall), `laneChange`, `overtake` (every threshold and driver-trait formula), `crews` (timings, crews per depot level, the depot snap radius, how many cleared incidents are kept), `roads` (speed limit and price per type, earthworks, demolition, upgrade refund, downgrade and re-signing prices, bridge and tunnel thresholds and prices, crossing clearance, `trafficLight`), `economy` (starting money, loan limit, day length, days per month and months per year, interest, insolvency, sale refund, depot prices), `stations` (price, upkeep, coverage radius, stop geometry), `towns.growth` (thresholds, time budget, lot clearance, whether to extend the grid), `trips` (group size, car share, walking, nearness, windows and jitter, cost weights, the limits on cars and waiting), `towns` (names, satisfaction rules, unmet-needs penalty, starting and maximum stock, access radius, abandonment months, `generation`: attempts, margins, spacing, grid size, `rings`, and `buildingTypes` with three tiers each: size, height, people, jobs, demolition price, passengers per day, `sustain` and `construction` lists), `industries` (generation margins and `chainReach`, counts for few, normal and many, and the seven types: size, output, rate, stock limits, inputs, colour), `cargo` (name, pay rate, delay tolerance, supply on empty maps and waiting limit per cargo type) `vehicles` (the roster: cargo, capacity, length, top speed, price, upkeep, dwell times) and `rail` (track prices and limits, signals, station and depot prices, `movement` (braking, resistance, how far ahead a train asks for track, when a wait is a block), `crossings` (barrier timing, minimum distances, collision halt) and the `locomotives` and `wagons` rosters). A change to a number is a behaviour change: the golden hash in `DeterminismTests` will move and the traffic tests must still pass (see section 7).

Still in code, on purpose: step 0.05 s (ADR 0003); a car's 4.5 m and every vehicle's 1.9 m width, and lane width 3.5 m; the 12 m click radius and 5 m step for stations and the 40 m watch distance when a vehicle leaves a depot; the lateral thresholds `HomeLaneLat` 0.5 and `OncomingSightLat` 0.3 (they define when a vehicle counts as being in the other lane); road geometry rules in `RoadNetwork` (minimum length, maximum grade, minimum radius, maximum sweep, curve comfort). Eras come later as their own file.

## 9. Findings worth remembering

- Anything that lets a vehicle appear in front of traffic that cannot see it (lane change right after a junction) produces rear-end pile-ups; the fix is margins, not braking.
- A head-on standoff between an overtaker and stopped oncoming traffic is a permanent deadlock unless something forces a resolution; queues then spread across the grid.
- Strong braking hides failures: with instant full braking almost no overtake ended in a crash. Reaction delay (jerk limit) and attention are what make rare failures happen.
- The old chord approximation of junction paths ignored every pair of moves that shared an arm, including a left turn against the oncoming straight traffic. Real paths found those conflicts; the extra waiting built long crawling queues at junctions, and overtakers then tried to pass them and sideswiped oncoming cars. Fix: no overtake starts with a queue (vehicles under 2 m/s) within 100 m ahead of the target.
- A right of way that is decided pair by pair is not transitive: when each pair compares arrival at its own conflict stretch, four vehicles at a four-way junction can each wait for the next, and a 1000-car grid locked into such rings. Rank vehicles by one number that does not depend on the pair (time to the junction edge) and let them wait outside the junction.
- Centre lines that never cross are not enough to call two moves compatible: a vehicle reaches half its length ahead of its path and half its width beside it, so paths closer than 3.3 m conflict. The exact box test inside the junction (`IncidentKind.Junction`) is what showed it.
- A junction path needs a length: lanes meet at the node point, so paths start and end 8 m from it.
- Junction conflicts depend on the size of the bodies: the stretch where two paths meet and the 3.3 m "paths too close" test were derived for cars. A 10 m bus makes a crash out of cases that are clear for cars (box corners sweep further on a turn), and widening the stretch by a guessed margin changed nothing. The fix was a second set of tables, built with larger distances, for any pair with a long vehicle.
- A vehicle put on the road at a junction is hit from behind by one that is just leaving the junction onto the same lane, so a purchase waits until nothing is about to arrive there.
- Test helpers that turn days into ticks must round: `60f / 0.05f` is just under 1200 and a cast truncates it, which dropped a day of upkeep and made a replay stop one tick short.
- A boundary node cannot belong to the stretch that ends at it: if the train waiting at a signal holds the node, it blocks every other train that wants to start a stretch there, and two trains waiting nose to nose at the two ends of one block each hold the node the other needs. A stretch holds its start node, not its end node (except at the station a train is stopping at).
- Block signals cannot make a passing loop work: a block request takes the whole loop, both tracks. Passing needs path signals (only the train's path) and a train that changes its route around held track.
- Putting a level crossing close to a junction makes cars stop on the rails: they come out of the junction too fast to stop at the line, brake as hard as they can, and stand in the band. 24 m from a junction or the end of a road leaves room to stop; 14 m did not (seven cars in eight days on one map).
- A speed profile that reads "slow down for a barrier that is not down yet" makes every train slow for every crossing, since the barrier comes down only a few seconds before it is needed. The hold has to use the emergency brake: it bites only when the train would otherwise arrive before the barrier, which is exactly when it is needed.
- `Worlds.Run(sim, days)` takes days and `RailWorlds.Run(sim, seconds)` seconds; a 1200 in the wrong one runs 1200 days.
- `unproject_position` returns viewport coordinates and synthetic input events need window coordinates; with a stretch mode the two differ, so the self-tests convert through `get_final_transform()`.

## 10. Known limitations and technical debt

Behaviour:
- Ambient traffic that is not a trip car (the few cars `SeedTraffic` puts on a map) is a random walk; drivers only steer away from blocked roads. Company vehicles and trip cars are routed.
- Only tier 0 fidelity exists (ADR 0002 tiers 1 and 2 are not built, deliberately left out of M2); 1000 vehicles are all fully simulated.
- Accident causes are overtaking, rear-ending, meeting inside a junction, level crossings and, for the company's own fleet, breakdowns from low Condition (ambient cars do not break down); no weather.
- Crews are abstract (a straight line from the depot, no return trip) and, for signals and trains, only a time; depot level sets crews and what the depot repairs.
- Removal and moving leave the terrain as it was carved, on purpose: demolishing a road does not rebuild the hill (decision, not a gap); money now exists (5.10).
- Junctions have traffic lights (5.20) and signs (5.22), and a vehicle that cannot stop in time goes on into the junction; the lane length is the centre-line length for every lane of a curve.
- Lakes are not exactly level (the water surface has one gentle slope for the whole map), and there are no tides or boats (decision, 5.8).
- An overtaker alongside its target at a lane end can reappear overlapping it.
- Tiny dense networks (a few roads, dozens of cars) still produce occasional pile-ups at junctions.

Rail (M4):
- Trains cannot collide with each other (reservation) and there is no derailment; the only incidents on the rail side are trains hitting vehicles at crossings. Signal failures, breakdowns and wear came with M5 (5.24).
- All trains are push-pull and reverse in a station wherever it is quicker; there is no run-round loop and no timetable. A train longer than the track behind its station cannot reverse until it is fully on its route (it carries on instead).
- Unsignalled track is one block per stretch between stations and depots: a second train waits for the first. Two trains on one single track turning round at its ends deadlock when they meet; passing loops need path signals and the re-routing of 5.19. Re-routing only changes the part of the route that has not been given to the train and does not look at where other trains are going, only at what they hold.
- A signal faces the track it was built on (the direction chosen by the click side); it cannot be changed except by taking it down. Block and path signals are the only kinds; there is no one-way signal that lets trains pass the other way unchecked.
- Level crossings: a crossing is refused near a junction or road end (24 m), so a track cannot go through a town block that is shorter than 48 m between junctions; the street between two crossings of one railway can hold only as many cars as it is long. Moving a road junction so that its roads would cross a track is refused rather than priced. Towns do not grow within 14 m of a track, so a railway through a town stops it on that side.
- Rail stations are only at nodes (the track is split), with a platform drawn on both sides of the node; a station that is an end of the line needs a dead-end node. Platform capacity and a train too long for its platform are not modelled (the front stops at the node; the rest hangs back along the track).
- Trains are boxes: no pantograph, no smoke, a locomotive is a taller box. Stations, signals, barriers and tunnel portals are boxes too. The 3D side of M4 was not played: only the simulation is tested (444 tests), the C# compiled, the GDScript parsed and the HUD run headless with made-up data.
- Maintenance (M5): the 3D side was not played (only the simulation, the C# compiled, the GDScript parsed and the HUD run headless with made-up data). A company vehicle wrecked in a collision is still scrapped, not towed. Resurfacing a road with traffic on it traps vehicles behind the debris until it is done. A parked vehicle follows the company policy only (a policy set on the vehicle is lost when it is parked); rail track has no Condition. Condition of roads is in the hash only when the company has lines.
- Rail balance had a first pass (a line between two towns, a freight line from a forest to a sawmill), not a thorough one; the price of track, the locomotive roster and the pay rates are first numbers.

Economy and fleet (M2):
- Stations produce a fixed amount of each cargo and accept any; there are no buildings, towns or industries until M3, so demand is not related to where a station stands and `coverageRadius` is unused.
- A vehicle stops in its lane at a station built without bays: a loading bus holds up everything behind it. A station with bays (5.15) takes it out of the lane, on the right-most lane only, and a second bus beyond `bayCapacity` loads in the lane again. A bay is drawn beside the lane, not as a built pocket in the road.
- Cargo has no destination; a vehicle unloads everything at the next stop, and a line of three or more stations pays by the hop. Pay uses straight-line distance.
- Vehicles are not serviced and do not wear (M5); a wrecked company vehicle is removed with its cargo when the crew clears it, with no insurance. A purchase can be refused for lack of room at a busy depot junction and must be retried.
- Vehicle width is 1.9 m for all; the bus and truck differ in length and height only.
- Lines can be edited, named and coloured, and stations renamed, closed and given bays later (5.15, 5.17); a station that was never renamed is named for the nearest town or industry. A closed station cannot be reopened (build another at its node). There is no map overlay for coverage yet.
- The game layer of M2 (tools, Lines panel, drawing) was compiled and the GDScript read through, not played: no Godot binary was available in the environment where it was written, so `SimHost.cs` was compiled with `dotnet build transport-madness.csproj` and the GDScript was only read through.
- Pay rates, prices and supply had a first balance pass with the probes of 5.15 (passenger and freight lines both pay, freight no longer several times a bus); upkeep, prices and the chains were not touched and the final balance is M6.

Towns (M3a):
- (Replaced in M3c: stations no longer collect passengers by themselves, people come from trip groups, 5.13.)
- Buildings have no needs yet: nothing is delivered to them, they do not grow, and satisfaction can only fall by demolition and abandonment and recover on a timer. Industries do not exist (M3b); growth is M3d.
- Streets are all straight lines on a square grid, axis-aligned to the contours; a town does not follow rivers or hills beyond staying on level dry ground. Towns do not extend their grid.
- Road access is "a road centre line within `accessRadius` of the building's middle", not a tie to a particular street, so a player's road that passes near an abandoned lot revives its building.
- A road that ends on a lot, or widening a street (upgrade), is not checked against buildings: lots are set back far enough that an avenue never reaches one, and a station is a node, not a footprint.
- A town's name is drawn by a `Label3D` with a guessed fixed size; the buildings are boxes. The 3D side of M3a (drawing, name sizes, the new-game slider) was not played: only the C# compiled and the HUD, menu and build-tool scripts were parsed and the HUD run headless.
- Generation takes about 0.15 s for five towns on a default map; it runs on the main thread when a game starts.

Industries and cargo (M3b):
- Production is a fixed rate: no boost for good service, no seasons, no closure. Processors use their inputs one for one in every chain.
- A freight truck carries one cargo type per trip, the one with most waiting that the next stop accepts, and it will carry on with a load a stop does not accept. A line whose destination is saturated wastes the excess (it is lost, not paid).
- Freight trucks still unload everything at the first accepting stop; cargo has no destination beyond acceptance. Passengers have destinations since M3c.
- Nothing spends the construction stock yet (M3d); satisfaction can only recover on its timer or fall by demolition, abandonment and unmet needs.
- The generator keeps each processor within `chainReach` (1500 m, straight line) of a producer of each input and of a town, but only as the crow flies: the ground between may be a lake or a hill, and it does not build roads to industries. On about 1 map in 50 a processor does not fit (the map says so, 5.16).
- Industries are one box each; the names above them are `Label3D` with guessed sizes. The 3D side of M3b was not played.

Trip groups (M3c):
- The cost of a car trip uses the empty-road time times the congestion estimate of the hour (5.15), learned from the trips that ended: it only moves people when there is something to move to (a line) and it lags a day or two behind a new jam. A town with every group driving still shows jams: with the 600 s day a cross-town trip is hours of game time, a car stands for 5 people and the cap is 200, which the rush of five towns of 4000 to 5400 people only just reaches; company buses average 3 to 9 m/s through it (they crawled at 1 to 8 m/s with the old 2 and 300, and the cap was reached in every rush). A town of that size still jams its centre at 8 to 9 and 17 to 19 o'clock, and one seed of six (721 m between its towns) still has buses at 3 m/s and a line that barely pays.
- A group is made once, per home, when the world is added; M3d must make groups for the people of a building that grows and retire those of one that is demolished (today a demolished home's groups simply stay at home).
- Public transport is direct lines only, the wait is a fixed time over the vehicles on the line (not the line's real round trip), and the fare in the cost is the passenger pay rate. Buses carry whatever queue they find, in the order people arrived; there is no capacity planning and no way to see a line's load.
- A car is taken off the road 30 m before its destination node (it is not parked or drawn at a building) and appears at the node nearest the origin; walkers and passengers are not drawn at all (pedestrians are a visual for later).
- Walking and leisure follow the 600 s day: a 100 m walk takes about a tenth of a day, so late evenings slip and a leisure trip is dropped when its window is gone. The numbers (`walkSpeed`, windows) are first guesses to be played with.
- Workplaces are drawn once; a group does not change job when a better one appears (only when its own is gone). Inter-town work trips need a road or line between the towns, otherwise the group cannot go and is counted as staying home.
- The 3D side of M3c (the number and look of trip cars) was not played.

Growth (M3d):
- Growth and the construction stock have not been balanced: costs per size are the first numbers of M3a, deliveries are limited only by the trucks the player runs. The town window shows what happened last month, there is no forecast.
- A town extends its grid only by whole columns and rows on four sides, with straight streets and no regard for the terrain beyond dry ground; steep ground makes `BuildRoad` refuse, and then that side is skipped. New blocks are not checked against the player's roads (a road through the strip just gets junctions) or against other towns beyond buildings and industries.
- A home never gets smaller (a town does not shrink), so only demolition ends groups; a group that is away or travelling when its home goes is retired a day later. Workplaces are not drawn again when a bigger one appears.
- Every building in id order gets its turn each month, so older buildings found new ones first; with scarce stock the centre grows before the edge.
- The 3D side of M3d (new buildings, new blocks, taller boxes) was not played; the roads are redrawn from scratch when a town builds streets, which takes a moment on a big map.

Engineering:
- `GoldenHash` in `DeterminismTests` has one pinned value for macOS (`GoldenHashMac`) and one for Linux (`GoldenHashLinux`, the same on x64 and arm64); a system with neither (Windows) only has the two-runs-agree check. `AvenueGoldenHash` is the same on both. `MathF.Sin/Cos/Sqrt` come from the platform's maths library, so a change that moves the hash must be pinned twice (the Linux value comes from the container run or from the CI log).
- The command log is in memory only; saving it (and a snapshot, M6) is not done. `DemoWorld` builds its grid outside the log.
- Cleared incidents are remembered up to `crews.incidentsKept` (500) plus a batch of up to 16 (5.18); one very old incident that is still open holds back the dropping of the ones after it. Spawning a vehicle, clearing an incident and a network change still rebuild the whole per-lane ordering; only a lane change is local.
- Tuning is read through section records cached in `Simulation` fields rather than constants; step time at 1000 vehicles measured 0.8 to 1.1 ms in `--bench` after the change (it was about 0.9 ms), so nothing is gained by flattening it yet.
- `SpawnDebris` goes through `SpawnVehicle` (draws RNG, rebuilds order) although debris never drives.
- `SimHost` still does picking and drawing (snapping a click to a node or a road, the colours and shapes), which is the game layer's job; the rules it used to carry now live in the simulation (5.18).
- `DepotLevel` and the crew count anticipate M5; the depot level only sets the number of crews (in `data/tuning.json`) for now.
- `main.gd` carries the benchmark and self-test harness.
- Frame-rate numbers are manual, not part of CI.

1. **Congestion probe.** Not a bug: the identical runs came from the shell not splitting the settings. The rush is now 5 people a car and at most 200 cars; buses average 3 to 9 m/s and none is lost on the probe seeds.
2. **Growth balance (M3d).** Measured, costs and thresholds kept, pace pinned in `BalanceTests`; found and fixed a bug that stopped towns growing when deliveries were plentiful.
3. **Chains within reach (M3b).** Processors are placed within `chainReach` of an input and a town; the map says when one did not fit.
4. **Trip groups of a demolished home (M3c, M3d).** Retired, jobs given back. A home never shrinks.
5. **CI.** GitHub Actions on Ubuntu; golden hash pinned per system. Not yet seen running on GitHub.

**Done: medium priority (2026-10-19, 5.17)**
6. **Transit wait from the line's round trip.**
7. **"What the town still needs"** in the Towns window and the **connectivity view on the map** (compiled and run on made-up data; the 3D drawing was not seen).
8. **Small interface items:** colour of a line, renaming a station, closing a station, adding bays later, more window sizes, bays drawn as a pocket.

**Later, or needs a decision**
- Buses in the rush and overtaking accidents that wreck them (core of the traffic model, sensitive; the right change depends on how the rush should feel, see item 1).
- Tier 1 and 2 ambient traffic: the step costs about 0.04 ms with five towns, so no profile asks for it yet; do it when one does.
- Rescue crews that drive on the roads, signals, level crossings, incident variety: M4 and M5.
- Towns whose streets follow the terrain, production boosts, cargo with a destination, pedestrians drawn on the streets, a platform model with loading per vehicle, insurance for wrecked vehicles: new design, not finishing work.
- Save and load, a benchmark in CI, platform-independent hashes: M6.
- Low technical debt (`SpawnDebris` going through `SpawnVehicle`, the harness in `main.gd`): only when it starts to cost something. Cleared incidents in memory and the rules in `SimHost` were dealt with (5.18).
- Play-test of the 3D side of the follow-ups (windows, bays, the new-game screen) and of M3a to M3d: the user plays; nothing here can run the 3D game.

**Close out M0 (small)**
Done: command log with tick stamps and replay, determinism test over crashes, crews and overtaking with a golden hash, tuning and prices in `data/tuning.json`, `CONTEXT.md` brought in line (Wreck, Debris, Command, depot levels Simple, Workshop, Complex).
Also done: every per-lane lookup the hot loops use (length, limit, end node, road, `Oncoming`, `Left`, `Right`, `Next`) is a flat array in `Simulation`, refreshed when the network version changes; a lane change re-files only that vehicle (`MoveInOrder`) instead of rebuilding the ordering. A temporary check against `RebuildOrder` over 4533 lane changes found no difference, and `DeterminismTests` pins an avenue scenario (`AvenueGoldenHash`) that exercises lane changes, which the main golden run does not. Step time was not re-measured (`--bench` needs Godot).
Open: none.

**M1: terrain and road building with cut and fill**
Done: seeded heightmap, lane elevation and grade with speed limits, cut and fill cost and terrain carving, build tool cost display, picking and drawing on terrain, circular arcs with curve speed limits, junction conflicts from real path geometry, road removal and upgrade, water (lakes, rivers that run downhill, a sea) with bridges and tunnels, vehicles driving the junction paths (5.9). Cleanup done: downgrade, speed-limit change and moving a node (commands, costs, tests, tools `Downgrade`, `Faster`, `Slower`, `Move`); a depot closes when its node loses every road; `MovesConflict` flattened into per-node tables; vehicles drawn along the junction path; camera focus follows the ground; terrain drawn in chunks.
Closed at the end of M1: grade-separated crossings and junctions on bridges and tunnels (5.8); moving a node keeps curves and vehicles, may cross other roads and takes its depot along (5.7); a sloping water surface with a sea (5.8; tides and boats are out of scope); right of way by arrival order with conflict stretches, box clear and a collision check inside junctions (5.9). A headless run of 1000 cars on the 10 x 10 grid steps in about 0.09 ms in a Release build (the Godot side was not run for this change: the Godot build available here has no .NET support, so `SimHost.cs` and the tool changes were compiled and the GDScript parsed, not played).
Open: none.

**M2: stations, lines, player vehicles, basic economy**
Done: company with money, loan, daily accounts and insolvency; every road, depot and station command is priced and refused when unaffordable; stations at nodes (the road is split), lines, buying and selling vehicles from a data roster at depots; routes over the lane graph by backward distance fields; stops with loading time, cargo supply per station, pay by distance and delay, upkeep; bus (10 m) and truck (8 m) with their own length and separate junction tables for long vehicles; UI for money, stations, lines and the fleet; tests including a profitable bus line and truck line.
Left out on purpose: tier 1 and tier 2 ambient traffic. The plan said "once the road network is large enough for 1000 fully simulated vehicles to stop being the norm"; with a hand-built network of a few kilometres that is not yet true, and the step-time test with 1000 vehicles still passes (the step was not re-measured in a Release build). Do it when a profile of a real M3 map says so.
Done in the follow-up (5.15): line editing and names, the station and vehicle windows, stations with bays, a first balance pass.
Done since: bays drawn as a pocket of paving, colour of a line, renaming, closing and adding bays by hand (5.17). Open: play-test the bays and the windows in 3D, platforms and loading time per vehicle.
